## Findings

1. **Growth is accelerating, not slowing.** Q3 GDP was revised up 0.9 point to 4.3% (investment and consumer spending); private final demand +5.8%. ISM manufacturing production at 59.0 is the highest since Nov 2021. Claims at 189k are very low. The Fed's own Oct statement upgraded "solid" to "strong pace". Sources: BEA GDP release; ISM; https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm (vs https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm). Meaning: breaks the "growth falling" half of stagflation. Confidence: high.

2. **Crack inside the strength: the consumer and rate-sensitive sectors.** Real PCE was flat in October (0.0%), retail sales +0.2% (ex-gas only +0.1%), motor vehicle dealers -1.6%, part-time-for-economic-reasons jumped 339k to 4.8m. Fed minutes say housing and interest-sensitive sectors are weak while AI investment, manufacturing and data centres are strong. Sources: BEA PIO; Census; BLS jobs; https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcminutes20261028.htm. Meaning: a two-speed economy, not a broad slowdown. Confidence: medium (one month of consumer data).

3. **Inflation rose, and PCE now runs hotter than CPI, which is unusual.** PCE 3.8% y/y and core 3.3% in October, up from staff estimates of 3.6% and 3.1% for September. Core CPI was only 2.6% y/y in September. The minutes flag "an unusual discrepancy between CPI and PCE". Participants say the AI buildout is raising prices of tech goods and spreading beyond data-centre industries. Sources: BEA PIO; BLS Sep CPI; Fed minutes. Meaning: the inflation the Fed targets is higher than the CPI headline suggests; the wedge is a lead for tech-hardware pricing. Confidence: medium (the cause of the wedge is not stated by BEA). Side note: BEA's Q3 PCE price index was 2.1% annualized while Oct is 3.8% y/y; they are different windows and I did not reconcile them.

4. **Energy is still the shock in CPI.** Gasoline +37.4% y/y in October (Sep 34.2%); fuel oil +74.2% y/y in September; energy index +5.2% in Sep then +0.7% in Oct. Retail gas-station sales +30.3% y/y. Airline fares index rose again in Oct. ISM prices indexes: manufacturing 75.2, services 73.0 (services 12-month average 69.2, highest since April 2023). Sources: BLS CPI; Census; ISM. Meaning: input-cost pressure for fuel users. Confidence: high.

5. **Fed: hold on Oct 28 (10-2), hawkish tilt, December hike is the base case.** Cook and Hammack dissented for a hike. Minutes (released 2026-11-18): "almost all" participants see further hikes as appropriate; "some" wanted a hike in October; a couple would consider more than 25bp in December if inflation data are bad. Market pricing (per the Desk) had high odds of a hike by year-end and two more by summer 2027. September SEP (https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcprojtabl20260916.htm): 2026 funds rate median 4.1 (one more hike), core PCE 3.4, GDP 2.3, unemployment 4.1. Meaning: confirms "hiking", but the reason is overheating plus an inflation expectations worry, not only the oil shock. Confidence: high. Note: Chair Warsh voted with the majority.

6. **Yields have fallen sharply since Nov 18 while the Fed still leans to hike.** 2y 4.80% (Nov 18) to 4.61% (Nov 25); 10y 5.07% to 4.85%; 30y 5.41% to 5.24% (https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/daily-treasury-rates.csv/2026/all?type=daily_treasury_yield_curve&field_tdr_date_value=2026&page&_format=csv). The prior view's Nov 17 levels (2y 4.80, 10y 5.10, 30y 5.46) are stale. 10y peaked 5.31% on Oct 5, 30y 5.67% on Oct 7. The 3-month bill (4.34%) sits above the funds-rate ceiling of 4.00%, so a hike is priced. The biggest daily drop was Nov 20 (2y -13bp); I could not find an official cause. Meaning: market and Fed disagree somewhat on how far hikes go; watch Dec 8-9. Confidence: high on levels, none on cause.

7. **Credit: spreads widening at the risky end, tied to AI financing.** Minutes: speculative-grade spreads widened notably, especially bonds financing data centres; bank stocks fell sharply on slower loan growth and commercial real estate worries; distressed private-credit loans to software firms rose in Q2; credit card delinquencies elevated. But bank lending standards were slightly easier than normal. Meaning: credit stress is selective, not systemic. Confidence: medium.

8. **Treasury supply: no change.** Nov 4 refunding statement (https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0678): coupon sizes kept for "the next several quarters"; $125bn refunding to raise about $28bn new cash; up to $70bn liquidity buybacks and $25bn cash-management buybacks; $850bn year-end cash balance. Bills cut in December, raised in January. Auctions: the fiscaldata auctions dataset has results only up to the Oct 8 30-year reopening (high yield 5.618%, bid-to-cover 2.54 vs 2.61 in Sept, 5.308%); the November refunding auctions and Oct 21-22 results are **not in the dataset yet** (unreachable/late), so no tails could be computed (the dataset has no when-issued yield). 10y Oct 7: 5.300%, cover 2.77 (Sep 2.71). Meaning: demand looks steady; nothing here. Confidence: low on auctions because of the gap.

## Regime call
**Overheat** (growth rising, inflation rising). Alternatives: Stagflation if Q4 consumer data keep weakening (watch Dec 4 jobs, Dec 16 retail). Overlays: **rates rising** (Fed on a hiking path; long yields easing since Nov 18); **dollar firmer** (minutes: dollar appreciated as US-foreign yield gaps widened; no index level taken); **credit** widening at the risky end, investment grade tight; **liquidity** ample (reserves ample, bill purchases at zero, TGA about $1.0tn peak late Nov). Confidence: medium.
