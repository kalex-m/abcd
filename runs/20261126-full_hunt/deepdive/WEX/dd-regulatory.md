# WEX dd-regulatory (run 20261126-full_hunt, as of 2026-11-27)

## Summary for main thread
1. WEX Bank is under an FDIC consent order (issued 2023-09-20, fixing identified issues and the compliance programme) that the FY2025 10-K says stays in force. The FDIC also fined the bank $650,000 on 2024-12-17 for unfair and deceptive fee practices. The conduct covered undisclosed or higher-than-disclosed late, paper and reactivation fees, and duplicate monthly fees. This is the same type of conduct the FTC went after at Corpay.
2. FTC v. Corpay ended 2026-09-17 with a $100M settlement and a permanent bar on charging fees without express informed consent. It is a live standard for fleet-card fees on small businesses. No action against WEX itself was found.
3. WEX Bank's total capital ratio fell from 15.94% (2025-12-31) to 13.01% (2026-09-30), against a 10% "well capitalized" line. The fuel-price lift is consuming bank capital.

Thesis impact: weakens (modestly). No HSA rule change that hurts Benefits economics was found.

## Findings

**F1. FDIC consent order on WEX Bank, still in force.**
- Fact: the FDIC issued a consent order on 2023-09-20 (the "2023 Order"). It requires WEX Bank to correct identified issues and strengthen its compliance management programme. The 10-K says the order's terms remain in effect "until they are modified, terminated, suspended or set aside by the FDIC". An earlier FDIC order dated 2022-05-06 was also disclosed in the 2023 10-Qs.
- Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1309108/000130910826000010/wex-20251231.htm (10-K filed 2026-02-13, risk factors); https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1309108/000130910823000183/wex-20230930.htm (Q3 2023 10-Q, 2023-09-30).
- Means: the bank is the engine for fleet credit, deposits and HSA custody. An open order limits its ability to expand and puts it under tighter oversight. A step up to a stronger action would hit the model directly. The company said earlier that it did not expect a material effect on results. The MD&A "Regulatory Matters" text could not be retrieved, so current remediation status is unverified (see Coverage).
- Confidence: high that the order exists and was in force as of the 10-K. Medium on current status.

**F2. FDIC $650,000 civil penalty for unfair and deceptive fees (consumer-style findings on commercial cards).**
- Fact: Order to Pay FDIC-23-0134k, dated 2024-12-17, was issued under a consent agreement signed 2024-12-05. The bank neither admitted nor denied the findings. The findings were:
  - failing to disclose personal guarantor liability on commercial credit applications;
  - charging higher late, paper-delivery and reactivation fees than disclosed;
  - failing to disclose a returned-payment fee;
  - charging duplicate monthly fees.
  The bank may not seek indemnification.
- Source: https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/880/2025/02/Wex_Bank_Order_to_Pay.pdf (read as PDF, as of 2024-12-17).
- Means: this is a primary-source fee-disclosure finding against WEX's own bank. The amount is immaterial. The importance is that a regulator has already found fee-disclosure failures at WEX Bank, so the Corpay precedent (F3) is not a purely foreign risk. I did not verify the claim in a secondary summary that WEX's filings say the penalty was paid in full.
- Confidence: high on the order, medium on payment status.

**F3. Corpay precedent finalised: $100M and a fee-consent rule.**
- Fact: on 2026-09-17 the FTC announced FleetCor/Corpay and its CEO will pay $100M in redress to business customers. The court injunction permanently bars billing charges without "express informed consent" and "clear and unavoidable" disclosure, and bars hiding charge information behind hyperlinks. The 11th Circuit upheld summary judgment on all counts against the company in 2026 (cited FTC v. Corpay, 164 F.4th 807). The comment period on the administrative consent order ended 2026-10-22.
- Source: https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/09/fleetcor-agrees-pay-100-million-resolve-administrative-action-after-federal-court-finds-it-violated (2026-09-17); https://openclassactions.com/news/fleetcor-corpay-ftc-100-million-fuel-card-settlement.php (secondary, for the citation).
- Means: appellate-tested law now says fuel-card fees to small businesses need clear consent. The FTC page makes no statement about industry-wide practices. I found no FTC, CFPB or state action naming WEX or Fleet One. Fee-income practices (late fees, card fees) at WEX are the exposed area. The 10-K itself states that FTC Act section 5 and the 50 state UDAP laws apply to WEX.
- Confidence: high on facts. Whether WEX faces a copycat action: unknown, nothing found.

**F4. WEX Bank capital cushion has thinned in 2026.**
- Fact: total capital to risk-weighted assets was 13.01% at 2026-09-30, down from 15.94% at 2025-12-31 ($1,007.5M vs $813.1M of capital). The 10% line is "well capitalized". Tier 1 leverage was 9.26% vs 9.04%, with a 5% line. Implied risk-weighted assets rose from about $5.1B to about $7.7B (derived: required 8% minimum of $408.1M and $619.3M).
- Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1309108/000130910826000043/R68.htm and R25.htm (Q3 2026 10-Q, 2026-09-30).
- Means: higher fuel prices inflate receivables, which raises bank risk-weighted assets. At 13%, there is about 3 points of room over the 10% line (roughly $230M of capital at the current asset base, derived). Losing "well capitalized" status would restrict brokered deposits and could cap deposit rates, per the 10-K. Together with the open consent order, this is the real regulatory tail risk, not the HSA rules.
- Confidence: high on the figures; medium on the interpretation.

**F5. Legal-proceedings note, year over year: unchanged and generic.**
- Fact: the 10-K note (FY2025) and the Q3 2026 10-Q note both say WEX is routinely subject to commercial, contract, employment and IP disputes and regulatory matters, and that reasonably possible loss from all matters "is not material". The 10-K names no lawsuits. The Q3 note says commitments are generally consistent with the 10-K note.
- Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1309108/000130910826000010/R28.htm (2025-12-31); https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1309108/000130910826000043/R22.htm (2026-09-30).
- Means: no new disclosed matter. The FY2024-to-FY2025 comparison was not completed (the FY2024 note could not be reached), so "year over year" here means FY2025 to Q3 2026.
- Confidence: high.

**F6. Named litigation found outside the filings, none material.**
- Patterson v. WEX Inc., N.D. Cal. No. 25-8557: a proposed consumer class action over a processing fee on the COBRA payment platform. On 2026-02-10 the court partly denied WEX's motion to dismiss (plausible claim that the fee description could deceive). Source: https://ogletree.com/?p=94931 (secondary summary; the order itself was not read). Means: another fee-disclosure claim, in the Benefits business, in the same theme as F2 and F3. Confidence: medium.
- Parker v. WEX, D. Utah 2:26-cv-00285, filed 2026-04-06: individual employment discrimination claim, pro se, screening stage. Source: https://dockets.justia.com/docket/utah/utdce/2:2026cv00285/163448. Immaterial.
- Patent Armory v. WEX, D. Del. 1:26-cv-00843, filed 2026-07-13: patent claim, no details found. Source: https://dockets.justia.com/docket/delaware/dedce/1:2026cv00843/93843. Unknown size, likely immaterial.

**F7. HSA rules: no change that hurts custodian economics found.**
- Fact: IRS Notice 2026-05 (2025-12-09) widens HSA eligibility (bronze and catastrophic exchange plans, telehealth safe harbour, direct primary care). Higher contribution limits were not enacted. Comments closed 2026-03-06. The 10-K says WEX Inc. is an IRS-approved passive non-bank custodian under Treasury Reg. 1.408-2(e) (net worth, bond, audit, fiduciary requirements), and that failing the rules could lose that status. A CFPB 2024 issue spotlight flagged HSA fees versus interest earned.
- Source: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-05.pdf (2025-12-09); https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_health-savings-account-issue-spotlight_2024-04.pdf (2024-04); 10-K link above.
- Means: eligibility widening is mildly positive for account counts. The CFPB spotlight is the only regulator signal on custodian economics, and it is a 2024 staff paper, not a rule. No Treasury, IRS or DOL proposal changing custodian rules or interest treatment was found.
- Confidence: medium (search-based, no official docket for custodians exists to check).

**F8. Interchange: legislative risk not advanced.**
- Fact: the Credit Card Competition Act (S. 3623) was reintroduced January 2026. The Senate passed a housing bill on 2026-03-12 without it as an amendment. No floor vote found as of an August 2026 update. WEX says Durbin applies minimally to its products.
- Source: https://www.durbin.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/durbin-marshall-reintroduce-the-credit-card-competition-act (2026-01-13); https://www.pymnts.com/credit-cards/2026/senate-passes-housing-bill-without-credit-card-competition-act-amendment/ (2026).
- Means: low near-term effect; the bill targets banks above $100B in assets, and WEX Bank is far below that.
- Confidence: medium.
