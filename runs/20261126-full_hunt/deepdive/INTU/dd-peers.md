# INTU dd-peers (run 20261126-full_hunt; written 2026-11-28)

## Summary for main thread
1. **Consumer share loss is confirmed by rivals' and IRS data.** IRS self-prepared e-files rose 1.7% (2026 vs 2025, season-end snapshot) and H&R Block's paid DIY revenue was flat (+0.2%) on a 4.2% price rise, which implies fewer HRB DIY returns too. Intuit reported fewer federal TurboTax units. So the pool grew and the big paid-software names lost units; the gainers are free and low-cost filers that disclose nothing.
2. **GBS beats every peer on the margin before shared costs, and probably after.** GBS segment margin is 76.9% before $9.4B of shared platform costs. Peers: Sage 24.5% operating margin, Xero 7.1% operating income, BILL 14.5%, Toast 8.3%. My rough revenue-based allocation still leaves GBS near 33%.
3. **Credit Karma partners show the dependency risk.** Upstart says a significant amount of its loan originations comes from Credit Karma traffic.

Thesis impact: supports (GBS standalone strength) but confirms the Consumer erosion; neutral on Credit Karma.

## Findings

**F1. The IRS shows the self-prepared pool growing while Intuit's units fell.**
Fact: Through 16 Oct 2026, self-prepared e-filed returns were 67,997,000 vs 66,839,000 a year earlier (+1.7%). Professional e-files were +0.5% and total returns -0.1%. At 17 Apr 2026 the figures were 64,796,000 vs 63,726,000 (+1.7%).
Source: https://www.irs.gov/node/167596 (page updated 2026-11-18); https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/filing-season-statistics-for-week-ending-april-17-2026 (as of 2026-04-17).
Means: The self-prepared market did not shrink. Intuit's 10-K says TurboTax federal units fell in FY26, so TurboTax lost share of a growing pool. This matches management's admission that it lost DIY customers to lower-cost providers.
Confidence: high on the IRS numbers. Medium on the share inference, because Intuit's unit count is undisclosed.
Source for Intuit: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000037/intu-20260731.htm (2026-07-31).
Caution: a search-engine summary quoted 166.9M total returns and +2.0% for the October table. The IRS page itself says 163.4M and -0.1%. I used the IRS page.

**F2. H&R Block's DIY unit is shrinking in units, so the loss is not only TurboTax's.**
Fact: HRB FY26 (to 30 Jun 2026) paid DIY tax revenue was $384.6M, +0.2%, on a 4.2% rise in paid net average charge. It filed 3.6M paid DIY returns. Total assisted return volume was -0.1% (company-owned +2.0%). Assisted revenue was +6.1% on a 4.0% higher net average charge.
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/12659/000001265926000026/hrb-20260630.htm (2026-06-30).
Means: Flat revenue with a 4.2% price rise implies about 4% fewer paid DIY returns (my arithmetic, not a disclosed count). Paid DIY is losing units to free or cheaper options industry-wide. This supports Intuit's own explanation, and the competitor that is gaining is not HRB. Intuit's price cut aims to win these filers back; HRB's pricing power in assisted tax shows people still pay for human help.
Confidence: medium (derived). HRB's Q1 FY27 10-Q (to 30 Sep 2026) shows only tiny off-season revenue, so no read.

**F3. Free-filer rivals disclose nothing quantifiable.**
Fact: Block's FY25 10-K describes Cash App Taxes as a free mobile filing product, with no return counts. HRB's 10-K lists IRS Free File (agreement runs to Oct 2029) as a risk.
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1512673/000162828026012254/xyz-20251231.htm (2025-12-31).
Means: We cannot size who took Intuit's units. This is the main gap for the Zoom test on TurboTax.
Confidence: high that the data is absent.

**F4. Small-business peers earn far less than GBS's segment margin.**
Fact (Intuit): FY26 GBS revenue $12,864M, segment operating income $9,887M (76.9%), +16% revenue. Unallocated corporate items were $10,311M, of which other corporate expenses $7,303M and share-based pay $2,056M.
Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000037/intu-20260731.htm (2026-07-31).
Fact (peers):
- Xero H1 FY27 (6 months to 30 Sep 2026, NZD): revenue $1,745M (+46%; +27% pro-forma for the Melio payments deal), adjusted EBITDA $362M (20.7% of revenue), operating income $124.5M (7.1%), gross margin 76.0% (down from 79.1% pro-forma as payments mix rose), free cash flow $238M, customers 5.146M (+12%), average revenue per customer +25%. Source: https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20261112/pdf/076hqdzy5xhp2z.pdf (2026-11-12).
- BILL quarter to 30 Sep 2026: revenue $353.8M (+12.5%), gross profit $272.1M (76.9%), GAAP operating income $51.2M (14.5%, after $24.4M restructuring). Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1786352/000162828026071196/bill-20260930.htm (2026-09-30).
- Toast quarter to 30 Sep 2026: revenue $1,981M (+21%), gross profit $543M (27.4%), operating income $165M (8.3%). Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650164/000165016426000216/tost-20260930.htm (2026-09-30).
- Sage FY26 (to 30 Sep 2026): underlying operating margin 24.5%, +13% operating profit, about 10% organic growth guided for FY27. From a search summary of the company release (the release page returned HTTP 403), so low confidence. https://www.sage.com/investors/investor-downloads/press-releases/2026/11/full-year-2026-results-19-november-2026/ (2026-11-19).
- H&R Block's Wave: FY26 revenue $122.7M, +12.3% on subscriptions and payments volume; profit not separated. Source: HRB 10-K above.
Means: Standalone small-business software and payments businesses earn roughly 7-25% operating margins and grow 10-27%. GBS's 76.9% is before platform costs (R&D, cloud hosting, experts, SBC) that serve both segments. If I spread the $7.3B other corporate expenses and $2.06B SBC by revenue share (GBS is 60% of $21.4B revenue), GBS keeps roughly $4.3B, about 33% of revenue. This is my illustration, not a disclosed number. Even then GBS sits above every peer margin with growth of 16%, between Sage and Xero. Peers with a payments mix (Toast, Xero's Melio) show payments dilutes margin, and Intuit's money growth (+33% volume) will do the same to GBS margin over time.
Confidence: medium-high on peer figures; the allocation is illustrative only.

**F5. Peers confirm Intuit is the incumbent that rivals fear, and a risk to Credit Karma's partners too.**
Fact: BILL's 10-Q names Intuit's October 2023 launch of native bill pay inside QuickBooks as a competitive threat and lists QuickBooks as a key integration. Xero says it is growing US customers to 455,000 (US revenue +51% pro-forma), against QuickBooks's undisclosed base.
Source: BILL 10-Q above; Xero release above.
Means: Rivals say QuickBooks is bundling payments and pushing into their niches; none reports losing share to a new AI-native rival. This supports GBS's moat. Confidence: medium.

**F6. Credit Karma is a distribution channel partners depend on, which cuts both ways.**
Fact: Upstart's 10-Q says a significant amount of its originations comes from Credit Karma traffic and aggregators may stop showing its offers. Oportun reports paying Credit Karma about $126,000 in 2024 and nothing in 2025. NerdWallet names Credit Karma as a competitor.
Sources: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1647639/000164763926000084/upst-20260930.htm (2026-09-30); https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1538716/000114036126018269/ef20071667_10ka.htm (2026-04-30); https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1625278/000162527826000014/nrds-20251231.htm (2025-12-31).
Means: Lenders still lean on Credit Karma, so its partner base is real. No partner discloses a drop in Credit Karma volume or a shift to AI discovery. The weakness management cites cannot be confirmed or denied from partner filings. Confidence: low-medium.
