# INTU dd-credit (as of 2026-11-28; latest balance sheet 2026-10-31)

## Summary for main thread
1. Balance sheet is not a risk: gross debt $6.97B vs cash and investments $5.3B, ratings A3/A stable, revolver covenant (debt/EBITDA max 4.0x) sits near 0.8-1.2x, and no maturity wall ($500M due July 2027 is covered by cash).
2. Owner cash flow is lower than headline FCF: FY26 FCF $8.6B less $2.06B stock pay is about $6.6B, and the FY26 FCF was lifted by roughly $1.7B of deferred cash taxes that the 10-K says reverse (about $2B cash taxes expected in FY27).
3. Mailchimp: $3.31B cash (plus $368M of Bending Spoons stock) is earmarked for buybacks in FY27, none for debt paydown. Buybacks run well ahead of stock pay (FY26 $5.5B vs $2.06B), but FY25 buybacks barely offset issuance.

Thesis impact: neutral (credit does not break it; the reverse DCF should use about $6.6B or lower, not $8.6B).

## Findings

**1. Maturity ladder is smooth, no wall.** Principal $6,970M at 2026-10-31: FY27 (rest) $500M, FY28 $400M, FY29 $1,100M, FY30 $720M, FY31 $750M, after FY31 $3,500M. Senior notes $6,000M: 1.350% Jul 2027 $500M; 5.125% Sep 2028 $750M; 1.650% Jul 2030 $500M; 4.950% Jun 2031 $750M; 5.200% Sep 2033 $1,250M; 5.500% Jun 2036 $1,000M; 5.500% Sep 2053 $1,250M. The rest is $970M of non-recourse secured facilities (my mapping: FY28 $400M, FY29 $350M, FY30 $220M, consistent with the 10-K contractual-obligations table). The $750M 5.25% Sep 2026 notes were repaid in August 2026 with the June 2026 issue ($1.75B at 4.95%/5.50%) plus cash. → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000045/intu-20261031.htm (2026-10-31) and https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000037/intu-20260731.htm (2026-07-31) → Refinancing risk is nil near term; the 2020 low-coupon notes (1.35%, 1.65%) will be replaced at about 5%, adding roughly $35M/yr of interest on $1B (my arithmetic), trivial against FY26 interest of $256M. → high.

**2. Covenants: thin only on paper, wide in practice.** The $2.2B unsecured revolver (undrawn, expires 2031-01-09, JPMorgan agent) has one financial test: Leverage Ratio (debt for borrowed money / EBITDA, trailing four quarters) not above 4.00x. The agreement's EBITDA adds back stock pay and impairments and deducts interest income; up to $1.5B of structured-finance debt is excluded. My calculation for FY26: EBITDA about $8,995M (net income 4,566 + interest 256 + tax 1,451 + depreciation 187 + amortisation 659 + stock pay 2,056 - interest income 180); debt $7,669M carrying (or $6,699M excluding the $970M secured facilities) gives 0.75x-0.85x. Even without the stock-pay add-back (EBITDA $6,730M, debt $7,720M) it is 1.15x. EBITDA would have to fall about 75-80% to breach. Change of control trigger is 35% of voting power. The senior notes have no financial covenant (only limits on liens and sale-leasebacks); the 2020 notes (2027, 2030) carry a 101% put on change of control plus downgrade. Compliance stated at 2026-10-31. → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000004/exhibit1001-revolvingcredi.htm (2026-01-09); https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000045/intu-20261031.htm → no covenant risk. → high (calculation is mine from filed figures).

**3. Ratings: A3 / A stable (Moody's / S&P) at the June 2026 bond sale.** Pricing: 4.95% 2031 notes at +70bp, 5.50% 2036 at +100bp. No later rating action found in filings. → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000119312526262087/d35506dfwp.htm (2026-06-08) → Investment grade; revolver pricing is rating-linked (0.70%-1.125% over SOFR). → high for the date, medium for "no action since" (agency sites not read).

**4. Buybacks and dividends vs FCF.** FY26: operating cash flow $8,838M, capex $221M, FCF $8,617M. Returned: buybacks $5.4B (13.37M shares, average about $409), dividends $1.3B, plus $709M cash for taxes on vesting stock = about $7.4B (86% of FCF). FY25: FCF about $6.1B vs buybacks $2.8B, dividends $1.2B, RSU tax $982M (82%). Q1 FY27: buybacks $1,472M and dividends $378M against operating cash flow $1,062M less capex $75M, so Q1 was funded from cash (cash and investments fell $7.2B to $5.3B; $750M was debt repayment); Q1 is seasonally weak. Dividend $1.38/quarter (+15% year over year; $4.80 FY26 per share). Buyback authorisation left $6.4B. → 10-K and 10-Q URLs above → Payouts are covered by FCF over a full year and the dividend is small (about $1.5B/yr). → high.

**5. Stock pay vs buybacks (the key test).** Stock pay $2,056M FY26 (FY25 $1,968M; FY24 $1,940M). Owner FCF = 8,617 - 2,056 = about $6.56B, 24% below headline; FCF per diluted share on that basis about $23.7 (277M shares; my arithmetic). Share count: 279.13M (Jul 2025) to 268.38M (Jul 2026), -3.9% net; 264.0M at 2026-11-12. In FY26 gross repurchases were 13.37M shares and employee issuance (net of withholding) 2.62M, so issuance offset about 20% of buybacks. In FY25 it was different: 4.32M repurchased vs 3.18M issued (74% offset), net share count -0.4%. Buyback dollars were 2.7x stock pay in FY26 but only 1.4x in FY25. Q1 FY27: 4.60M bought at $318.18 average, 0.77M issued, net -1.4%. Diluted weighted shares 284M (FY24) to 277M (FY26). → 10-K (2026-07-31), 10-Q (2026-10-31) → The shrinkage is real but recent; the FY26 average buyback price (about $409) is about 50% above the $271.72 price, so repurchases created little value per dollar. At today's price the same dollars buy more. → high.

**6. FY26 cash flow was flattered by taxes.** Deferred income taxes added $1,279M to FY26 operating cash flow (FY25: -$435M) because of the 2025 US tax law change (OBBBA); the 10-K says about $2B of income taxes will be paid in FY27. If the benefit is a timing gain, FCF net of stock pay could be nearer $4.8B (8,617 - 2,056 - about 1,714 swing; my estimate, not company guidance). → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000037/intu-20260731.htm (2026-07-31) → This is the biggest threat to the reverse-DCF base: it should start from normalised cash taxes, not FY26 operating cash flow. → medium (the swing is my estimate; the company did not quantify the reversal).

**7. Mailchimp proceeds: buybacks, not debt paydown.** Total about $3.68B: $3.31B cash plus $368M in Bending Spoons shares (new equity stake, six-month sale restriction, value reset at close). Close expected early 2027; outside date 2027-08-19 (plus 3 months for regulatory approvals); needs HSR clearance. The company says it intends to use cash proceeds to repurchase shares within FY27 after close; nothing on debt paydown, and the July 2027 notes are covered by cash. Proceeds are before tax on the gain (not disclosed). The $3.3B is only the cash piece and is below the $6.4B authorisation left, but with Q1's $1.5B pace for three more quarters (about $4.5B) the authorisation would need to grow. Also guidance excludes the sale. → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000119312526468834/d182669d8k.htm (2026-11-19); https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000043/fy27q1earningspressrelease.htm (2026-11-19) → The $3.3B buyback is plausible but is "intends", not a commitment, and may replace, not add to, regular buybacks. At $271.72 it retires about 12M shares (4.6%), my arithmetic before tax. Mailchimp's $313M quarterly revenue leaves with it. → high on terms, medium on incrementality.

**8. Off-balance-sheet and other claims.** Operating lease liabilities $789M ($937M undiscounted), plus $56M signed not started (Jul 2026). Purchase obligations $4.93B, mostly a cloud services agreement ($1,160M due within a year; $2,079M in years 2-3), which is a fixed hosting commitment as AI use grows. Secured facilities $970M are non-recourse (loan-funding vehicles); $2.1B of business loans sold in FY26 (servicing income not material); notes receivable held for investment $1.57B. Customer funds $5.7B are matched by equal payables. Deferred comp $308M. Short-term refund-advance facility ($5.8B, Jan 30 to terminated Feb 26, 2026) and commercial paper upsized to $3.2B then back to $2.2B; nothing outstanding at year end. → 10-K and 10-Q above → Real but modest; the cloud commitment is the one to watch if consumer volume falls. → high.
