# INTU dd-transcripts (run 20261126-full_hunt, written 2026-11-28)

Basis: Intuit's own prepared remarks (earnings scripts) read in full or in part for Q1 FY27, Q4 FY26 and Q3 FY26. Q&A comes only from third-party summaries and is flagged. No Q&A transcript for Q1 FY27 was reachable. Quotes are short phrases; shifts are described in my own words.

## Summary for main thread
1. Consumer voice moved from "raise prices, tax is fine" (Feb 2026) to "we lost quality DIY customers to lower-cost providers; price is the top reason people leave TurboTax" (Aug 2026), then to "Credit Karma below expectations" with AI discovery named as a cause (Nov 2026). Real Consumer-side share and relevance pressure, now admitted.
2. The business side voice is backed by numbers: Business Money payment volume +33% (Q1 FY27), up from +30%; mid-market Online Ecosystem +41%. Management still guides GBS +13-14% for FY27.
3. Dodges: no TurboTax or Credit Karma customer counts; no date for reacceleration; no split of Credit Karma weakness between rates and AI (third-party Q&A summary).

Thesis impact: neutral

## Findings

**F1. Credit Karma: from momentum to an admitted miss, with AI named as a cause**
- Fact: Credit Karma growth ran +32% (FY25), +23% (Q2 FY26, third-party), +15% (Q3), +16% (Q4), then +9% in Q1 FY27. In Q1 management said performance was "below our expectations" from higher rates and a "faster-than-anticipated" consumer shift to starting in AI-powered tools. FY27 guide cut to +3-7% (it was +11-13% three months earlier). Q2 guide assumes demand stays at current levels.
- Source: https://investors.intuit.com/_assets/_7a9f62942b3b9f5151594b96c12aa9df/intuit/db/946/10425/webcast_transcript/Q1_FY27_Earnings_Script.pdf (2026-11-19); Q3 script https://investors.intuit.com/_assets/_82269698e57f0291e947d183719775e9/intuit/db/946/10372/webcast_transcript/Q3FY26+Earnings+Script.pdf (2026-05-20); FY25 and Q2 FY26 figures from search summaries (Fool/Zacks, third-party, not primary).
- Means: Earlier calls credited personal loans and insurance and spoke of "momentum". The new wording puts the cause outside the company (rates, consumer behaviour). The fix described (brand campaign, "top choice in AI-powered experiences") is a plan, not a result. Credit Karma is about 12% of FY26 revenue ($2.6B per triage), so the cut matters to growth but offsets against GBS strength.
- Confidence: high on the numbers, medium on interpretation.

**F2. TurboTax: framing of share loss shifted over four calls**
- Fact: Aug 2025 (Q4 FY25, search summary): units down 2% described as yielding share in lower-revenue customers. Feb 2026 (Q2 FY26, Fool summary): management said tax-season filing dip was timing and planned to raise prices over time. May 2026 (Q3 script): "did not have the overall tax season we expected", expected online paying units +2% "on share gains from higher ARPU filers", pay-nothing customers about 7M vs 8M (search summary), and a need to "evolve" low-end DIY. Aug 2026 (Q4 script): "we lost quality DIY customers to lower-cost providers", price is the number one reason customers leave. Nov 2026: goal is to "regain share of total IRS e-filers"; tax revenue per customer expected to fall in FY27; TurboTax Q2 revenue guided down year over year; FY27 TurboTax +2-3%.
- Source: Q4 FY26 script https://investors.intuit.com/_assets/_7a9f62942b3b9f5151594b96c12aa9df/intuit/db/946/10378/webcast_transcript/Q4+FY26+Earnings+Script.pdf (2026-08-25); Q1 FY27 and Q3 scripts above; Fool Q2 FY26 https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/26/intuit-intu-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript (2026-02-26, third-party).
- Means: The "timing" explanation in February did not hold; by August the company called it a lost-customer problem. The May claim of unit growth sits awkwardly beside the August admission and the 10-K's fewer federal units (triage). The strategy is a deliberate price cut to buy back filers. Delivery test is the Q3 FY27 season (May 2027), not anything said so far.
- Confidence: high that the wording moved; medium on whether May unit guidance was met (actual TurboTax unit comparison not confirmed by me).

**F3. TurboTax Live and assisted tax is the working half; DIY is the broken half**
- Fact: TurboTax Live customers +38%, revenue +36%, 53% of TurboTax revenue (Q3 script). Over three-quarters of Live additions were upgrades from DIY, and management expects those upgrades to "moderate" (Q4). New-to-franchise Live customers grew 15%. "Sign and File" tested in the extension season at a "disruptive price point"; about twice as many opted in versus expert review alone (early test, Q1 FY27).
- Source: Q3, Q4 and Q1 FY27 scripts above.
- Means: Growth in Live came mostly from the existing DIY base, which is the same base now leaving on price. The next leg depends on new-to-franchise filers, which grew far slower (+15% on a smaller base). Extension-season tests are small samples.
- Confidence: medium.

**F4. Business money and payments: voice and numbers agree**
- Fact: Business Money total payment volume +33% in Q1 FY27 (payments, bill pay, buy now pay later); +30% in Q3 FY26 and over $225B online volume for FY26. Money revenue growth in Q1 came from payments and Capital (working-capital loan volume). QuickBooks Online accounting +19%, Online Services +21%. Mid-market Online Ecosystem +41%, IES annualized revenue $177M, mid-market customers +30% (helped by an August promotional price).
- Source: Q1 FY27 script; Q4 FY26 script (2026-08-25).
- Means: Management speaks about Money in plain numbers and the figure is accelerating, supporting the survivor thesis on the GBS side. Note part of mid-market customer growth was promotion-driven, and Capital growth is loan-volume driven (rate and credit sensitive).
- Confidence: high.

**F5. AI agents: reframed from threat to distribution, but early and unproven at scale**
- Fact: Management now calls AI tools "new front doors" and says Intuit supplies the "system of intelligence" behind them. It signed partnerships with Meta, Microsoft and a third (SpaceXSI); QuickBooks sits inside Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity; TurboTax and Credit Karma inside Claude and ChatGPT. Over 2 million QuickBooks calls a week; over 30% of connected QuickBooks customers engage weekly; connector payments run rate over $1B annualized (about 0.4% of the $225B+ payment volume). Self-reported claim: TurboTax ranked first in AI answers about four times as often as the next tax brand (no method given).
- Source: Q1 FY27 script (2026-11-19). Feb 2026 Q&A (Fool, third-party): management said proprietary data and models stay in-house and partners get no share of economics.
- Means: Confirms Intuit is trying to be the engine behind AI front doors rather than the front door itself. That works for QuickBooks (customer already holds the books) and is exactly what Credit Karma lacks, since referral revenue depends on who owns discovery. The Q1 wording conceded Credit Karma is behind QuickBooks and TurboTax on AI discovery ("applying those best practices").
- Confidence: medium.

**F6. Dodged or unanswered questions (third-party Q&A summary of the Q4 FY26 call, Aug 2026)**
- Fact: Asked whether FY27 is the bottom or structural, the CEO framed the reset as offensive ("we will be the disruptor") without ruling out structural pressure. Asked when growth reaccelerates, management declined a FY28 timetable and said to judge quarterly. A question on why the long-term GBS growth outlook fell from 15-20% to 10-15% got "we are creating the pressure" rather than a competitive answer. No split of guide cut between market pressure and prudence. Online paying customers +3% (8.9M), about 2 points slower, with no specific cause given.
- Source: https://www.tradingkey.com/news/transcripts/262131467-tradingkey (call 2026-08-25; summary partly garbled by audio problems, third-party, treat as medium reliability). The long-term GBS cut is also only in this summary; I did not find it in an Intuit document.
- Means: The consistent non-answers are on timing and on cause. The GBS long-term range cut, if accurate, is the most important unverified item for the survivor thesis (GBS is the leg we lean on).
- Confidence: medium-low.

**F7. Promises versus delivery**
- Delivered: Q1 FY27 revenue, operating income and EPS above the top of guidance (Q1 script); FY26 results above initial guide (triage). Dividend raised 15% to $1.38; Q1 buyback $1.5B (+73%); plan to use Mailchimp proceeds for extra buybacks after close.
- Not delivered or reversed: Credit Karma FY27 guide cut inside three months; Feb 2026 "timing" and price-increase talk reversed; Mailchimp "double-digit growth beyond FY26" (Feb) ended in a sale agreement (Nov) with revenue flat to down 1% guided. Q1 left operating income guidance unchanged because lower stock pay offsets the Credit Karma shortfall, which means the profit guide is held up by a cost line, not by revenue.
- Source: Q1 FY27 script; Fool Q2 FY26.
- Means: Mixed record. Cost and margin promises are kept; Consumer revenue promises have been missed twice in 9 months.
- Confidence: medium-high.
