# INTU dd-edgar (EDGAR layer), run 20261126-full_hunt, as of 2026-11-28

## Summary for main thread
1. **The "GBS carries most of the profit" claim is only true on a measure that leaves out the biggest costs.** Segment operating income is $16.2B, but product development and customer success sit in $7.3B of unallocated "other corporate expenses" (FY26), so no filing shows true segment profit. GBS share of segment OI is 61% as filed (56% is the triage's recast figure, not verifiable from the recast exhibit I could read).
2. **New legal risk:** two securities class actions (filed Jul 10 and Aug 17, 2026) allege false statements in 2025-26. Not in the FY25 10-K.
3. **Mailchimp:** $3.68B ($3.31B cash, $368M Bending Spoons stock). It is a shrinking business (-1%), sold at about 2.9x revenue. Cash is earmarked for buybacks. No termination fee found in the agreement text.

Risk-factor text on AI, Direct File and competition is almost unchanged year over year. The filings do not show the consumer side getting better.

Thesis impact: neutral

## Findings

**F1. Segment profit is not a clean measure of GBS strength.** GBS FY26 revenue $12,864M (+16%), segment OI $9,887M (+17%); Consumer revenue $8,584M (+11%), OI $6,308M (+10%). Segment OI totals $16,195M against GAAP operating income $5,884M. The gap includes other corporate expenses $7,303M (+9% on $6,693M), SBC $2,056M, amortisation $659M and restructuring $293M. The FY25 10-K says technology and customer success costs were moved out of segments in Aug 2024 (about $1.4B from GBS, $573M from Consumer, FY24 basis). → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000037/intu-20260731.htm (Note 14, 2026-09-09) → The 56-61% share is before shared platform costs, so GBS's true profit share is unknown and probably lower than shown, because GBS is the larger user of the shared cost pool (platform costs, including AI build-out). GBS growth of 15-17% is real; the profit-share figure is not. Confidence: high on the facts, medium on the inference.

**F2. Consumer (Zoom test): revenue still grew, units and credit-card referrals are the weak spots.** FY26 Consumer +11%, with Credit Karma +$441M (personal loans +$235M, cards +$101M, insurance +$85M) and TurboTax +$363M "partially offset by fewer TurboTax federal units". Q1 FY27 Consumer +7% ($958M); Credit Karma credit card vertical -$16M, "impacted by higher interest rates and a shift in consumer behavior"; the Consumer segment OI is $628M (+8%). The filings use "AI" only as a competitive risk, not a quantified share loss. → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000045/intu-20261031.htm (2026-11-19) → The filings confirm falling TurboTax units and a card-referral decline, but show no collapse; Consumer is still growing and still the customer base that feeds the money products. They do not give unit counts, so the Zoom test cannot be settled from EDGAR. Confidence: medium.

**F3. Risk-factor diff FY25 → FY26 (147 vs 146 paragraphs; I compared them paragraph by paragraph).** Changes found: (a) Direct File wording went from "the IRS made available a free direct filing system and has stated it will explore ways to expand" to "although the IRS free direct filing system was suspended, proponents continue to advocate" (legacy Free File risk kept); (b) new sentence that AI enhancements may not be "adopted, or monetizable"; (c) new stock-price risk naming "perceptions about the impact of evolving technologies, including AI"; (d) "large language models" added to the cloud-dependence risk; (e) AI named as a new security attack vector; (f) a new line that Credit Karma data use is under an FTC order. No new Direct File or AI-competitor entry beyond these. Sources: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000037/intu-20260731.htm and https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687825000035/intu-20250731.htm (2026-09-09; 2025-09-03). → Management added AI-demand and AI-sentiment language but did not add a distinct new risk factor; the Direct File threat was reduced. Confidence: high.

**F4. New: two shareholder class actions and an Ontario certification.** Suits filed Jul 10 and Aug 17, 2026 (N.D. Cal., Exchange Act 10(b) and 20(a)) over statements "in 2025 and 2026"; no lead plaintiff yet. The Ontario free-filing class was certified Jul 24, 2026 (Intuit expects to appeal). Fifth Circuit action on the FTC order is Mar 20, 2026. FY25 note listed none of these. → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000037/intu-20260731.htm (Note 13, 2026-09-09) → Loss not estimable per the filing; a signal that investors claim they were misled about 2025-26 outlook. Confidence: high.

**F5. Mailchimp sale terms (8-K Items 1.01/8.01, 2026-11-19).** Buyer Bending Spoons US Inc. Base price $3.68B, all cash before adjustment; Intuit reinvests about $368M for 8,818,595 Bending Spoons shares at closing, so cash is about $3.31B before tax and adjustments. Conditions: HSR and other approvals, no Burdensome Condition, no MAE; no financing condition; no Intuit shareholder vote. Outside date Aug 19, 2027 (+3 months for approvals). Intuit gives a 2-year non-compete. Intuit says it will use cash proceeds to repurchase shares within FY27 after closing. A text search of EX-2.01 found no termination fee. Mailchimp revenue was $1,272M in FY26 (about 2.9x sale price) and Q1 FY27 revenue fell 1% to $313M, with segment OI $202M (65% margin on a basis that now includes product development). → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000119312526468834/d182669d8k.htm ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000043/fy27q1earningspressrelease.htm (2026-11-19) → The $3.3B buyback at about $272 retires about 12M shares (4.6% of 264.0M). The sale removes a shrinking unit; it also removes about $0.8B a year of segment OI on the new basis, so it is not free earnings-wise. Confidence: high on terms, medium on earnings effect.

**F6. Buybacks are outrunning dilution, but at higher prices.** Shares outstanding 264.0M (Nov 12, 2026) vs weighted 279M in Q1 FY26 (-4.3%). Q1 FY27 repurchases $1.5B for 4.6M shares (about $326 each); FY26 $5.4B. Remaining authorisation $6.4B. S-8 filings: last new S-8s Feb 2023 and Feb 2024 (plan amendments); the Sep 2026 S-8 POS only deregisters old acquired-company plans (Mint, Demandforce, etc.), so it adds no dilution. SBC $2,056M (9.6% of revenue). On Mar 16, 2026 the founder and executive team ended all 10b5-1 sale plans and the company said it would accelerate buybacks. → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000045/intu-20261031.htm ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000017/intu-20260316.htm (2026-11-19; 2026-03-16) → Dilution is covered; the cost is that FY26 repurchases at about $410 are far above today's price. Confidence: high.

**F7. Pay metrics.** Annual bonus tied to revenue and non-GAAP operating income; the CEO's bonus paid 90% of target against 112.6% pool funding. Long-term PSUs are relative TSR; the 2023 PSU 36-month leg paid 0% (TSR below the 25th percentile), overall 66%. Say-on-pay support 93.2%. Mix is about 96% variable. → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000053/intu-20261125.htm (2026-11-25) → Pay is tied to revenue and non-GAAP profit, which excludes SBC; TSR leg shows real penalty. Confidence: high.

**F8. Auditor and controls.** EY (auditor since 1990) clean opinion on statements and internal control; one critical audit matter: determining distinct performance obligations in revenue contracts. No material weakness. → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000037/intu-20260731.htm (2026-09-09) → Revenue-recognition judgment is routine; no red flag. Confidence: high.

**F9. Restructuring, debt and leadership.** 8-K 2.05 (2026-05-20): about 17% workforce cut, charges $300-340M; 10-K shows $293M in FY26. Debt: Jan 2026 $2.2B revolver to 2031 and $5.8B seasonal refund-advance facility (matured Mar 31, 2026), both with a maximum leverage covenant; Jun 2026 $750M 4.95% notes due 2031 and $1B 5.5% due 2036, aimed at the $750M 5.25% 2026 and $500M 1.35% 2027 notes. Apr 2026 8-K 5.02: the Small Business Group head stepped down and Mid-Market head took both. → https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000024/intu-20260520.htm ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000119312526267866/d159651d8k.htm ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/896878/000089687826000020/intu-20260428.htm (as of 2026-05-20, 2026-06-11, 2026-04-28) → Refinancing is done at about 5% coupons; no maturity problem seen here (dd-credit owns the ladder). The GBS leadership change is the only small-business leadership event; its link to the guidance cut is unproven. Confidence: medium.
