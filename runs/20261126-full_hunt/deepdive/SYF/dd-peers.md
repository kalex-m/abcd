# SYF dd-peers (run 20261126-full_hunt; as-of 2026-11-27)

## Summary for main thread
1. Peers show the SAME direction as Synchrony: COF, AXP and Bread all report lower charge-offs and delinquency than a year ago and high-single-digit spend growth. SYF's credit improvement is a sector tailwind, not company-specific skill. SYF's year-on-year delinquency gain (about -10bp) is the smallest of the group (BFH -41bp Oct).
2. Peers released reserves (COF $225M, AXP release, BFH reserve rate -60bp) while SYF built reserves in Q3. This supports "Q3 EPS dip is reserve timing", but also shows SYF is the one leaning cautious.
3. No partner filing names a program ending. Lowe's 10-K confirms Synchrony as sole card issuer; PayPal's 10-Q shows rising revenue from its card partner. Partner filings otherwise silent on SYF (not proof of renewal).

Thesis impact: supports

## Findings

**F1. Peer credit is improving in step with SYF.**
- COF Q3 2026: domestic card net charge-off (NCO) rate 3.98% vs 4.63% a year ago; 30+ day delinquency 3.72% vs 3.84%; $225M reserve release. Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927628/000092762826000142/ex992q32026earningsrelease.htm and ex991 (2026-10-20).
- AXP Q3 2026: net write-off rate (principal) 1.8% vs 1.9%; provisions $985M vs $1,287M on a reserve release vs a prior-year build; U.S. consumer 30+ days past due 1.2% vs 1.3%. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/4962/000000496226000405/q326exhibit992.htm (2026-10-23).
- BFH Oct 2026 (monthly): net principal loss rate 6.69% vs 7.50%; delinquency 5.64% vs 6.05%. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1101215/000110121526000102/october2026creditstatsex99.htm (2026-11-10). Q3: loss rate 6.40%, reserve rate down 60bp to 11.12%; FY net loss outlook cut to below 7%. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1101215/000110121526000087/exh9913q26bfhepr_final.htm (2026-10-22).
- SYF Q3: NCO 4.79% vs 5.16% (-37bp); reserve ratio 10.01% vs 10.35%. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1601712/000160171226000043/syf-20260930.htm (2026-10-22).
- Means: the triage "credit is better than a year ago" is confirmed, but it is industry-wide. Not a hidden SYF edge; the "mistake" must rest on price, not credit alone. Confidence: high.

**F2. SYF's improvement is the smallest in the group.** SYF delinquency 4.4% vs 4.5% (Oct; triage figure, from SYF's 8-K) against BFH -41bp, COF card -12bp (Q3). SYF's NCO fell 37bp vs COF domestic -65bp and BFH -81bp (Oct). Means: SYF started from a lower loss rate than BFH, so less room; or the Q3 SYF book (Lifestyle -1%, mix) is not improving as fast. If the sector tailwind fades (vintages seasoned), SYF has less cushion from further improvement. Confidence: medium (different loss definitions and mix across issuers).

**F3. Reserve behaviour diverges.** COF released $225M, AXP released, BFH lowered its rate; SYF's provision rose $120M in Q3 on a reserve build vs a prior-year release (SYF 10-Q, as above). SYF's ratio still fell 34bp year on year. Means: the "reserve timing, not worse credit" read holds; SYF building while peers release is more conservative, which is a mild positive for later release if credit holds. Confidence: medium.

**F4. Spend is solid in nominal terms across all four.** SYF purchase volume $49.7B vs $46.0B (+8%); BFH credit sales +9%; AXP total billed business +8% ($454.0B vs $421.0B); COF domestic card purchase volume $250.0B vs $226.1B (+11%, includes Brex and acquisition effects). Sources: SYF 10-Q, BFH release, AXP exhibit 99.1 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/4962/000000496226000405/q326exhibit991.htm), COF supplement. Means: card spend does not yet show the "real spending flat in October" slowdown in the regime read; Q3 data predates it. Confidence: medium.

**F5. Industry data agrees but shows a plateau.** Fed all-bank card charge-off rate 3.82% in 2026 Q2 vs 4.18% in 2025 Q3; delinquency 2.85% vs 2.99% (https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/chargeoff/chgallsa.htm and delallsa.htm, updated 2026-08-25; the fetch tool flagged that column labels were inferred). NY Fed: flow into serious card delinquency 6.87% in Q3 2026 vs 7.05% a year ago; card balances $1.29T, +$52B y/y (https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20261110, 2026-11-10). Fed G.19 revolving credit +14.5% annualised in September after -4.4% in August; Q3 +4.1% (https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm, 2026-11-06). Means: improvement is real but flattening (Fed charge-offs flat Q1 to Q2). Confidence: high on direction.

**F6. Income divide is a watch item for a non-prime-heavy lender.** NY Fed Liberty Street (2026-11-10): card delinquency rise since 2022 was steepest in lower-income neighborhoods and the gap has widened; cards issued since early 2023 went to higher-score borrowers and authors expect new-card delinquency to keep improving absent a shock. https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/11/a-look-at-the-wider-divide-in-credit-card-delinquency-by-income/. Means: supports SYF's tighter post-2023 underwriting story; a shock (Slowdown regime) would hit lower-income first. Confidence: medium.

**F7. Lowe's confirms Synchrony is its sole credit provider, with a buy-back option.** Lowe's FY2025 10-K: credit is extended directly by Synchrony Bank, which performs all program services; Lowe's "has the option, but no obligation, to purchase the receivables at the end of the agreement." https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/60667/000006066726000029/low-20260130.htm (filed 2026-03-23). The Nov 2026 Lowe's 10-Q (filed 2026-11-25) does not mention credit programs. Means: confirms the relationship but the filing gives no term or expiry; an end-of-term option is a built-in exit route for a >10% partner. Term must come from SYF exhibits (dd-edgar/dd-ir). Confidence: high on fact; renewal status unknown.

**F8. PayPal's card partner is earning more.** PayPal Q3 2026 10-Q: nine-month revenue from an unnamed "independent chartered financial institution" (revenue share on the U.S. revolving credit product and PayPal/Venmo cards) up about $90M; PayPal bought $1.2B of consumer receivables from a partner institution in nine months vs $934M. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1633917/000163391726000088/pypl-20260930.htm (2026-10-27). Means: PayPal does not name Synchrony; SYF names PayPal and Venmo as digital partners (SYF 8-K 2026-06-29, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1601712/000160171226000027/projectmoonstonefinalpress.htm). The rising PayPal revenue-share is consistent with healthy program economics, but the installment receivables partner may be a different bank; do not over-read. Confidence: low-medium.

**F9. Walmart/OnePay exists; Walmart filings are thin.** SYF 10-Q lists OnePay as exclusive issuer of the general-purpose and private-label program at Walmart. Walmart's FY2026 10-K names OnePay and "co-branded credit cards" as risk areas but not Synchrony (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000055/wmt-20260131.htm, 2026-03-13); Walmart's Nov 19 2026 release has no OnePay or credit-card text (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/104169/000010416926000168/earningsreleasefy27q3.htm). Means: no confirmation or contradiction from Walmart's side; ramp must be judged from SYF. Confidence: low.

**F10. No lost partner visible; non-renewed programs are tiny.** SYF's "Corp, Other" bucket (programs not renewed or terminated) holds $81M of receivables vs $113M a year ago and zero purchase volume. SYF 10-Q lists new and extended programs (more than 40), none among the top five. Amazon's 10-K only warns co-brand programs could hurt if "renewed on less favorable terms or terminated" (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872426000004/amzn-20251231.htm, 2026-02-06). Means: no evidence of a top-5 exit; also no evidence of renewal terms. Confidence: medium.

**F11. Bread shows partners renew when programs perform.** BFH renewed Signet (adds Blue Nile) and Sephora, two top-10 partners, and Moody's upgraded it to Ba1 (BFH Q3 release above). Means: sector-wide partner renewals are occurring; supports the idea that retail-card programs remain valued. Confidence: medium.
