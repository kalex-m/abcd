# SW (Smurfit Westrock) red-team, written 2026-11-21

Sources: card.py SW (price $40.33 on 2026-11-18, EDGAR XBRL), 10-Q for period 2026-09-30 filed 2026-11-04: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2005951/000162828026069766/smur-20260930.htm ; 10-K filed 2026-02-27.

## 1. The strongest bear case
Smurfit Westrock is a leveraged cyclical whose cash flow does not cover its dividend, in the year after a merger that was supposed to fix that. For the nine months to 2026-09-30, operating cash flow was $1,843M (down $354M from $2,197M), capital expenditures were $1,603M, so free cash flow was $240M, against $712M of dividends paid: a $472M gap, which management filled with a $334M net increase in debt (10-Q cash flow statement and MD&A). Total debt is $14,049M (up from $13,773M at year-end 2025) against cash of $723M, with $842M current (vs $346M at year-end) and commercial paper up from $155M to $518M. Earnings are going the wrong way: nine-month net income fell 38% ($602M to $376M), operating profit fell 21% ($1,330M to $1,049M), gross profit fell $554M ($4,661M to $4,107M) on revenue up only 1.3% ($23.6B to $23.9B); Q3 segment Adjusted EBITDA was $1,265M vs $1,345M, with North America down $50M (-6%) on "lower volumes of $196 million" and higher freight and energy costs. Nine-month segment EBITDA of $3,600M vs $3,881M is -7%. The yfinance forward P/E of 12.5 only works if earnings rebound, while trailing P/E is 30x (vs a 10-year median of 19.8x) and EV/EBIT 19.6x vs 13.7x median; FCF yield on FY2025 was 5.7% on $1.20B, which already needed $2.19B of capex (the company has been in an upgrade-and-closure cycle: impairment and restructuring of $199M in 2026 nine months after $360M in 2025's, and accelerated depreciation of machine closures is in D&A of $2,084M, up from $1,875M). The shares were issued, not bought: weighted shares went from 261M (2022) to 526M (2025), so per-share history is not comparable and the dividend ($1.36 per share declared in the nine months, about $712M) now eats all free cash flow in a year when packaging volumes are weak. Return on invested capital is 4.1% (5y average 6.9%), below any reasonable cost of capital, so this is a business that destroys value at the current price level of containerboard. The takeaway: this is a levered bet on a box-price recovery that has not arrived, with the dividend funded by borrowing.

## 2. The single scariest fact
Quote (from the cash flow statement, nine months ended 2026-09-30): net cash provided by operating activities $1,843M; capital expenditures $(1,603)M; cash dividends paid to shareholders $(712)M; and MD&A: "The Company's net cash inflow from changes in debt was $334 million". (10-Q for period 2026-09-30, filed 2026-11-04, URL above.)
Why it matters: after capex the company generated $240M and paid out $712M, so about two-thirds of the dividend came from new borrowing, while total debt is already $14.0B. If volumes do not recover, the choices are a dividend cut, a capex cut that damages the mills, or more debt.

## 3. Pre-mortem (November 2029, stock down 50% to about $20)
Containerboard demand stays flat and new capacity in North America keeps prices from recovering; EBITDA stays near the roughly $4.8B annualised pace of 2026 (nine-month segment EBITDA $3,600M) instead of rising to the $6B+ management implied after the merger; interest at about $700M a year and capex near $2.1B leave no free cash flow; net debt stays above $13B, so leverage is above 3x and a recession or a rise in refinancing costs pushes it past 4x. The company says "the Board has decided to rebase the dividend" and "reprioritise capital allocation", cuts the dividend by half or more, the income buyers leave, and the stock re-rates to 6x EBITDA on lower earnings.

## 4. Other wounds
- Interest: net interest expense of $519M in nine months 2026 (about $690M a year) is 49% of operating profit of $1,049M; the dividend is not even the largest claim.
- Pricing and volume: North America volume down enough to cost $196M of profit in one quarter; "typically a three- to six-month lag" before price increases are realized (risk factors), so announced price rises do not show up on time.
- Closures that never end: $360M of impairment and restructuring in 2025 nine months and $199M in 2026 nine months, plus the April 2025 closures (St. Paul, Forney, two German plants); "past the restructuring" is not what the numbers show.
- Legal/regulatory: Italian Competition Authority (AGCM) cartel fine investigation on Smurfit Kappa Italia since August 2019; the 10-Q shows only a $13M reduction in the fine and costs this quarter. I did not verify the total amount at stake.
- Return on capital: ROIC of 4.1% on net PP&E of $22.5B (2026-09-30) much of it marked at the Westrock merger, so further impairment is plausible (nine-month 2026 impairments already $108M); 3-year impairments already $0.28B.

## 5. What the bulls will say, and why it is not enough
Bulls: the Smurfit Kappa management team has a record of integration, trough earnings, a 12.5x forward P/E and a dividend yield of about 4.5% ($1.36 per share declared in nine months, annualised, on $40.33) on a market cap of $21B, with box demand cyclical and due to turn. Answer: the dividend yield is the warning light, not the reward: it is paid out of debt in the first nine months of 2026, the three post-merger years have produced falling profit rather than synergy-led growth (nine-month net income -38%), and a "cyclical bottom" call has so far been wrong (profit still falling in 2026); a recovery must be large to cover capex and interest and also leave return to equity.

## 6. Verdict
WOUNDED, leaning DEAD for a mispricing thesis. The business is real, but a dividend uncovered by $472M in nine months, falling profit and $14B of debt mean it should be sized small and only owned with a view on box prices.
