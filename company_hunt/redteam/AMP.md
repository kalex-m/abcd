# AMP (Ameriprise Financial) red-team, written 2026-11-21

Sources: card.py AMP (yfinance prices to 2026-11-18, EDGAR XBRL), 10-Q for 2026-09-30 filed 2026-10-30: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/820027/000082002726000054/amp-20260930.htm

## 1. The strongest bear case
The screen headline is an 18.7% free cash flow yield and a P/FCF of 5.3, and it is an artifact. FY2025 operating cash flow of $8.32B is 2.3x net income of $3.56B, and the 5-year OCF/NI ratio is 1.70, because Ameriprise is a bank, an insurer and a broker-dealer in one cash-flow statement: operating cash flow swings with derivative collateral posted by counterparties, brokerage and certificate balances, and consolidated investment entities. The 10-Q says nine-month operating cash flow fell $838M to $4.8B "primarily reflecting a decrease in cash collateral received from derivative counterparties". That is borrowed-from-counterparty cash, not earnings. The real earnings number is adjusted operating earnings of $4.16B trailing (to 2026-09-30), and on that the stock at $501 and about 91.4M diluted shares (about $45.8B) trades near 11x, which is a fair price for a business whose results are the market level. Q3 2026 adjusted EPS was $11.26 (+14%), but the drivers are fee revenue up 15% on equity-market appreciation: wrap assets went from $615B to $731B to $729B and the market moved -$7.5B in Q3 alone, so the earnings are a levered long position in the S&P. The parts that are not market beta are weak. Asset Management (Columbia Threadneedle) had net outflows in every line: Global Retail Funds -$4.4B and Global Institutional -$6.4B in Q3 2026, institutional outflows of $50.9B vs inflows of $37.4B over nine months, i.e. -$13.5B. Retirement & Protection Solutions adjusted earnings fell 39% in Q3 ($217M to $132M) and 19% over nine months ($646M to $524M), with unlocking charges of $91M in Q3 from worse variable annuity surrender and utilization and LTC morbidity assumptions; the prior-year Q3 review also had net unfavorable VA and LTC changes (same filing). Certificate balances at Ameriprise Certificate Company have shrunk for eleven straight quarters ($8.9B to $7.3B). Buybacks of $2.57B in nine months (about 6% of the market cap a year) are what lifts EPS growth to 14%; adjusted operating income is growing mid-single to low-double digits, and the stock is at 3-year highs, 12.2x GAAP earnings against a 10-year median of 12.5x, so there is no discount left. The takeaway: the stock is not cheap on a clean measure, it is fairly priced beta with a hidden free-cash-flow mirage on top.

## 2. The single scariest fact
Quote: "Net cash provided by operating activities decreased $838 million to $4.8 billion for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 compared to $5.7 billion for the prior year period primarily reflecting a decrease in cash collateral received from derivative counterparties as they increased use of securities collateral." (10-Q for period 2026-09-30, filed 2026-10-30, URL above.)
Why it matters: anyone who buys on the 18% FCF yield is counting collateral that sits on the balance sheet as a matching liability. Real distributable cash is closer to the parent-company dividend capacity from subsidiaries, which is tied to earnings (about $4.2B), not $8B. Also in the same filing: the Q3 earnings-driver table shows a $128M market-impact benefit (excluded from adjusted earnings) and a $91M unlocking hit inside them.

## 3. Pre-mortem (November 2029, stock down 50% to about $250)
The S&P falls 25-30% and stays down 12-18 months, as in 2022 (AMP was $223 at the 2022 low, from $281 a year before it). Wrap assets drop from about $730B to about $540B, fee revenue falls about 12-15%, and because distribution expenses (paid out to advisors, $1.91B of $2.38B segment expense in Q3 2026) are only half variable, Advice & Wealth adjusted earnings fall 25%. Asset Management outflows of $10B+ a quarter turn from a drag into a spiral, Retirement & Protection takes a third unlocking charge, and the company says "market conditions" while cutting the buyback from $3.4B to $1.5B. EPS falls from about $45 to about $33 and the multiple goes from 11x to 8x on the cut buyback. 50% down.

## 4. Other wounds
- Advisor and cash sweep economics: client cash is $35.4B total, bank deposits at 0.22% paid; falling rates already hit certificates (crediting rate 3.48% to 3.04%) and the sweep spread. A rate-cut cycle shrinks net investment income at Advice & Wealth (net investment income line down 5% y/y in Q3 2026). Cash sweep litigation across the industry (Wells, Morgan Stanley, Merrill) is a live legal risk; not verified for AMP specifically.
- Long term care and annuity tail: LTC interest expense on reserves was $200M vs $122M of premiums in nine months 2026 (an obvious loss-making closed block), and net unfavorable assumption changes recur.
- Share count math: diluted shares fell from 168M (2016) to 98M (2025) and 91.4M in Q3 2026; at $501 and a 3-year-high price each dollar of buyback retires fewer shares, so EPS growth at 12-15% is partly mechanical.
- Leverage inside the structure: Ameriprise Bank holds $20.3B available-for-sale securities against $23.6B deposits; unrealized losses sit in AOCI of -$1.4B, equity ex-AOCI is $7.5B so adjusted ROE above 50% is a function of a thin equity base.
- Debt maturities are not a problem (long-term debt $3.3B, next maturity 2028 $600M). I could not verify any regulatory finding that would change that.

## 5. What the bulls will say, and why it is not enough
Bulls: 13% share count shrink in three years, 30%+ ROE, record wrap inflows ($4.9B in Q3), earnings growth of 12-15%, 10x forward earnings, a franchise with sticky advisors. Answer: all true and already in a 12x multiple at a 3-year high; most earnings are market-linked (my estimate, not a filed figure), 1/3 of the group (Asset Management plus Retirement) is shrinking or stagnant ex-market, and the cash flow bulls point to is inflated by collateral flows. A 12x multiple on peak-cycle market-sensitive earnings is what you pay for that, not a mispricing.

## 6. Verdict
WOUNDED. Quality compounder at a fair price, not mispriced; the FCF-yield argument is a mirage, and a 25% market drop plausibly halves the thesis return.
