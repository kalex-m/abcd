# MBGL (Mobility Global / CARFAX) red-team, run 2026-11-21

Sources: card.py (EDGAR + yfinance 2026-11-18); 10-Q for quarter to 2026-09-30, filed 2026-11-12 https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2090312/000209031226000035/mbgl-20260930.htm ; Q3 earnings 8-K filed 2026-11-12 https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2090312/000209031226000034/mbgl-20261112.htm . Note: no 10-K exists (spun off 2026-07-01), so all history is S&P carve-out numbers. I could not open the earnings-release text directly; guidance and the "$22M pricing" detail below come from a WebSearch summary of the Q3 release and are marked "not verified in the filing".

## 1. Strongest bear case
Every historical number on this stock is fiction in a specific way: it is a carve-out from S&P Global, with S&P's allocations, no real interest, no real standalone costs and cash swept to the parent. The card's 6.5% FCF yield ($0.46B 2025) is before the $2.0B of new senior notes (5.05% 2029, 5.45% 2031, 6.05% 2036) that were issued to pay S&P a $2.0B dividend on the way out. Interest expense and other jumped to $26M in Q3 2026 from $4M, selling and general expenses jumped to $203M from $135M (+50%), and operating profit fell to $92M from $104M despite revenue up 7% ($478M vs $445M). Net income fell 41% to $42M ($71M a year ago) and nine-month net income fell to $150M from $194M. The nine-month GAAP numbers carry $73M of transaction costs, but even adding those back, Q3 profit before one-offs is falling while revenue rises, and Q4 is guided as the lowest margin quarter with a full quarter of standalone cost (WebSearch summary of release, not verified in filing). The B2B segment is the clearest decay: Q3 revenue +6% to $164M but Adjusted EBITDA down 13% to $45M from $52M (margin 27% vs 34%). Growth quality is poor: about $22M of Q3's $33M revenue gain came from pricing actions (WebSearch summary, not verified in filing), meaning volume added roughly 2-3% and the business is mostly raising prices on dealers and consumers who can leave. Net debt is about $1.6B (debt $1.98B, cash $0.35B) against 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance of $750-760M (about 2.1x), the GAAP tax rate guide was raised to 32%, and intangibles amortization of $296M a year hides that GAAP P/E is 32x. EV/EBIT is 25.6x, ROIC 2.0% (the parent paid $9.8B-plus of equity for these assets), and goodwill plus intangibles are $12.4B of $13.2B total assets. The stock has just jumped (3-year high within 0.5% as of 2026-11-18; one source says 16% on 2026-11-12) on an $800M buyback authorization, so the bargain, if any, was available a week ago. Takeaway: an unproven standalone company with falling GAAP profit, price-led growth, a leveraged balance sheet and a freshly re-rated stock.

## 2. Scariest fact
10-Q filed 2026-11-12: three-month operating profit $92M vs $104M and net income $42M vs $71M while revenue rose from $445M to $478M; Selling and general expenses $203M vs $135M. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2090312/000209031226000035/mbgl-20260930.htm . Plain words: the first full quarter as an independent company shows profit going down 41% on higher sales, meaning the margins we thought belonged to CARFAX depended on S&P's cheap shared services, and the price of being public plus $2.0B of debt will take away a large chunk of what the old FCF numbers promised. The 10-Q also says historical results are "not necessarily indicative of future operating results" because the company will "incur incremental stand-alone public company and corporate costs".

## 3. Pre-mortem
November 2029, stock down 50% to about $12. Consumers and dealers resisted another round of price increases; CARFAX subscription growth went to 1-3% as dealers used free or bundled history reports from rivals (AutoCheck/Experian, CarGurus, Cox/Autotrader listings with built-in history) and as AI search and OEM-direct data reduced the need to buy a report. Adjusted EBITDA stalled near $800M while GAAP net income stayed near $200M because amortization, 32% tax and $110M+ of interest ate the cash flow, so FCF was about $400M, not $460M. The $800M buyback was funded partly with debt, net leverage went to 3x, and a $2.0B refinancing of the 2029 notes at higher rates cost 100 bp. The company said "pricing", "stand-alone costs", "international planning softness" and "delayed deals".

## 4. Other wounds
- Debt: $2.0B senior notes, $650M due 2029, $650M due 2031, $700M due 2036, coupons 5.05%-6.05% (10-Q Note 4), about $105M a year of interest vs $11M for the first nine months of 2025 (card: interest cover n/a).
- Concentration in one product: CARFAX segment is 66% of 2026 nine-month revenue ($923M of $1,401M) and about 77% of segment Adjusted EBITDA ($446M of $593M before corporate).
- B2B margin: Adjusted EBITDA $147M on $477M nine-month revenue (31%), and Q3 down 13% year on year as international planning solutions weakened (WebSearch summary).
- Dependence on S&P transition services and a Tax Matters Agreement: the 10-Q lists a Transition Services Agreement, Tax Matters Agreement and Employee Matters Agreement; tax-free spin status restricts equity issuance and M&A for about two years. Not verified: exact restriction terms.
- Dilution and incentives: SBC $18M in nine months (and card shows 12 analysts and only 0.6% insider ownership). Insider ownership is thin, with management mostly holding spin-converted awards. The buyback of $350-450M by end of 2027 (WebSearch summary) mostly offsets conversion awards rather than shrinking the base.

## 5. Bulls vs. the facts
Bull: a toll-booth data asset with an iconic brand, around 40% Adjusted EBITDA margin, FCF conversion over 100%, subscription revenue 82% of the total ($1,144M of $1,401M), 2.1x net leverage and an $800M buyback, with forced spin-off selling by S&P holders now over. Answer: Adjusted EBITDA excludes $73M of transaction costs, SBC and $296M of amortization, and GAAP operating profit is down 11% in Q3; the 40% margin is a pre-standalone number. Growth is mostly price, so the volume engine is 2-3%. After a jump to near the 3-year high on day one of real results, the forced-selling discount is gone and the stock sits at 14x forward earnings only on the guidance that Q4 and 2027 stand-alone costs have been fully absorbed.

## 6. Verdict
WOUNDED: the asset is excellent, but there is no standalone track record, GAAP profit fell 41% in the first public quarter, growth is price-led and the price just ran; require another two quarters of clean standalone margins before sizing up.
