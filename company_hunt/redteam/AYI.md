# AYI (Acuity Inc., formerly Acuity Brands) red-team, run 2026-11-21

Sources: card.py (EDGAR XBRL + yfinance 2026-11-18); 10-K FY2026 (year to 2026-08-31) filed 2026-10-23 https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144215/000114421526000052/ayi-20260831.htm ; 8-K filed 2026-11-20 https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144215/000110465926130602/tm2630326d1_8k.htm

## 1. Strongest bear case
The FY2026 numbers that make AYI look cheap (13x EV/EBIT-ish, 13x free cash flow, $0.75B FCF, operating margin 15.4% vs 13.0%) are flattered by things that do not repeat. Operating profit rose $149.8M to $713.7M, but $51.3M of that year's operating cash flow and a matching profit lift came from IEEPA tariff refunds from U.S. Customs (the 10-K says ABL gross profit includes $38.2M of refunds), and the prior-year base was depressed by $29.6M of QSC inventory step-up and $29.7M special charges. Strip the refund and operating profit is about $662M (14.3% margin), and FCF about $0.70B before any normalisation. The core lighting business is shrinking: ABL net sales were $3,576M vs $3,612M (-1.0%) in FY2026 after $3,573M in 2024 and $3,723M in 2023, i.e. flat-to-down for four years; ABL's margin gain (16.4% to 17.4%) came from "lower net tariffs cost, including $38.2M in IEEPA refunds" and lower materials, not from growth. All the growth is bought: Intelligent Spaces went from $292M (FY2024) to $1,107M mostly through the $1.2B QSC acquisition (closed 2025-01-01), and on 2026-11-19 the company signed a $400M delayed-draw term loan to pay for another acquisition (Parameter Technologies), while debt had only just come down to $697M and cash is $0.64B. Goodwill is $1.5B. AIS operating margin is 16.9% in FY2026 vs 10.0% in FY2025 on a pro-forma basis that includes QSC's non-recurring step-up in the base; QSC is pro audio, which is a discretionary live-events and AV category, not building lighting. The stock is at $326.51, up 82% in three years and only 13% below its high, so the multiple discount to history is gone (P/E 18.3x vs 18.2x median) while the earnings base is inflated. Takeaway: you are paying a full price for a shrinking core, a one-time tariff refund and a serial-acquisition story.

## 2. Scariest fact
FY2026 10-K (filed 2026-10-23): "Cash flows from operations increased due primarily to higher profit, including $51.3 million of received tariff refunds" and ABL gross profit "increase ... was due primarily to lower net tariffs cost, including $38.2 million in IEEPA refunds". https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144215/000114421526000052/ayi-20260831.htm . Plain words: about 7% of the year's operating profit and 7% of its cash flow is a refund of past tariffs that will not recur. The entire margin story for the legacy business (+100 bp) is smaller than that refund. The risk factor in the same filing says "If any such tariffs are deemed unlawful, we may also have to seek refunds" and that tariff costs "could increase", so the same lever can swing the other way.

## 3. Pre-mortem
November 2029, stock down 50% to about $165. ABL sales kept sliding 1-3% a year as LED lighting became a commodity with price deflation and non-residential construction (offices, warehouses) stayed weak; ABL gross margin gave back the tariff and material tailwind (46.8% to about 43%). AIS missed: QSC audio and Distech controls grew 3% not 15% because of project delays, and Parameter plus QSC acquisition debt pushed net debt to about $1.0B. Operating margin fell from 15.4% to about 12%, EPS from $17.2 to about $13, a goodwill impairment on QSC ($1.2B price) hit, and the multiple dropped from 18x to 12x. The company said "lower net sales", "pricing" and "integration" in each quarter.

## 4. Other wounds
- Net cash has flipped: debt $697M vs cash $0.64B (net debt about $0.06B at Aug 2026), now with a new $400M delayed-draw term loan (8-K 2026-11-19) for Parameter Technologies; the card shows 5y acquisitions of $1.24B.
- Core ABL is flat for four years ($3.72B FY2023 to $3.58B FY2026); 10-K cites direct sales network and OEM channels down. Lighting is a mature, deflationary category with LED price erosion.
- Goodwill $1.5B, mostly from QSC; special charges (restructuring and impairments) of $23.7M and $29.7M in the last two years and the card shows $0.03B impairments in 3 years, so "special" is recurring.
- Tariff cost swing is large: refunds of $51M in FY2026 show the scale of cost moving through the P&L; a new tariff regime moves margin by a full percentage point.
- 5.02 8-Ks (officer change) on 2026-09-29; shareholder buybacks of $0.29B in FY2026 ran at about 40% of FCF and shares are down 14.8% in five years, which helps EPS but was bought at ever-higher prices ($326 now).

## 5. Bulls vs. the facts
Bull: Intelligent Spaces is the high-margin, recurring growth engine, ABL margins are structurally higher after productivity programs, FCF yield is 7.7%, and buybacks have shrunk the share count 15% in five years. Answer: the FCF yield is overstated by the one-time $51M refund and a working-capital release (OCF/NI 1.4x this year), AIS growth in the reported numbers is acquisition-driven (QSC), and with ABL declining the buyback only compensates for a shrinking base. The stock already trades at its historical average earnings multiple on peak-ish earnings.

## 6. Verdict
WOUNDED: not a broken business, but FY2026 earnings include about $51M of non-recurring tariff refunds, the core is shrinking and the story now depends on more acquisition debt; cut size and underwrite on about $660M operating profit, not $714M.
