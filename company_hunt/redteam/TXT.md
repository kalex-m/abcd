# TXT (Textron) red-team, written 2026-11-21

Sources: card.py TXT (price $82.79 on 2026-11-18, EDGAR XBRL), 10-Q for period 2026-10-03 filed 2026-10-29: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/217346/000021734626000044/txt-20261003.htm ; 10-K filed 2026-02-11 (period 2026-01-03).

## 1. The strongest bear case
Textron is a 1%-a-year grower dressed as a buyback story: revenue was $13.79B in 2016 and $14.80B in 2025 (10-year CAGR 1.0%), while diluted shares fell from 272M to 180M, so every dollar of EPS growth since 2016 is the share count, not the business. The latest quarter shows the engine is stalling where it counts. Nine-month 2026 segment profit is $1,028M vs $1,002M (+2.6%) on revenue up 6%, and the Q3 number is down: $355M vs $360M. Bell, the helicopter and tiltrotor unit, saw Q3 segment profit fall 17% ($92M to $76M) and nine-month profit fall 15% ($262M to $223M); margin 7.2% vs 8.7%; commercial helicopter deliveries 85 vs 91. The only Bell growth engine, the Army's MV-75 tiltrotor, is a fixed-price-leaning program that ran out of funding: "In mid-July 2026, Bell exhausted substantially all available fiscal 2026 MV-75 program funds and continued working on the program at its own risk", rescued by a reprogramming of about $300M of the $350M asked, and the company now expects an unfavorable cumulative catch-up charge of $110M-$150M when the low-rate production option is awarded in 2027, up from the $60M-$110M it told investors before. That is a 36-150% miss on the one program that supposedly justifies the defense multiple, and the award depends on a FY2027 budget that is running on a continuing resolution to 2026-12-11. Operating cash flow is $613M for nine months, flat, and free cash flow is mostly consumed by an inventory build of $563M (vs $437M a year ago) in nine months; pension income of $210M in the non-service line props pre-tax income (that is 23.5% of $893M pre-tax). Textron Aviation, the cash cow, is growing revenue 14% but profit only 4% in Q3 with margin down from 11.6% to 10.7%, blaming "manufacturing inefficiencies" and inflation of $137M year to date. At 15.2x trailing earnings (vs a 10-year median of 17.6x) and 5.6% FCF/EV on cash flow that excludes a pending $110-150M charge, the market is not mispricing anything; it is paying a fair price for flat business jets, a slipping helicopter franchise and a deal-making story. The takeaway: the thesis that Textron is a cheap, steady compounder fails on the segment numbers.

## 2. The single scariest fact
Quote: "In mid-July 2026, Bell exhausted substantially all available fiscal 2026 MV-75 program funds and continued working on the program at its own risk during the third quarter" and "If the LRIP option is awarded during 2027, we now expect to record an unfavorable cumulative catch-up program adjustment in the range of $110 million to $150 million ... Our previously disclosed range of $60 million to $110 million". (Note 12 and Segment Analysis, 10-Q period 2026-10-03, filed 2026-10-29, URL above.)
Why it matters: Bell is spending its own money on a program it cannot yet bill, the cost to build is higher than bid, the loss estimate has been raised once and the production price is largely fixed. If costs keep drifting, the next revision is bigger, and this is the program the company has "significantly increased" its facilities and people for.

## 3. Pre-mortem (November 2029, stock down 50% to about $41)
LRIP is awarded in 2027 at the fixed price, the catch-up charge lands at the top of the range or above, and in 2028 a second charge follows as the production lines ramp at a cost above the bid (the same thing that happened on fixed-price programs at Boeing and Lockheed). Bell segment margin falls from 7.2% to low single digits, and business jet demand softens: Textron Aviation margin goes from 10.6% to 8%. The Industrial segment sale ($2.4B of $11.3B nine-month revenue, 21%) is done at a low price or abandoned. Segment profit drops from about $1.4B to $1.0B, EPS from about $5.5 to about $3.8 and the multiple compresses from 15x to 11x. The company's wording: "unfavorable cumulative catch-up adjustment", "manufacturing inefficiencies", "contract performance".

## 4. Other wounds
- Government concentration: 27% of 2025 revenue from the U.S. Government (10-Q risk factors); the FY2027 appropriation is still not enacted and a shutdown "could" delay payments.
- Backlog is flat: $19,048M at 2026-10-03 vs $18,823M at 2026-01-03 (+1.2% in nine months); Bell backlog fell 3% ($7,795M to $7,562M) while revenue is the thing growing, so book-to-bill is below 1 at Bell.
- Net debt: card shows debt $4.21B vs cash $1.70B (includes the captive finance arm; I did not split Manufacturing group debt), with interest expense up 10% ($90M to $99M for nine months).
- The Industrial separation announced 2026-04-30 has "uncertainty as to the terms, structure and timing"; special charges of 3 restructuring 8-Ks in 3 years (Item 2.05 x3) show a repeat habit of "restructuring".
- Buyback dependence: shares down 16% in 3 years; at $14B market cap, a $0.8-1.2B a year buyback is the whole EPS story and is funded by free cash flow of $0.65-0.93B in 2024-25, i.e. at or above 100% of FCF.

## 5. What the bulls will say, and why it is not enough
Bulls: $19B backlog, Aviation recovery and pricing power, MV-75 is a multi-decade franchise, spin of Industrial unlocks a pure aerospace and defense multiple, forward P/E 11.7. Answer: the backlog is flat, MV-75 is currently a cost problem (a charge of up to $150M, about 1% of the $14B market cap, but a signal of cost drift on a fixed price), Industrial is the lowest-margin segment (Q3 profit $38M on $765M revenue, 5%) and its sale does not change the fact that the three remaining segments grew profit 0-4% in Q3. A forward P/E of 11.7 depends on 2027 earnings that sit after the charge.

## 6. Verdict
WOUNDED. Not dead: Aviation and Systems are healthy and the price is not extreme, but flat profit, a growing MV-75 loss and flat backlog mean this is not a mispriced compounder; cut the size.
