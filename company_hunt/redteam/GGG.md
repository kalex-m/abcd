# GGG (Graco) red-team, run 2026-11-21

Sources: card.py (EDGAR XBRL + yfinance 2026-11-18); 10-K FY2025 filed 2026-02-17 https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/42888/000004288826000081/ggg-20251226.htm ; 10-Q Q3 2026 filed 2026-10-21 https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/42888/000004288826000145/ggg-20260925.htm

## 1. Strongest bear case
Graco is a very good business priced as if it is still a compounder, but the growth is not there. Revenue was $1.65B in 2018 and $2.24B in 2025: 6.3% a year over five years but only 1.4% a year over three (2022 $2.14B to 2025 $2.24B). Over that stretch the organic engine stalled and the 2023 jump came from buying Industrial/Process businesses; Process segment sales collapsed from $495M (2022) to $168M (2025) and its operating income from $122M to $41M, so one of the three old segments has lost about two thirds of its profit. Operating margin of 27.9% in 2025 sits above the 10-year median of 26.6%, which means the easy margin gain is banked. The latest quarter shows the first crack: Q3 2026 (to 2026-09-25) sales rose 8% but only 4 points were organic, gross margin fell from 53.2% to 50.5% (cost of products sold 49.5% of sales vs 46.8%), and operating earnings fell 10% ($148.1M vs $164.7M; $150.7M last year after removing a $14M one-off gain, so still down about 2% on 8% more sales). Segment profit in Industrial fell $74.5M vs $80.7M on higher sales. Management answered by spending: $436M on acquisitions in nine months (Valco Melton, $462M cash, closed 2026-08-31, $220M of new goodwill on top of $585M) and $463M of buybacks in nine months, which drained cash from $624M at year end to $140M and required $219M of new revolver borrowing. The stock at $71.61 is 22x trailing earnings and 18x free cash flow ($0.64B, 5.6% yield). Those multiples are below the company's own 10-year medians (28x and 28x), which is what the bull sees as cheap; the bear sees a company growing revenue 1-4% organically, losing gross margin, and now paying up for acquired, lower-margin revenue, so the old multiple is not coming back. Takeaway: you are paying a fair price for a no-growth, margin-peaked business that has begun using the balance sheet to manufacture growth.

## 2. Scariest fact
Q3 2026 10-Q (filed 2026-10-21): "The gross profit margin rate decreased approximately 3 percentage points for the third quarter compared to the same period last year, primarily driven by higher product costs, the unfavorable effects of lower margin rates of acquired operations and unfavorable product and channel mix." Link: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/42888/000004288826000145/ggg-20260925.htm . Graco's whole case is a 52-53% gross margin that came from pricing power and mix. A 3-point drop in a single quarter, with only one month of the new acquisition included, means the damage is mostly organic (cost and mix), and Valco Melton's lower margin will weigh on all of Q4 and 2027. Each point of gross margin is about $22M of operating profit on $2.2B revenue.

## 3. Pre-mortem
November 2029, stock down 50% to about $36. Revenue stayed near $2.5B because organic growth ran 1-3% a year and acquisitions bought the rest. Gross margin slid from 52.5% (2025) to about 47-48% as tariff-driven product costs stuck, contractor mix shifted to home-center and lower-priced sprayers, and acquired businesses carried margins in the low 40s. Operating margin fell from 27.9% to about 22%, EPS went from $3.25 to about $2.60, and the multiple compressed from 22x to 14x as the market priced Graco as an ordinary industrial. The company said "mix", "channel" and "acquired operations" for eight quarters in a row. A goodwill charge on the 2020-vintage and 2026 acquisitions ($794M goodwill, up from $585M in nine months) arrived in the same period.

## 4. Other wounds
- Net cash has gone from $0.5B to roughly zero in nine months: cash $139.8M vs $624.1M at 2025 year end; revolver drew $219M in Q3 2026 and $119M was repaid (10-Q 2026-10-21). The buyback ($463M in nine months, vs $420M all of 2025) is now running above free cash flow after acquisitions.
- Contractor segment (about 48% of 2025 sales, $1,072M) depends on residential and non-residential construction; 10-K says construction markets are "continued softness". Contractor operating margin went from 27% (2021) to 25% (2025).
- Goodwill and intangibles total $1.23B (Sep 2026) vs equity of $2.47B; 10-K lists "potential goodwill impairment" as a risk. A $7.8M impairment already happened in 2023 on a 2020 acquisition.
- Tariff and China exposure: the company manufactures in China (all segments) and sources components from China; the Q3 10-Q notes the Feb 2026 Supreme Court IEEPA ruling and refunds, which flatter YTD margins, so some reported margin is one-off.
- Sells through third-party distributors; 10-K lists "dependence on a few large customers" and cyclical industries. Insiders own 0.8%; 10 Item 5.02 (officer change) 8-Ks in three years suggest heavy management turnover.

## 5. Bulls vs. the facts
Bull: 28% operating margins, 20%+ ROIC, an asset-light 5.6% FCF yield, multiples below their 10-year median, a drawdown of 23.6% from the 3-year high, and a buyback retiring 2% of shares a year. Answer: the multiple discount is explained by the fact that revenue growth over three years was 1.4% a year, and the most recent quarter shows margin starting to fall; EPS growth of 9.9% five-year was largely margin expansion and buyback, both of which are now exhausted (buyback financed with the revolver). At 18x FCF with 4% organic growth and falling margins, there is no margin of safety.

## 6. Verdict
WOUNDED: the quality is real, but a 3-point gross margin drop plus a cash-to-debt swing from the acquisition and buyback binge removes the margin of safety; size down until two quarters show gross margin stabilizing.
