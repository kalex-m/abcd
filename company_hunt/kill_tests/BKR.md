## Reading
Baker Hughes is now two businesses: an oilfield-services unit with flat orders and a gas-equipment unit (IET plus the newly bought Chart) that holds $43B of the $46.4B backlog and is growing orders about 40%. The Phase 3 claim that the market is pricing the oilfield label rather than the gas order book checks out on the backlog numbers, which I confirmed in the 10-Q. But the price drop is not a hidden fear: the company took on about $14B of net debt for Chart in July, and with 24 analysts and a record-order headline in every release, the order book is not unseen. The balance sheet gate fails on net debt of roughly $14B against a $47B market value, with cash flow flattered by customer prepayments, and the Form 4 and pay-design work was not completed within budget. Takeaway: a good gas-equipment franchise, fully looked at and now levered; rejected on gates 3 and 10.
