## Reading
Honeywell Aerospace is the avionics, systems and aftermarket parts business spun out of Honeywell on 2026-06-29: $17.4B of sales in 2025, an 18.7% operating margin, sole-sourced content on the Boeing 737 and Airbus A320 families and a $19.3B backlog up 12%. The Phase 3 case was a 20% post-spin drop on a supplier bottleneck that a growing business should outgrow, at a 6.4% FCF yield and net debt near zero. The 10-Q breaks two parts of that: net debt is $14.3B (the Phase 3 debt figure of $0.27B was the pre-spin balance sheet), which puts EV near $64B and the FCF yield on EV near 5%; and nine-month FCF was only $586M against $1.64B of net income, helped by $344M of receivables sold. It also fails on attention, because the supply-chain cause of the guidance cut is the most reported fact about the stock, a securities class action is pending, and 15 analysts follow it. The franchise is real and demand is not the problem. Takeaway: a good aerospace business with a bottleneck everyone can see and a levered, thinly proven balance sheet and cash record, not an overlooked bargain.
