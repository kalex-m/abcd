## Reading
Textron is a $14.8B conglomerate: Textron Aviation (business jets, about half of revenue), Bell (helicopters and the Army's MV-75 tiltrotor), Textron Systems (defense) and a small industrial unit. It is cheap-ish (about 12.5x 2026 adjusted EPS of $6.60 against a 17.6x median, 5-6% FCF yield) because revenue grows only 4-5% a year, manufacturing gross margin has slid from 20.5% to 17.8%, and the Army program ran out of funded money in July 2026 before Congress fixed it. Q3 was good: jets +14%, defense double-digit, guidance raised. The gates show a clean, well-run, honestly reported industrial: no accounting flags, cash conversion 1.56x, share count down 21% in five years, pay tied to ROIC and cash flow. What they do not show is a margin of safety: net debt about $2.5B after leases, a 27% government revenue share riding partly on one program, a worsening gross margin, and insiders who sold $90M while buying $1M. Nobody is arguing about it, but that is because there is little to argue about. Verdict: survives as an average-quality cyclical at a fair-to-modest discount, with Bell's contract and jet earnings through a downturn as the two open questions.
