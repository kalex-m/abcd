## Reading
Accenture is the world's largest IT services and consulting firm: $74.2B revenue, 15.4% operating margin, $11.6B free cash flow, 7% revenue growth in Q4 FY26 and $84.5B of bookings. It is cheap (16x earnings vs a 24x median, 41% below its three-year high) because investors fear AI will cut the hours it bills, and the filings do not yet show that: revenue per employee is flat, bookings run ahead of revenue, ROIC is 30% and the share count is falling 1% a year. The balance sheet is not a war chest: debt doubled to $10B in a year to fund $7.5B of buybacks and $4.7B of acquisitions, and after leases and pensions net cash is about -$1.8B, though cash flow makes that harmless. The accounting and pay design are clean apart from a business-optimization charge excluded every year, and the CEO bought $3M of stock in October. It fails on attention: the AI question is the most argued fact about the stock (24 analysts, an 18% one-day drop, a 22% bounce, public downgrades), so there is no overlooked fact, only a debate the market is actively pricing. Verdict: a high-quality, honestly reported franchise where the risk is the exact one everyone is watching; it fails gate 10.
