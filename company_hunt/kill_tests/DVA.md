## Reading
DaVita runs about 2,700 dialysis clinics as one of two U.S. providers, earning a steady 15% operating margin and $1.3B of free cash flow, and it has cut its share count 38% in five years with $863M of buybacks in nine months of 2026. It looks cheap at 9.4x earnings because volume is flat to down, the company carries $10.8B of debt and $14.3B of obligations once leases and partner puts are counted, and a 45% owner keeps selling back to it. Pay design and accounting are sound and dilution is the opposite of a problem. The business fails on structure: two thirds of revenue is set by Medicare, 11% of patients (commercial) carry the profit, and the GLP-1 and SGLT2 threat to new patient numbers is named in its own 10-K, while the lawsuits cannot be sized. It also fails on attention, since 16.3% short interest and a visible Berkshire stake mean the dependence on payors is the thoroughly argued bear case. Takeaway: a good cash machine priced for its known limits, rejected on gates 7 and 10.
