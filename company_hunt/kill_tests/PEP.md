## Reading
PepsiCo is a $94B snack and drinks company: Frito-Lay, Pepsi, Gatorade, Quaker and a fast-growing international arm. On GAAP trailing earnings it looks fairly priced (23x) only because 2025 carried $2B of brand impairments; on core earnings it trades near 17x with the stock 15% off its high and flat over five years. The causes are in the filings: North America beverage volume -3% held up by price, Foods North America flat, operating margin 12.2% vs a 14.2% median, ROIC down to 13.7%, and $41B of net debt with a dividend that takes all of free cash flow. The gates are mostly clean: no accounting flags beyond a habit of excluding charges, a director who bought $23.7M of stock, sensible pay metrics, and no dilution. But it fails on attention. Elliott put $4B into the stock in September 2025, got a price-cut and efficiency plan from management in December, and the whole North America volume and cost debate has been public for over a year, so the market is pricing a well-studied problem rather than missing a fact. Verdict: solid franchise, honest numbers, no information edge, and low balance-sheet room.
