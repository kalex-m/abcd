Bottom line up front: a good business, sensibly run, priced about right. The building-controls and audio unit (AIS) is not hidden. At 9x EBITDA for lighting, the market is already paying about 18x for AIS. Then, on 2026-10-26, Acuity agreed to pay $675M (about 16x forward EBITDA, 22x trailing on one trade outlet's reading) for Parameter Technologies. ## 1. The one-paragraph truth

Acuity sells two things. First, light fixtures and lighting controls: Acuity Brands Lighting (ABL), with $3,576M of FY2026 sales (fiscal year ended 2026-08-31). These go mostly to North American commercial and industrial buildings, through independent electrical sales agents and distributors, and are specified by architects and engineers. Second, the parts that make a building's systems talk to each other: Acuity Intelligent Spaces (AIS), with $1,107M of sales. AIS is Distech building-automation controllers plus QSC's Q-SYS audio, video and control platform, bought on 2025-01-01 for $1,240.7M cash (10-K FY2026, Acquisitions note, filed 2026-10-23, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144215/000114421526000052/ayi-20260831.htm). The money comes from ABL's scale and spec position. ABL turns flat sales into a 17.4% GAAP operating margin ($623.5M) by pushing price ("we announced a price increase in September, it goes live in December", CEO Neil Ashe, Q4 FY2026 call, 2026-10-01, https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/01/acuity-ayi-q4-2026-earnings-call-transcript/) and productivity. AIS earns a 61.3% gross margin and a 16.9% GAAP / 23.0% adjusted operating margin. The consolidated result: $4,641.8M of sales, $713.7M GAAP operating profit (including $51.3M of one-off IEEPA tariff refunds), $825.6M operating cash flow, and about $750M of free cash flow (10-K FY2026; 8-K EX-99.1 2026-10-01, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144215/000114421526000047/ayi-20261001x8xk_ex991.htm). That cash mostly goes into buybacks and acquisitions for AIS. In short, Acuity is a mature lighting cash machine paying for a faster controls business, and the controls business is now a quarter of sales.

## 2. Inside the company

**Leadership.** Neil Ashe has been CEO since January 2020 and Chairman since January 2021. Karen Holcom is CFO and Barry Goldman is General Counsel. On the Q4 FY2026 call (2026-10-01) the company announced Ruth Gratzke, ex-Siemens, as President of ABL. On 2026-09-24 the board grew from nine to ten seats to add Nick Tzitzon, Vice Chairman of ServiceNow and head of its AI Institute (8-K Item 5.02, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144215/000110465926111917/tm2626454d1_8k.htm). Both appointments point the same way: a software and controls story bolted onto a lighting company.

**Pay design** (DEF 14A filed 2025-12-11, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144215/000114421525000104/ayi-20251211.htm):
- Bonus: 80% on net sales, operating profit and free cash flow (each "as may be adjusted"), 20% individual.
- CEO long-term award: 75% PSUs (50% three-year adjusted ROIC above WACC, 25% relative TSR against the S&P 400 Capital Goods index) and 25% RSUs.
- CEO total FY2025 pay: $12.5M, a 640:1 ratio to the median employee (143:1 to the US median).

The design sounds owner-like, but the ROIC bar is soft. Target payout needs adjusted ROIC only 2 points above WACC, and maximum needs 6 points. In the 2022-2025 cycle, average adjusted ROIC was 18.3% against a 10.7% WACC, and the plan paid **200%**, plus a 162% rTSR payout. When the starting point is already 7-8 points above the hurdle, the plan pays maximum for standing still. It also gives no reason to avoid paying 16-22x EBITDA for deals, provided adjusted ROIC (which adds back acquisition amortisation) stays above about 16.7%.

**Strategy now vs three years ago.** In the FY2023 10-K (filed 2023-10-26) the word "audio" appears zero times and "data center" zero times. In the FY2026 10-K they appear 22 times and once, and a whole new risk section on "our strategic adoption of AI" has been added. "Tariff" went from 13 to 32 mentions. Capital allocation as Ashe stated it on 2026-10-01: "We grow our current businesses. We go through acquisitions…" He also said: "we also have a strong acquisition pipeline with multiple choices of companies… our priority is… growing Intelligent Spaces into a larger and larger portion of our company… We buy high-quality assets at fair prices." Three weeks later came Parameter Technologies, and on 2026-11-19 a new $400M delayed-draw term loan to fund "Project Sentinel", in which Acuity Intelligent Spaces US Inc. acquires MRC Springfield Intermediate Inc. (Amendment No. 1 to the Credit Agreement, 8-K 2026-11-20, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1144215/000110465926130602/tm2630326d1_ex10-1.htm). I take MRC Springfield to be Parameter's holding company: the timing and the AIS buyer match. I could not confirm the name link from a primary source. The organisation is turning into a serial acquirer for AIS, with lighting as the source of funds.

- The adjusted figures the company leads with ($828.7M adjusted operating profit, $19.90 adjusted EPS) exclude $49.5M SBC and $93.1M amortisation. SBC is a real cost. Amortisation is non-cash, but it stands for the price paid for QSC.

## 5. Ground(s) and evidence

**G4 Quiet Vault: passes on quality, fails on price.**
- Six analysts cover the stock and short interest is 3.8%.
- 10-year revenue CAGR is only 3.5%, but FCF per share compounded 19%/yr over five years. Driver: margin expansion from 12.7% (10y median operating margin) to 15.4%, plus a 14.8% share-count cut.
- Diluted shares went from 43M (FY2017) to 29.7M outstanding on 2026-10-20 (10-K cover).

## 8. The thesis in one sentence, and why the market is not seeing it

The would-be thesis is "a 25%-of-sales controls and audio business growing mid-teens at 23-25% adjusted margins is hidden inside a flat lighting company". The numbers show the market is not missing it. The market cap already pays about 18x EBITDA for AIS, and management keeps narrating AIS growth on every call and press release (AIS leads the Q4 release headline). There is no edge here that rests on something the market is not looking at, and this section concedes that plainly.

## 9. The industry picture

Commercial lighting in North America has become a replacement-and-renovation business. The LED conversion wave that lifted volumes in the 2010s is largely done. Fixtures are now electronics with falling unit prices, imported fixtures compete on cost, and tariffs move costs around the industry every year. Acuity's own FY2026 ABL sales fell 1.0%, and management expects the ABL market to be "flat to down low singles" in FY2027 (Q4 call, 2026-10-01). Acuity's claim to be winning share rests on two data points from the same call: the independent sales network grew 4% in Q4 after two flat quarters, and FY2026 was "a very strong year in data centers" for lighting. Neither is audited, and I treat both as management claims.

Growth in the industry has moved to the layer above the fixture: building-automation controllers, integrations with HVAC and power systems, and networked AV. Data centres are the hottest end market for all of it, which explains three moves:
- Distech's PLC (programmable logic controller) line, aimed at hyperscalers.
- The purpose-built data-centre luminaire (BLT).
- Parameter Technologies: RLE leak detection and CellWatch battery monitoring, with about $100M expected calendar-2027 sales growing 30%+ at about 40% EBITDA margins (WebSearch snippets of the 2026-10-26 release and Inside Lighting).

In that layer Acuity is a mid-sized player against much larger building-automation incumbents (Johnson Controls, Siemens, Honeywell, Schneider). In AV it competes with Crestron, Biamp and others for Q-SYS. I did not verify market shares, so I claim none.

Where Acuity sits: the scale leader in a flat fixture market, using that cash to buy its way into the faster layer. The winners in this industry will be whoever owns the controls software and the data-centre spec. Acuity is buying a seat at that table, at market prices.

## 14. What would make this thesis wrong

The thesis is "fairly priced quality". These facts would break it on the downside:
1. AIS like-for-like growth below 8% for two consecutive quarters (it ran 14.9% and 16.6% in Q3 and Q4 FY26).
2. ABL adjusted operating margin below 16.5% for a full year (FY26: 18.1%), which would mean price no longer offsets cost.
3. Another AIS acquisition above $500M at more than 20x trailing EBITDA, or net debt above 2x EBITDA.
4. Owner cash (FCF minus SBC) below $500M in FY2027 (FY2026 normalised: about $600M).
5. Goodwill impairment in AIS (goodwill $1,493.5M).

On the upside, a price at or below about $250 (bottom of the 9% / -2% to 0% grid) would make it a buy without changing anything else.

## 16. Confidence score: 6/10

I am fairly confident in the conclusion that there is no mispricing. The SOTP, reverse DCF and historical multiples all land within ±10% of the price.

The two biggest unknowns:
