## 1. The one-paragraph truth

Graco designs and builds, mostly in Minnesota, the pumps, spray guns, proportioners, lubrication systems and dispensing valves that move paint, foam, sealants, adhesives, lubricants and powder from a drum onto a surface or into a product, and sells them almost entirely through independent distributors, paint stores and home centers (Contractor segment) and to industrial OEMs and plants (Industrial and Expansion Markets). It makes money the way a high-end tool maker does: an installed base of professional equipment that wears out, a parts-and-accessories tail, prices raised every year, a manufacturing base concentrated in the US (US long-lived assets $605M of $770M at 2026-09-25, Q3-26 10-Q), and very little capital need, so a 52.5% gross margin and 27.9% operating margin on $2.24B of 2025 sales (FY25 10-K) turn into $0.5-0.65B of free cash a year. The weak spot is volume: organic growth has been roughly zero for three years (2022 sales $2.14B, 2025 $2.24B, with about $100M+ of the gap bought through acquisitions), and the company has answered by buying growth (Valco Melton for $462M cash on 2026-08-31) and buying back stock ($463M in nine months of 2026 plus a $600M accelerated repurchase signed 2026-11-10). It is a toll on professional painting and industrial fluid handling whose traffic has stalled, not a toll whose rate is under threat.

## 2. Inside the company

Leadership: Mark Sheahan has been CEO since 2021 (a Graco lifer; executive officers averaged about 22 years of tenure at 2025-12-26, FY25 10-K). The finance seat has turned over twice in two years: Christopher Knutson was CFO on the Q3 2025 call (2025-10-23), David Lowe was CFO and Treasurer in the 2026 proxy, and Sanjiv Gupta presented as CFO and Treasurer on the 2026-10-22 call, with Knutson back as Chief Accounting Officer. One director (Brett Carter) resigned 2026-02-13 and Steven Hedlund joined 2026-09-10 (8-Ks 2026-02-13 and 2026-06-15). Pay design (DEF 14A 2026): the cash bonus is 60% corporate net sales and 40% "Incentive EPS" (division heads add division sales and EPS), targets set using GDP growth as a guide; Sheahan earned a $1.26M bonus for 2025 at 118% of target on a $1.065M salary. Long-term pay is stock options only, sized at the 75th percentile of market data (Sheahan: 204,910 options in February 2025). There are no return-on-capital metrics and no PSUs. Net sales at 60% weight counts acquired revenue, and Incentive EPS excludes purchase accounting and transaction costs, so the bonus plan rewards buying revenue and is blind to the price paid. Strategy and language: three years ago the pitch was organic product launches and "leveraging products into new end markets"; the FY25 10-K and the 2026-01-26 release now list "pursuing strategic acquisitions" as a named key initiative next to the "One Graco" operating model (merging division sales forces and channels), the Q3 2026 call showed a slide with "over 100 names" in the active M&A pipeline aimed at semiconductor and water, and from Q2 2026 the company changed its non-GAAP method to exclude amortization of acquired intangibles and acquisition costs (Q3-26 10-Q, MD&A). Capital allocation has moved from "fortress cash" ($675M of cash at 2024 year-end, $624M at 2025 year-end) to deliberately levering up: on the Q3 call the CFO said "we are going to be using our balance sheet as appropriate" and "we will be buying stock for sure". Takeaway: a conservative engineering culture is being steered toward M&A and leverage by a pay plan that rewards size and adjusted EPS, which is not dangerous at Graco's scale but is the opposite of what a toll-road owner should be paid for.

## 5. Ground(s) and evidence

G3 Toll Road. The Phase 2 screen required a 10-year median operating margin of at least 22%, capex under 10% of revenue, a 5-year ROIC of at least 8% and stable margins. Graco qualifies on every count: 10-year median operating margin 26.6%; worst year since 2017 about 23.6% (2020); capex 2-9% of sales over ten years; 5-year average ROIC 30%; no negative free-cash-flow year (card). The toll: a painting contractor who owns a Graco airless sprayer buys Graco tips, filters, pumps and repair kits, and distributors stock the brand professionals ask for; in industrial fluid handling the equipment is a small share of the customer's line cost, so price matters less than uptime, and Graco has raised prices every year (price offset cost in Q3 2026, "price cost was flat", per the call). Evidence the toll is holding: Contractor margin held at 25-27% through two years of weak US housing; Industrial ex-Valco ran 33% in Q3 2026 (CFO, call). Evidence the traffic is not: Contractor sales were $999M in 2022 and $1,072M in 2025, only +7% in three years, with acquisitions inside that; the Industrial book-to-bill was "flattish, up about 1%" year-to-date (call). The Phase 3 "balance sheet" support is gone (section 4). The ground is the multiple: P/E 22.0 vs a 10-year median of 28.1 and P/FCF 18.0 vs 27.8 (card). Takeaway: the toll is real but the discount to its own history reflects a real growth reset (from 6-8% to 0-2% organic), so the old multiple is the wrong anchor.

## 6. What the market is pricing

Reverse DCF (`scripts/valuation.py GGG --cash 0.53e9 --r 0.09 --tg 0.025 --shares 163.0e6 --netcash 0.0 --price 71.61`, run 2026-11-21). Inputs: starting owner cash $0.53B (TTM OCF about $0.66B less normalised capex $0.10B less SBC $0.03B, section 3); 163.0M diluted shares (Q3 2026 weighted diluted, Q3-26 10-Q) with net cash about zero, i.e. the pre-ASR basis (the ASR swaps about $0.6B of debt for about 8M shares at about market price, which is roughly value-neutral at these prices); terminal growth 2.5%; 10-year horizon. Result: at a 9% discount rate the price implies owner cash growing 8.4% a year for five years and fading to 2.5% by year 10; at 8% it implies 5.4%; at 10%, 11.2%. Zero-growth value is $36 a share. My reading: for a business with 30% returns, low cyclicality and a 0.89 beta, an 8-9% hurdle is the right zone, so the market wants 5-8% annual owner-cash growth. Graco delivered about 9% a year from 2018 (about $0.29B) to 2025 (about $0.53B normalised), but that included a capex cycle, acquisitions and a buyback; organic sales growth since 2022 has been about 1% a year. Reaching 5-8% from here needs organic growth back at 3-4% plus price, plus acquisitions that earn their cost of capital (Valco Melton at roughly 3.2x sales and about 18x EBITDA at high-teens margins does not, until the promised mid-20s margin arrives), plus buybacks. Takeaway: the implied path is fair to slightly optimistic, not pessimistic; the market is still paying for a recovery in volume.

## 8. The thesis in one sentence, and why the market is not seeing it

Thesis: Graco is a durable toll on professional fluid handling whose de-rating from 28x to 22x earnings correctly reflects a three-year organic growth stall, and the buyer at $72 is paying a fair price for that toll, not getting it cheap. There is nothing the market is missing: the volume stall is in every 10-Q, the margin dilution from Valco Melton was disclosed in the 2026-10-21 10-Q and punished the next day (close $81.81 on 2026-10-21 to $69.28 on 2026-10-22, -15%), and the $600M ASR was publicised. The only thing I can see that the market may be underweighting is the improving order book (organic orders +7% in Q3, organic backlog +18% since year-end, Expansion Markets bookings +62%, per the call), but that is a timing call on the cycle, not a mispricing. Takeaway: no hidden insight; reject as a mispricing.

## 9. The industry picture

Graco's peers in fluid handling are Nordson (adhesives, dispensing; Valco Melton is a direct challenger to Nordson's adhesive business), IDEX, Wagner (private, German, Graco's main sprayer rival), Lincoln (SKF, now Timken) in lubrication, and Gema/Wagner in powder. The industry is consolidating around high-margin, distributor-led franchises that buy small technology firms; Graco has been a late convert to that playbook. The demand drivers are US repaint and remodel activity (pinned by low existing-home turnover since 2023), non-residential and infrastructure construction (healthy: protective coatings, spray foam and pavement products grew in 2025-2026), general industrial capex (weak: "customers continue to take a measured approach to certain larger capital projects", Q3 call), and newer pockets such as semiconductor fluid handling (best quarter "in several years") and electric motors via ETM (the ABB licence for data-centre cooling motors, first installations targeted for end-2029). Winners: those with installed bases and pricing power that can sit through a volume trough; Graco is one. Losers: undifferentiated pump makers and low-end sprayer brands squeezed by tariffs and Asian imports. Graco sits at the top on margins (28% vs Nordson's mid-20s and IDEX's low 20s in my memory of their filings; I did not re-verify peers for this dossier) but at the bottom on growth. Takeaway: a premium franchise in a mature, cyclical niche whose growth now depends on what it buys.

## 13. Catalysts and timeline

- Late January 2027: Q4 2026 results and 2027 guide. The Q4 range ($610-640M) implies flat organic growth; a 2027 guide of low-single-digit organic with Valco Melton adding about 6 points would confirm the stall is ending. Organic backlog +18% since year-end gives some support.
- March 2027: final settlement of the $600M ASR; share count should be about 152M (from 160.4M at 2026-09-25), lifting 2027 EPS by about 5% mechanically, offset by about $25-35M of new interest cost.
- 2027-2029: Valco Melton margin from high teens to mid-20s EBITDA (management target, Q3 call); ETM/ABB motor launches (conveyors 2027, data-centre cooling end-2029).
- A US housing turnover recovery (existing-home sales) would lift the Contractor pro-paint and home-center channels, the segment with the most operating leverage.
Slow-compounding case with no catalyst: organic 2%, price 2%, buyback 2-3% and a 1.7% dividend gives a 7-9% annual total return at a constant 22x multiple, roughly the cost of equity. Takeaway: catalysts exist but would merely justify today's price; the no-catalyst case is a market-like return.

## 14. What would make this thesis wrong

I am not long, so "wrong" means either the stock is genuinely cheaper than I think or worse than I think.
- Upside error (would justify revisiting): two consecutive quarters of organic growth of 5% or more with gross margin back above 53%, and Industrial ex-acquisitions margin at 33% or more; or a price below $50 (about 16x 2025 diluted EPS of $3.08, FY25 10-K) with the franchise unchanged, which would put the stock below my bear-to-base range.
- Downside triggers (would rule it out even at lower prices): consolidated gross margin below 50% for two quarters (Q3 2026 was 50.5%); Contractor operating margin below 23%; Valco Melton EBITDA margin still below 20% at the end of 2027; another acquisition above $300M paid mainly with revolver debt before the ASR is digested (net debt above 1.5x EBITDA); any goodwill impairment on Valco Melton or the 2024-2025 acquisitions; a home-center customer disclosed above 10% of sales with a channel loss.
