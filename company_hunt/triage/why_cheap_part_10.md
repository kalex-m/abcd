# Phase 3, batch 10 (second pass: spin-offs and low-attention names): why is it cheap? (checked 2026-11-21)

## MBGL (Mobility Global, Inc.): BORING, advance Y

Mobility Global is the CARFAX and Polk data business S&P Global spun out on 2026-07-01. Q3 revenue grew 7.4% to $478M at a 41% adjusted EBITDA margin and $186M of free cash flow, and the company loaded about $2B of debt onto itself to pay S&P a dividend, leaving net debt near 2x EBITDA. At about 11x EBITDA and 14x forward earnings it sits below Equifax (12.8x, 18.7x), Verisk (19.5x, 23.2x) and S&P Global (17.1x), which is a real but not dramatic discount, and with the stock up 14% since the spin there is no panic to buy into. The forced-selling story is over (S&P holders got one share per share and the first quarter reported fine), a senior officer bought $0.98M in August, and the $800M buyback is about 11% of the market cap. The open question is whether AI-built vehicle data erodes CARFAX's moat, and nothing in Q3 shows it. Verdict: a decent, modestly discounted compounder worth a closer look on the AI question, advance.

## HONA (Honeywell Aerospace Inc.): HURT, advance Y

Honeywell Aerospace trades at 18x forward earnings and 15x EBIT against 20-33x for GE, Howmet, RTX and TransDigm, 41% below its high, because its first standalone quarter came with a guidance cut. The cause is specific: a few precision casting and forging suppliers cannot ship enough, so scarce parts go to new-aircraft lines and the higher-margin aftermarket loses sales. Demand shows no damage: backlog is $19.3B (+12%), orders +9%, and Q3 earnings and second-half cash guidance were raised after the cut. A director put $1M in on the day of the cut, and the spin dynamics (index and Honeywell holders dumping, a class action) explain selling that is not about the business. What would make it wrong is a supply problem that lasts years or margin damage from the mix shift; management says 2027 should improve and I could not verify supplier capacity independently. Verdict: hurt, not broken, advance.

## AMRZ (Amrize Ltd): HURT, advance Y

Amrize is the Holcim North America business spun out in 2025: cement, aggregates and concrete (Building Materials) plus commercial and residential roofing (Building Envelope). The price reflects a flat year: Q3 revenue +2.2%, EBITDA +0.2%, and guidance trimmed to $3.0B EBITDA because oil-driven fuel and freight costs and high rates are squeezing roofing margins (down 410bp) and slowing volumes. The core cement and aggregates business is doing its job, with pricing up 1.8% and 6.9% and expected to be price-cost positive in Q4. At 9x EBITDA and 16x forward earnings it trades level with CRH and Eagle Materials, and a third below Martin Marietta and Vulcan at 21-28x, though those two are pure aggregates and Amrize is cement and roofing-heavy, so the gap is partly deserved. The strongest signal is the CEO buying $7.1M plus eight other insiders, and the buyback retired 4%. Debt is moderate at 1.8x. Verdict: hurt by a cost and rate squeeze, not a broken franchise; the discount to CRH is small, so the case rests on margin recovery, advance.

## FDXF (FedEx Freight Holding Company, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

FedEx Freight is the largest US less-than-truckload carrier, spun from FedEx in June 2026, and its stock is 31% below its high because freight volumes are soft and the first standalone numbers show costs rising: shipments -1.4%, tonnage flat, and adjusted EPS down 25% in the four-month transition period. Revenue growth of 9% is mostly fuel surcharge. Compared with its peers there is no discount: forward earnings of 25x match Old Dominion (24.9x) and XPO (25.6x), and annualised adjusted EBIT gives about 15x EV, with $3.8B of net debt and negative free cash flow last fiscal year. The market is already paying for a freight recovery, no insider has bought, and the spin selling has not produced a bargain price. Verdict: priced in at trough earnings, reject.

## SW (Smurfit Westrock Plc): HURT, advance Y

Smurfit Westrock, the 2024 merger of Smurfit Kappa and WestRock, is the world's largest corrugated packaging company, and Q3 EBITDA fell 7% to $1.2B because it is absorbing about $700M of unplanned freight and energy cost in 2026 while converting volumes came in weaker than planned. Guidance dropped to $4.7B. The business itself is selling everything it makes in North American containerboard, and price increases announced across paper grades are meant to cover the cost, with the full benefit in 2027. At 7.2x EBITDA it trades about 40% below Packaging Corp (11.8x) and below Amcor (9.9x), in line with Graphic Packaging; part of that gap is real, since capital spending eats most of cash flow ($2.2B of $3.4B), returns are low at 4% and net debt is 2.7x EBITDA. Insiders have been sellers, not buyers. Verdict: a cost-inflation hurt with a price-recovery path, advance for a closer look at whether the 2027 pricing sticks.

## SOLV (Solventum Corporation): DATA_ARTIFACT, advance N

Solventum, the 3M health care spin from April 2024, looks like 9x earnings only because 2025 GAAP profit included a $1.3B-plus gain on selling its filtration business. On the numbers management stands behind, adjusted EPS of about $7.25 gives 12x, which is the same multiple as Zimmer Biomet (11.3x) and below Becton Dickinson (15x). Organic growth is only 2.5-3%, Q3 organic sales fell 2.8% after an ERP-driven order pull-forward, and free cash flow is close to zero until separation costs end ($200-300M guided on $8B of sales, 1.5% yield). The stock is 6% off its high with the spin selling long finished. There is no mispricing to find at this level. Verdict: the screen picked it up on a one-time gain; real numbers do not qualify, reject.

## HLN (Haleon plc): BORING, advance Y

Haleon (Advil, Sensodyne, Voltaren, Centrum), the GSK consumer-health spin from 2022, fell 15% in a day after Q3 organic growth of 2.6% missed and the full-year growth guide dropped to about 2.5%. The causes named are a weak cold and flu season hitting North American orders and a China raw-material classification change on Centrum, which sound temporary, but this is the latest in several years where growth fell short of the 4-6% target, so the risk is that 2-3% is the real rate and mostly price (2.1% of the 2.6%). What keeps it interesting is the profit line: adjusted operating profit is still guided up high single digits, free cash flow is $2.2B (7.3% yield), margin is 22%, and a buyback started days after the miss. At 15x forward earnings it sits well below Procter & Gamble (22.8x) and Church & Dwight (27x) and level with Kenvue; only five analysts cover the ADR. Verdict: a quiet, cash-rich staples brand portfolio with a discount that rests on whether volume growth returns; advance.

## BIRK (Birkenstock Holding plc): HURT, advance Y

Birkenstock is 43% below its high with 12.9% of shares sold short, and the reasons are tariffs (the US duty rate doubled to 20%, costing about 100bp of margin), a stalling growth rate that led management to leave the sales outlook alone in May, and a $1.1B block sale by L Catterton's director, which is a sponsor leaving and a direct overhang. The business metrics are still strong: guidance around 15% constant-currency growth, EBITDA of at least EUR 710M, a 26% operating margin and 59% gross margin that few footwear names can match, with net debt about 1x EBITDA. At 13x forward earnings and roughly 8.5x EBITDA it is priced like mature footwear (Deckers 9.5x, Crocs 9.7x) rather than like a brand with 15% growth, though On trades at 14.5x. I could not read the filing text, so the guidance detail is from news reports. The December 10 full-year report is the test. Verdict: hurt by tariffs and a sponsor overhang, advance.
