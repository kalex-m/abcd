## BKR (Baker Hughes Company): UNSEEN, advance Y

Baker Hughes trades at about 17-18x earnings because the market still prices it as a cyclical oilfield-services company with a freshly leveraged balance sheet. Underneath, Q3 2026 orders rose 31% to $10.8B, and the Industrial & Energy Technology backlog hit a record $39.4B (turbines and compressors for LNG, pipelines and data-center power, with book-to-bill of 1.7), while operating margin has moved from a 4.5% ten-year median to about 10%. GAAP profit fell 33% on Chart deal costs even as adjusted EBITDA rose 14%, and debt tripled to $17.5B, which is why the stock sits 31% under its high. The leverage is the real risk (2.5x, free-cash-flow conversion cut to 40-45%), but a company with this backlog at this multiple, next to GE Vernova at 137x operating profit, is a structure the reported GAAP numbers do not show. Verdict: unseen, advance, with Chart integration and debt paydown as the first things to check.

## HUBB (Hubbell Incorporated): PRICED_IN, advance N

Hubbell sells connectors, arresters and substation gear to hundreds of utilities plus data-center electrical products, and it genuinely captures the shift: margin 20.7% against a 14.1% median, Q3 revenue +25%. But that is exactly what the price shows. The stock trades at 25x earnings against a 22.5x median, 25.8x free cash flow against 18.3x, with margins well above their own history, so the multiple is already paying for margin that would be hard to improve. No backlog disclosure means visibility rests on management's words. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## EME (EMCOR Group, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

EMCOR is a very good business: net cash, 36% return on invested capital, 18.5% fewer shares in five years, and a backlog up 47% across data centers, fabs, hospitals and water. The problem is the price. At 22x trailing earnings with margins at twice their median, the market is already assuming the labor-scarcity margin holds; at its median margin the same earnings would imply a multiple near 44x. A fall of 31% from the high has not changed that arithmetic. Verdict: priced in, reject; the bet here is margin durability, not mispricing.

## HII (Huntington Ingalls Industries, Inc.): HURT, advance Y

HII is the only builder of US aircraft carriers and a co-builder of nuclear submarines, and its backlog jumped to $86.2B this year, locking in roughly 90% of expected shipbuilding revenue through 2030. The stock is 36% below its high anyway because the yards have been slow: throughput up 9% against a 15% target, labor ramp problems at Ingalls, and ten-year-low margins (5.3% in FY2025 vs 7.5% median). Q3 shows the turn (margin 7.0% vs 5.0%, free cash flow $429M vs $16M), and on free cash flow the stock is at 14.3x against a 16.2x median. The trailing P/E of 18.8 looks high only because it is measured on trough margins. The government sets pricing, so the upside is capped by the 6-6.5% guided margin, and one more carrier setback would break the case. Verdict: hurt, not broken; advance.

## GD (General Dynamics Corporation): PRICED_IN, advance N

General Dynamics won a historic $84B of submarine work in one quarter, and Gulfstream adds a commercial leg with a 1.4x book-to-bill. But the government sets its margin (10.2% against a 10.9% median), so earnings grow with volume only, and the stock already trades at 22x earnings and 24x free cash flow against medians of 17x and 18x. It is also within 11% of its high, so there is no bruise to price. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## DY (Dycom Industries, Inc.): HURT, advance Y

Dycom builds fiber, long-haul and data-center connectivity for carriers and hyperscalers, and its backlog is a record $12.24B, up 53%. The stock is down 58% from its high because Q3 guidance came in under expectations: wireless work deferred a year, Communications margin down 134 bps to 13.6%, and fuel costs. Those are timing and cost problems, not lost demand, and management raised the full-year outlook the same day. On roughly $19 of annualised adjusted earnings the stock is at about 11x, though GAAP earnings (3.81 in Q2) show a 24x trailing multiple, and part of the 53% backlog growth is acquired (organic revenue +16.7%). The risks are real: AT&T, Verizon and Lumen are about a third of revenue and set the price, and $2.5B of net debt. Verdict: hurt, advance, with the Q3 margin print as the test.

## TT (Trane Technologies plc): PRICED_IN, advance N

Trane makes the chillers that cool data centers and commercial buildings, and its backlog of $12.5B is up 73%. But the stock is at 31x earnings and 32x free cash flow against medians of 24x and 26x, with margins that have stopped expanding (19.9% vs 20.3% in Q3). A 3.1% free-cash-flow yield and 23 analysts say the story is known. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## ETN (Eaton Corporation plc): PRICED_IN, advance N

Eaton has the biggest backlog in the group ($26.2B) and real pricing: Americas orders +39% organically, margin about 19% vs 16-17% history. It is also the name every investor looking for power-and-data-center exposure already owns (27 analysts, 2.7% free-cash-flow yield). At 32x earnings and 37x free cash flow against medians of 27x and 24x, the shift is in the price. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## LNG (Cheniere Energy, Inc.): DATA_ARTIFACT, advance N

Cheniere looks like the cheapest name in the group at 8.9x earnings, but that comes from FY2025 net income of $6.79B that includes non-cash derivative gains; nine-month 2026 net income is just $0.74B. On forward earnings it trades at 12.9x against a 12.3x median, and on free cash flow at 24.6x against 15.3x. The business itself is strong: fixed-fee contracts, guidance raised to $8.75-9.25B EBITDA, a 12% dividend increase, about 10% distributable-cash-flow yield on the market cap. But the glut concern is the most widely written risk in the sector, the stock is up 41% in a year, and the marketing margin is already shrinking (31% vs 41%). No hidden gap between price and structure. Verdict: data artifact on the cheap P/E, fairly priced underneath; reject.

## NOC (Northrop Grumman Corporation): PRICED_IN, advance N

Northrop is the least crowded of the big primes and its backlog is a record $109.6B, but it earns no extra margin from the shift (10.8% vs 11.4% median) and carries fixed-price risk on the B-21 bomber. At 18.5x earnings versus a 16.3x median, 19.6x operating profit versus 16x, there is no discount here for a bruise; free cash flow at 23x is slightly below its median, which is the only argument for cheapness. Verdict: priced in at a fair multiple; reject.

## RTX (RTX Corporation): PRICED_IN, advance N

RTX has the best pricing position among the primes (engine aftermarket, scarce missiles and air defense) and a $340B backlog, but the stock is up 163% in three years and trades at 40x earnings against a 27x median with a 3.0% free-cash-flow yield. Nothing is hidden. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## KMI (Kinder Morgan, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Kinder Morgan's gas network feeds LNG terminals and new power plants and its gas backlog is a record $13.2B, but a regulated pipeline earns a stable margin (27.9%), so the shift shows up as volume at a toll price. The market already pays for it: 3-year return +112%, P/E 22.9 vs 17.4, free cash flow 24x vs an 11.6x median, with $33B of debt. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## CMI (Cummins Inc.): HURT, advance Y

Cummins sells backup and prime-power generators to data centers alongside truck engines, and its Q3 2026 was a record: revenue +13%, EBITDA 18.9% of sales, EPS $8.25 vs $3.86. The stock is 41% under its high because revenue missed (supply limits in Power Systems, weak pickup-truck engines), because the AI-infrastructure trade was sold hard, and because investors suspect record margins are the peak of a diesel cycle. At about 12.7x forward earnings it trades below its 13.9x ten-year median even though the generator order book runs into 2028, so the market is pricing a cyclical peak; it is only half right, since heavy-duty truck sales are rising too (+49% in Q3). The caveats: the trailing P/E of 19.8 reflects 2025 charges, part of Q3 EPS was a tax gain, and trucks will cycle down. Verdict: hurt, not broken; advance, with the sustainability of the 18-19% EBITDA margin as the question.
