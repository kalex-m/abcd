# Phase 3, batch 3: why is it cheap? (checked 2026-11-19)

## PYPL (PayPal Holdings, Inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

PayPal trades at 9x earnings against a 41x ten-year median and throws off an 11.6% free-cash-flow yield, which looks like a gift until you split the revenue line. Total payment volume grew 6% in Q3 2026 but revenue grew only 2% and transaction margin dollars 2%, because the growth is coming from Braintree (unbranded card processing for big merchants, thin margins) and Venmo, while the branded PayPal button, the business that earns the money, lost about $240M of revenue year on year and needed bigger merchant incentives just to hold volume (10-Q filed 2026-10-27). The board fired Alex Chriss in February 2026, installed its own chair Enrique Lores, and abandoned the 2027 targets; shareholders are now suing specifically over the branded-checkout story. Buybacks (16% of shares retired in three years) keep per-share earnings rising, but that is a melting asset being shrunk into, not a cycle. The market is pricing a slowly eroding checkout franchise losing to Apple Pay and Shopify, and the filings agree with it. Verdict: down for a reason, reject.

## MA (Mastercard Incorporated): PRICED_IN, advance N

Mastercard is a toll road priced exactly like one. Q3 2026 revenue rose 14% to $9.8B, operating margin was 59.8%, EPS rose 22%, and every volume driver (gross dollar volume +7%, cross-border +11%, transactions +9%) is healthy (8-K filed 2026-10-29). The stock sits 4% below its high after a 62% three-year run and trades at 37x trailing earnings against a 36x ten-year median, with a 3.1% free-cash-flow yield. There is no hidden problem for the market to be overreacting to and no discount to exploit; the stablecoin and agentic-commerce worries that hang over payments have not touched the reported numbers, and the price shows nobody is fearing them. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.

## V (Visa Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Visa's fiscal 2026 was its best non-recovery year in a decade and a half: revenue up 15% to $45.9B, GAAP EPS up 21% to $12.32, payments volume up 9%, cross-border up 13% (8-K filed 2026-10-27). The stock is up 70% in three years and sits within 5% of its high at 33x trailing earnings and 35x free cash flow, a 2.8% cash yield. The screen could not compute Visa's own ten-year median multiple because Visa reports earnings per share by share class, but nothing in the price suggests a discount: there is no bruise, the litigation provisions ($469M in Q4) are a long-running cost of doing business, and the business is accelerating. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.

## COIN (Coinbase Global, Inc.): HURT, advance N

Coinbase is down 74% from its three-year high because crypto is in a bear market, not because the company broke: since the October 2025 bitcoin peak, market-wide trading volume and volatility have collapsed, and Coinbase's quarterly revenue went from $1.87B in Q3 2025 to $1.22B in Q3 2026 (-35%), with GAAP losses in Q1 and Q2 2026 and a Q3 profit of $719M that leaned on a September rebound in the crypto it holds at fair value (10-Qs, XBRL). The franchise itself is holding: its share of global crypto volume rose for a fourth straight quarter to 10.7%, USDC balances in its products hit $22B, and subscription and services revenue is now 49% of the total (Q3 deck, 2026-10-29). So the label is a cycle, but the screen's cheapness is an echo of 2025 boom earnings: on current numbers the stock trades at about 45x forward earnings and roughly 20x annualized adjusted EBITDA, with the share count up 29% in three years. Buying here is a bet on the next crypto upswing, not a mispriced business. Verdict: hurt by the cycle, but not cheap on trough earnings; do not advance.

## SPGI (S&P Global Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

S&P Global is the ratings and index duopoly plus a desktop-data business, and it trades at 29x earnings against a 30x ten-year median, so the screen found a toll road, not a bargain. Q3 2026 revenue grew 7% to $3.68B with a 53.5% adjusted margin, full-year organic growth is guided at 7.5-8.5%, and more than $7B of stock is being bought back this year (8-K filed 2026-10-27). The 15% slide from the August 2025 high is the market's AI worry about seat-based financial data, and management just conceded part of it by putting Capital IQ and associated data businesses under strategic review for a possible separation, keeping the ratings feeds it controls. The Mobility spin-off on 1 July 2026 also muddies the screen's per-share history. None of this creates a discount: the core tolls are growing and priced as such. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.

## MSCI (MSCI Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

MSCI is the cleanest toll road in this batch and the price knows it. Q3 2026 revenue rose 12.4% (12.3% organic), fees tied to assets benchmarked to its indexes rose 24.9% on rising markets and ETF inflows, subscription retention held at 95.0%, and the operating margin was 56.3% (8-K filed 2026-10-20). The stock is 5% below its three-year high and trades at 38x trailing earnings against a 37x ten-year median and a 3.2% free-cash-flow yield. The only risk worth naming is that a third of the growth is market-level beta (asset-based fees) that reverses in a drawdown, which is exactly why a 38x multiple is not a bargain. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.

## FDS (FactSet Research Systems Inc.): HURT, advance Y

FactSet is down 34% from its three-year high and trades at 21x earnings and 16x free cash flow against 30x and 26x for its own decade, because the market decided AI agents will shrink the number of analyst terminals and because margins stepped down. The filings do not show the substitution yet: in the year to August 2026 organic subscription value grew 7.0% (faster than the prior year), users rose 9.8% to 260,671, clients rose 2.9%, retention stayed above 95% of value, and free cash flow rose 14.6% to $708M (8-K 2026-09-30, 10-K 2026-10-20). What did get worse is cost: adjusted operating margin fell from 36.3% to 34.5% on AI spending, incentive pay and restructuring, and FY2027 guidance slows organic growth to 5.0-6.5%. The honest warning sign is mix: the new users are cheaper wealth-management seats, so value per user slipped about 3%, and the competitor S&P is shopping its own Capital IQ desktop, which says the incumbents do not love this category. Still, a data vendor whose seat count is rising 10% is not yet being replaced. Verdict: hurt, not broken, on current evidence; advance, with Phase 4 testing price per user and the source of the growth.

## ZTS (Zoetis Inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

Zoetis fell 55% from its high to 13x earnings against a 34x ten-year median because the pet-medicine franchise that justified the old multiple is being competed away. In Q3 2026 U.S. companion-animal revenue fell 8%; the company itself blames competitive pressure in dermatology (Apoquel and Cytopoint face Elanco's Zenrelia, reportedly about 20% cheaper), competition against Simparica Trio, generics on Convenia and Cerenia, lower Librela sales after safety concerns, and price-sensitive pet owners making fewer vet visits (8-K 2026-11-05, 10-Q). Full-year revenue is now guided to shrink 1-2% organically after two guidance cuts, and shareholders are suing over alleged concealment of falling demand for exactly those four products. Livestock (+5% to +9%) and a steady stream of approvals keep the company solid, but those new products are small next to the blockbusters losing exclusivity and pricing power. A 13x multiple on flat-to-falling earnings is fair, not a mistake. Verdict: down for a reason, reject; revisit only if U.S. companion-animal sales stop falling.

## DOCS (Doximity, Inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

Doximity is the Zoom test in healthcare form. It owns the physician audience (85%+ of U.S. doctors) and sells access to it to drug makers, but an AI clinical-search app, OpenEvidence, is now competing for the same doctors' attention and the same pharma budgets at lower prices. The filings show the effect: revenue growth fell to 4% in the September 2026 quarter, net revenue retention dropped from 118% to 103% in a year, adjusted EBITDA fell 12% as margins slid from 60% to 51% on AI spending, and the full-year guide implies only 6-7% growth (8-K 2026-11-05, 10-Q). The stock lost 24% on the May 2026 guidance and is 63% off its high, yet still trades at 28x earnings and a 3.6% free-cash-flow yield after stock pay, so it is not even cheap for a business whose moat (being the only place doctors go) has visibly been breached. The $713M of cash and record engagement numbers do not change the direction of the revenue line. Verdict: down for a reason, reject.

## VEEV (Veeva Systems Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Veeva entered the toll-road list because its multiple fell below its own decade (49x trailing earnings against a 69x median), but that decade was priced for 25% growth, and the business today grows 16-18%. Q2 FY2027 revenue rose 18% to $928M, subscriptions 16%, GAAP operating income 40%, and guidance went up (8-K 2026-08-26); the November investor-day update repeated that Q3 will meet or beat. The AI worry for pharma CRM (fewer sales reps, fewer seats) is not visible: 12 of the top 20 drug makers have committed to Vault CRM and subscription growth is steady. At about 30x forward non-GAAP earnings, a 3.1% free-cash-flow yield and 2.0% after stock pay, with $7.1B of net cash, the price is a fair one for a high-quality mid-teens grower, not a mispricing. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.
