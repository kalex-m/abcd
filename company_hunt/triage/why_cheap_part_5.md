# Phase 3, batch 5: why is it cheap? (checked 2026-11-21)

## BABA (Alibaba Group Holding Limited): HURT, advance N

The screen showed Alibaba at 14x earnings and a 5% free-cash-flow yield, but both rest on fiscal 2026 (year to March) numbers that no longer describe the business. The results the company filed on 2026-11-19 show a profit recovery (operating income RMB29.1B, up 443%, as the quick-commerce subsidy losses shrank and cloud margin reached 14.5%) alongside cash burn: free cash flow was minus RMB34.5B for the quarter and minus RMB79.1B for six months, because capex hit RMB50.5B in a single quarter (+60%) for AI data centres. Meanwhile the core Taobao advertising-and-commission line fell 5% (flat on a like-for-like basis), so the base business is not growing; the improvement comes from losing less money on food delivery. The fall is a hurt-by-an-investment-cycle story, not a broken franchise, and cloud demand is real. But a company burning about $12B of cash in half a year, whose payback depends on AI capex returns I cannot verify from filings, gives no margin of safety. Verdict: hurt, not broken, but no verifiable cheapness while free cash flow is deeply negative; do not advance.

## MELI (MercadoLibre, Inc.): HURT, advance Y

MercadoLibre is down 46% from its high because profit has stopped following revenue. Q3 2026 revenue rose 45% to $10.75B and payment volume 53%, yet operating income fell 6% to $676M and the margin slid to 6.3% from about 10% a year before (8-K filed 2026-11-04). The cause is a choice, not a collapse: Brazil's free-shipping threshold was lowered, new fulfilment capacity opened before peak season, and affiliate marketing was raised, which cut net shipping margin by about 200bps of GMV since early 2025 even as cost per shipment fell 5%; credit is growing 62% to $18B with a 20.1% net margin after losses. The screen's 14.9% free-cash-flow yield is wrong, because operating cash flow includes customer money and loan funding (adjusted FCF was $285M in Q3), so the honest multiple is 36x trailing earnings, about 27x forward, against 81x history. Credit quality on a book growing 62% is the thing to test next, along with whether the margin comes back as management promises for Q4. Verdict: hurt by deliberate reinvestment; advance, with Phase 4 testing delinquency and the margin path.

## CHH (Choice Hotels International, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Choice looked cheap at 15x trailing earnings against a 23x decade median, but that trailing figure is the strong 2025 year. The Q3 2026 release (filed 2026-11-02) cut full-year GAAP EPS guidance to $3.92-4.07, which puts the stock at about 31x this year's reported profit, and only the adjusted $6.92-7.07 figure (about 18x) looks moderate. The business itself is stabilising rather than broken: US rooms are flat after a period of net losses, RevPAR rose 3.8%, the royalty rate is creeping up to 5.23%, and the conversion pipeline is up 29%. But the stock is already up 38% in a year, the cash-flow multiple (20.5x) equals its ten-year median, and growth is low single digits, so the repair is visible and paid for. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.

## NVR (NVR, Inc.): HURT, advance Y

NVR is down 43% from its high because the US housing market is stuck on affordability: Q3 2026 orders fell 7%, the average selling price 5%, and homebuilding gross margin fell to 18.3% from 21.0% on higher lot costs and price cuts, taking quarterly EPS down 20% to $89.45 (8-K filed 2026-10-22). The screen's 11x P/E is on 2025 profits; on the trailing twelve months it is nearer 14x against a 16x median, so it is not yet cheap on current earnings, and earnings are still falling. What argues for a cycle rather than damage is the model: NVR buys lot options instead of owning land, carries almost no net debt, has retired about 12% of its shares in three years, and its backlog is up 10% in units. The risk to test is whether 18% is the bottom for margin and whether the price cuts are cyclical or a reset of what a buyer can pay. Verdict: hurt by the housing cycle, not broken; advance for Phase 4 to normalise margin and test through-cycle earnings.

## MGM (MGM Resorts International): HURT, advance Y

MGM is down 33% from its high because the Las Vegas Strip had a weak 2026 summer: Strip EBITDAR fell 12% to $531M in Q3 on lower room rates, regional same-store EBITDAR fell 7%, and consolidated adjusted EBITDA dropped to $403M from $506M, leaving a small net loss (8-K filed 2026-10-28). The earnings multiple (41x, against 14x historically) therefore says nothing; the real question is free cash flow after rent, which was $1.46B in FY2025 on an $8.5B market cap, with the share count down 44% in five years. The offsets are MGM China at a record 17.1% share and a digital business that is growing, and October room rates were reported to recover, though that evidence is secondary. The business is levered through $21.5B of lease obligations, so a second weak year would hurt equity quickly, and Osaka capex is coming. Verdict: hurt by a cyclical rate dip, not broken; advance for Phase 4 to test rate normalisation and lease-adjusted leverage.

## CZR (Caesars Entertainment, Inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

Caesars screens cheap on EV to operating profit (9x), but that multiple ignores that its interest bill ($570M in Q3, $1.71B in nine months) is bigger than its operating income ($457M in Q3, $1.47B in nine months), so every flat quarter is a loss: Q3 net loss was $123M against $55M a year before (8-K filed 2026-10-27). Las Vegas revenue is falling (-2.3%), regional growth is 5.9% but does not cover the interest, and the digital business earned $44M of EBITDA on $326M of revenue after years of spend. Net debt is $10.8B against a $6.0B equity value, so the shares are a leveraged bet on a Strip recovery rather than a mispriced compounding business. Verdict: down for a reason (over-levered balance sheet and thin profit), reject.

## YUM (Yum! Brands, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Yum is a franchise toll collector priced like one. The stock sits 3.5% below its high at 28x trailing earnings against a 23x decade median, with a 3.8% free-cash-flow yield. The Q3 2026 release (filed 2026-11-03) shows 7% unit growth but flat worldwide same-store sales, Taco Bell's US same-store sales down 4%, and ex-Pizza Hut core operating profit up only 1%; the headline GAAP EPS of $5.92 is mostly the $1.7B Pizza Hut sale gain. There is no discount to explain, only a fairly valued business with a slowing US flagship. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.

## BKNG (Booking Holdings Inc.): HURT, advance Y

Booking is down 33% from its high while the numbers keep growing: Q3 2026 room nights rose 7%, gross bookings 10%, adjusted EBITDA 9% at a 47% margin and adjusted EPS 17% (8-K filed 2026-11-03). Two things explain the price. One is a $1.0B FTC charge ($900M taken out of revenue, which is why reported revenue fell 1%), a one-off that excludes cleanly from the adjusted numbers. The other is the worry that AI assistants will sit between travellers and booking sites; nothing in the filing shows it yet, but marketing spend grew 12% over nine months against adjusted revenue growth of about 10% (my arithmetic, adding back the $900M), a mild gap but the number to watch because a disintermediated platform would show it first. The screen's trailing P/E of 21 is inflated by the charge and its ten-year median is broken by the 25-for-1 split, so the better read is roughly 12.5x forward earnings with 18% fewer shares than three years ago. Verdict: hurt by a one-off and a narrative, not broken; advance for Phase 4 to test marketing efficiency and direct-booking share.

## FUTU (Futu Holdings Limited): HURT, advance Y

Futu is down 57% from its high mainly because of one event: on 2026-05-22 it disclosed that China's securities regulator proposed a fine of about 1.85B yuan ($271M), ordered it to complete rectification within two years and stop providing trading services to mainland clients; the stock fell 31% that day (press reports, secondary). The latest figures (6-K filed 2026-11-19) show the business outside that line still growing: funded accounts +27.9% to 4.0M, trading volume +28.4%, but revenue only +4.5% and profit flat because market sentiment softened and client assets slipped 4.6% in the quarter. The screen's 44.9% cash-flow yield is a brokerage artifact (client money flows through operating cash), so the honest valuation is about 7x earnings, which is low because the market is pricing the regulator, not the demand. What I cannot yet size is how much of the revenue and client assets still come from mainland-resident clients (they were 13% of funded clients, but wealthier clients may be a larger share of assets), and the filings are a 6-K, not a full risk-factor read. Verdict: hurt by a one-off penalty and a rectification order, not by lost demand; advance, with Phase 4 sizing the mainland share of revenue and assets.

## MKTX (MarketAxess Holdings Inc.): DATA_ARTIFACT, advance N

MarketAxess is no longer an ordinary stock. Intercontinental Exchange agreed on 2026-07-29 to buy it for $167 per share in cash, and the stock at $165.40 trades about 1% under that, so the screen's low multiples are the old downtrend frozen in a takeover price (deal terms from press and ICE sources, which I treated as secondary; US antitrust clearance came on 2026-10-21 and closing is expected in Q1 2027). The old story was a fixed-income trading platform whose growth had stalled: Q3 2026 revenue +1%, operating margin down 560bps to 35.4% from a 48.5% median, five-year EPS growth negative, with Tradeweb and dealer-run platforms taking the best volume. The only decision left is a merger-arbitrage spread of about 1% for under six months. Verdict: data artifact (pending takeover), reject.

## SF (Stifel Financial Corp.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Stifel is a good, cyclical financial with nothing hidden: Q3 2026 core EPS of $1.46 was a third-quarter record, year-to-date core EPS is up 22%, return on tangible equity is 23.9%, and bank loans grew 21% (8-K filed 2026-10-21). On GAAP earnings it trades at 16x against an 8.6x ten-year median, and about 12x core earnings and 2.7x tangible book on my arithmetic, so the screen's 10% cash-flow yield (broker cash flows swing with client balances) is not a signal of cheapness. The stock is up 82% in three years and only 20% off its high, and the thing that would hurt it, a trading and deal-fee downturn plus credit risk on a fast-growing loan book, is what the multiple already allows for. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.

## CPRT (Copart, Inc.): HURT, advance Y

Copart is down 51% from its high because its volume has stopped growing: fiscal 2026 revenue was flat at $4.67B, net income fell 4.4% and Q4 EPS fell 14.6% to $0.35, as fewer insured vehicles reached its auctions (the 10-K says U.S. growth came from revenue per car and mix, offset by lower volume; secondary sources attribute it to lower claim counts and drivers dropping collision cover). The multiple has compressed from 32x to 20x and from 52x to 23x on free cash flow, with margin still 35.4%, $1.9B of net cash and $1.63B of buybacks, which tells me the market is pricing the end of volume growth, not a loss of pricing power. The moat looks intact because revenue per car is rising and total-loss frequency is climbing, but a new CEO, a cash acquisition of ACV Auctions and a rival-share claim I could not verify mean this needs a hard look. Verdict: hurt by a volume cycle, not broken on current evidence; advance, with Phase 4 testing insurer share, units per year and the ACV price.

## PAYX (Paychex, Inc.): BORING, advance N

Paychex is a steady, ignored business, but not a bargain. The first quarter of fiscal 2027 (8-K filed 2026-09-23) showed total revenue up 6%, the core Management Solutions line up 4% that the company itself credits to higher revenue per client (price) and product penetration, PEO up 12%, and the full-year revenue guide of 5-6% unchanged. The stock is 24% off its high at 23x trailing and about 18x forward earnings, against a 28x decade median, with a 5.7% free-cash-flow yield and a near-4% dividend. That discount fits a business whose core line grows mostly by price, whose margin (38.6%) is already at its ten-year median, and whose end market is small-business employment that is not growing. There is no hidden change and no catalyst; fair price for a slow compounder. Verdict: boring, not weak, but not cheap enough to advance.

## QLYS (Qualys, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Qualys is a good, steadily growing security-software business and the market already knows it: the stock is up 42% in a year and 14% off its high. Q3 2026 revenue rose 11% to $188.0M, GAAP operating margin held at 35%, GAAP net income grew 14%, and the 2026 revenue guide was raised (8-K filed 2026-11-09). At 35x earnings and a 3.3% free-cash-flow yield after stock pay, the multiple is below the 52x decade median only because growth has settled at 10-11% from 20%. The product is priced on assets scanned, so AI agents do not shrink a seat count, but the market is not afraid of anything and there is nothing to exploit. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.

## CHKP (Check Point Software Technologies Ltd.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

Check Point trades at 14.9x earnings and 13.4x free cash flow against 20x and 16x history, but the profit engine has not grown for a decade: revenue is up 57% since 2016 while operating income stayed near $0.85B (FY25 $0.83B), and the operating margin is 30.5% against a 42.8% median. Q3 2026 revenue fell 1% because products and licences dropped 19% to $105.5M while subscriptions grew 11%, and the CEO said revenue 'reached a trough' with a recovery 'taking longer than anticipated' (6-K filed 2026-11-02). The shift to subscriptions is healthy in principle and the cash margin is 38%, but per-share growth comes from buying back 4% of the company a year, not from the business, and cheaper-looking multiples rest on a repeated promise of recovery. This is a close call because the cash generation is real. Verdict: down for a reason (ten years of flat operating profit and a miss on the recovery), reject.

## FISV (Fiserv, Inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

Fiserv is down 75% from its high because its main growth engine broke and the numbers have not recovered. Nine-month 2026 organic revenue fell 3% (Merchant Solutions -2% in Q3), adjusted operating margin dropped about 7 points in Merchant and 8 points in Financial Solutions versus a year ago, and free cash flow fell from $2.88B to $2.04B (8-K filed 2026-11-03). Management guides organic revenue to -1% to 0% for 2026, the CEO who set the 2025 reset left in June 2026, and there is a net debt load near $25B, so the 9x multiple on adjusted EPS of $7.20-7.40 reflects earnings that are still being cut, not a mood. The offset is a $1B cost-savings programme and a banking-client attrition rate down 25%, but those are management claims, not results. Verdict: down for a reason, reject.
