## HMC (Honda Motor Co., Ltd.): UNSEEN, advance Y

Honda's headline is the EV disaster: JPY1.58tn of EV charges and a first annual operating loss in decades in FY3/26, and a FY3/27 guide of JPY780bn that still includes heavy EV losses. The 6-K for the six months to Sep 2026 shows what sits under it: motorcycles earned JPY439bn (+19%), financial services JPY211bn, and the automobile segment an operating loss of JPY5.7bn on JPY7.5tn of revenue. So the reported total is real money from two arms the narrative ignores, and the car arm is the one the market is discussing. It is not a trapped-asset case in the sense of a lock-up: both arms are wholly owned and consolidated. The risk is that EV losses and supplier compensation consume the cash, which the filing does not rule out. The market looks at the cars, not the segments, so this is unseen rather than judged. Verdict: UNSEEN, advance.

## BIDU (Baidu, Inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

Baidu is not ignored: 31 analysts cover it and the stock is down 57% from its three-year high because the market has read the same numbers I did. Online marketing revenue fell 22% in Q3 2026 to RMB11.9bn and is now under half of the general business, share is moving to ByteDance and Alibaba, and the company is pouring cash into GPUs (FY2025 free cash flow -$2.2bn) after a roughly $16bn impairment earlier in the year. The RMB276.6bn cash and investments pile is verified and the first dividend is promised before year end, but the SOTP treats that cash as distributable while management is clearly choosing to spend it. AI Cloud is growing fast (+94%) yet it replaces a decaying profit pool rather than adding to it. Verdict: down for a reason, reject.

## JD (JD.com, Inc.): HURT, advance Y

JD's weak price is a story the market can see: the food-delivery subsidy war with Meituan and Alibaba took the group to its first quarterly loss in nearly four years (Q4 2025) and halved annual profit, and the stock is 69% below its five-year high. What the Q3 2026 release shows is that the war is cooling: marketing spend fell 26%, New Businesses losses narrowed 'significantly', group operating income was RMB5.2bn and JD Retail held RMB15.1bn, flat on last year at a 5.8% margin. So the cheapness is a cost problem, not demand loss, and the core earns about $8bn a year. What stops me calling it a clear bargain is that the retail core is not growing, management is still spending abroad (CECONOMY), and part of the SOTP cushion is cash that sits inside listed subsidiaries and payables. Verdict: hurt, not broken; advance with the trap noted.

## F (Ford Motor Company): PRICED_IN, advance N

Ford looks like a hidden-segment case: Ford Pro earned $5.1bn of EBIT in nine months and Ford Credit $2.3bn, against a $45bn market cap. But the market has seen all of it, because Ford prints every segment each quarter. It prices the whole at 3.6x free cash flow, the same as its ten-year median, for reasons visible in the 10-Q: Pro EBIT is down 10% year on year, Model e still loses $2.75bn in nine months, $4.5bn of special items (EV write-offs) hit the same period, the Novelis fire disrupted aluminum supply, and tariff costs are only partly reimbursed. Pro cannot be separated from Blue, so the sum of the parts is an accounting view, not an exit. Verdict: priced in at a historic auto discount, reject.

## NWSA (News Corporation): PRICED_IN, advance N

This is the textbook listed-stake case, and the market has found it: 62% of REA Group is about 60% of News Corp's market cap and is reported in plain view every quarter. The gap that remains is 12% after tax, and the price sits at its own ten-year multiple (P/E 39.6 vs 44.2). The stake is not trapped by debt (REA's borrowings are non-recourse) but by control and tax: the family holds the votes, a sale of REA would trigger tax on a near-zero basis, and the only action is a buyback. Nobody is failing to look. Verdict: priced in with no catalyst, reject.

## THC (Tenet Healthcare Corporation): PRICED_IN, advance N

Tenet is the opposite of an ignored asset. The surgery-center arm (USPI, $1.56bn of Adjusted EBITDA in nine months) is reported as its own segment, 22 analysts follow the stock, and it has risen 318% in three years to a multiple (9x earnings) above its own ten-year median of 6x. Two things cap the hidden value: physician partners take a large share of USPI profit ($642m of noncontrolling earnings in nine months), and the hospital base is leaning on one-off contract revenue while exchange-covered admissions fall. The conservative gap of 11% is inside the error of the multiples I chose. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## L (Loews Corporation): PRICED_IN, advance N

Loews is a famous holding company with a Tisch-family owner, and every part is reported: CNA (a listed insurer worth about $12bn at market), Boardwalk Pipelines, hotels and a parent cash pile. A conservative sum is $23.4bn against $22.2bn, a 5% cushion that one multiple change erases. The family retires stock rather than unlocking anything, so there is no event to close even that small gap. The market has looked and has priced it. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## EQH (Equitable Holdings, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Equitable owns 68% of AllianceBernstein, but it reports it as its own segment, the market has doubled the share price in three years, and the parts add to within 2% of the market cap. The screen's near-zero enterprise value is an insurance-accounting quirk, not a bargain. Management buys back shares rather than separating AB. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## LVS (Las Vegas Sands Corp.): HURT, advance N

Las Vegas Sands is down 42% from its three-year high because Macau profit is slipping, a large expansion is being funded at Marina Bay Sands, and short sellers hold 18.6% of the float. That is a cyclical bruise, not a loss of franchise. But the hidden-room test fails: Sands China at market plus MBS at 9x EBITDA less debt gives $24.9bn against a $24.6bn price, so the market is already paying for the parts. Verdict: hurt, but no gap, do not advance.

## BIO (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Bio-Rad is a case where few analysts follow the stock but the asset is not hidden: the Sartorius shares and the EUR400m secured loan are described in detail in every 10-Q. The market cap is $10.0bn against a taxed stake of about $5.2bn plus a core business with revenue flat for three years and a 1.8% operating margin. Take the tax out and value the core at a plain 10x free cash flow, and the sum is $9.8bn, the price. The stake is locked behind a family-controlled holder and a US tax bill. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## BN (Brookfield Corporation): PRICED_IN, advance N

Brookfield is the best-known discount in the market, which is why it no longer exists in strict numbers: the listed stakes alone equal about $84bn against an $87.7bn market cap before corporate debt and preferreds. The remaining argument is that carried interest and insurance earnings are worth more than book, and that is a growth debate the market has openly priced. The EV of $324bn on the screen is a consolidation artifact. Verdict: priced in, reject.
