# Phase 3, batch 2: why is it cheap? (run 2026-11-19)

## TAP | Molson Coors Beverage Company | DOWN_FOR_REASON | advance N

Molson Coors looks cheap at about 7x free cash flow and a 14% cash yield, but the cheapness is the market correctly pricing a shrinking product. Brand volume fell 4.9% in Q3 2026 and 4.3% over nine months, price and mix added only 1.8%, cost of goods per hectolitre rose 8.6%, and underlying pre-tax profit fell 17.4%; management then cut 2026 guidance for the third time in two years, to underlying pre-tax income down 21% to 24%. The $3.65B goodwill write-down of the Americas business in Q3 2025 was the company itself conceding the US beer franchise is worth less, the share gains from the 2023 Bud Light boycott have faded, and the 'Horizon 2030' plan launched this year has not stopped the slide. Leverage is creeping up (net debt to EBITDA 2.53x from 2.28x) while the company still spends on buybacks. Nothing here is a cycle: Americans are drinking less beer, and the cash yield is the price of a business in slow liquidation. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, reject.

## CELH | Celsius Holdings, Inc. | DOWN_FOR_REASON | advance N

Celsius is down 66% from its 3-year high, but it is not even cheap: 76x trailing GAAP earnings, about 25x free cash flow and a 3.9% FCF yield. What fell is the growth story. The CELSIUS brand itself shrank about 6% in Q3 2026 (after roughly -12% in Q2), with US retail sales down 7%, while all the reported growth came from Alani Nu, bought in 2025, which rose 24%; Rockstar, taken over from PepsiCo, is down 13% at retail. Gross margin dropped 450 bps to 46.8% on aluminium, fuel and the costlier PepsiCo distribution mix, and adjusted EPS was flat year on year despite a 17% revenue jump. The company has issued shares to buy growth (diluted shares +14% in five years) and leans on PepsiCo for distribution, while Costco's own-label energy drink presses on the club channel where CELSIUS is weak. This is a flagship brand losing relevance inside a growing category, priced at a multiple that still assumes growth. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, reject.

## BF-B | Brown-Forman Corporation | DOWN_FOR_REASON | advance N

Brown-Forman trades at about 20x earnings against a 29x ten-year median and is down 45% from its 3-year high, and on the surface that looks like a premium brand owner caught in a spirits downcycle. The filings point to something slower and less reversible. Revenue peaked at $4.23B in FY2022 and was $3.93B in FY2026; Q1 FY27 organic sales fell 1%, tequila fell 13% organic, developed international markets fell 8%, and management guides FY27 organic operating income down 3% to 5% in an 'operating environment' it calls challenging because of falling beverage-alcohol consumption in developed markets. The flattering 15.8x free-cash-flow multiple is mostly a working-capital effect: the company cut cash spent on new inventory from $403M in FY2023 to $22M in FY2026, which means it is distilling less whiskey because it expects to sell less of it in four to seven years. Growth now rests on Mexico's New Mix ready-to-drink cans and a Jack Daniel's flavour extension, neither of which carries Jack Daniel's economics. A great brand in a shrinking habit at 20x earnings is not a mispricing. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, reject.

## EDU | New Oriental Education & Technology Group Inc. | HURT | advance Y

New Oriental is 43% below its 3-year high and fell about 12% on 2026-10-28 even though Q1 FY27 revenue grew 23.1% and operating income 20.5%; what the market punished was a flat operating margin (20.0% vs 20.4%) as low-margin East Buy grocery livestreaming took a bigger share of revenue, on top of an overseas-study business growing only 7.7% because US policy is turning away Chinese students. Demand in the core is plainly intact: domestic adult test prep +22.3%, new education lines +24.7%, prepaid tuition (deferred revenue) up 11.2% to $2.12B, full-year guidance of +14% to +18% confirmed. Against an $8.2B market cap the company holds $5.62B of cash, term deposits and short-term investments (about $3.5B after netting the prepaid tuition it owes students), trades near 10.7x forward earnings, and is returning about $500M (6% of market cap) this year. The real risks are not in this year's numbers: China's births roughly halved from 17.9M in 2016 to about 9M in 2023-24, which will shrink the K-12 pool its non-academic tutoring depends on from the late 2020s, and investors own a Cayman holding company under a regulator that wiped out the old model in 2021. Those risks deserve a Phase 4 test, but the current discount looks like a margin-mix and geopolitics bruise on a growing, cash-rich business. Verdict: HURT, advance, with demographics and VIE structure as the two things to disprove.

## ADM | Archer-Daniels-Midland Company | PRICED_IN | advance N

ADM is a commodity processor whose profit swings with crush and ethanol margins, and it landed on the rebuild list because 2025 was a trough (net income $1.08B vs $4.34B in 2022) after a margin collapse and the November 2024 restatement scare in its Nutrition segment. That trough is already behind it and everyone can see it: Q3 2026 segment operating profit rose 64% to $1.38B, Crushing alone added $298M thanks to the EPA's March 2026 biofuel volume rule, and management raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $5.50-$5.80. At $67.78 the stock trades at about 12x that guidance, which is its own ten-year median of 12.6x, and it is up 15% in a year. The screen's eye-catching 13% free-cash-flow yield is partly an artifact: $1.51B of FY2025 operating cash came from inventories shrinking as grain prices fell, which reverses when prices rise. What remains is a well-run cyclical priced at its normal mid-cycle multiple on a recovery that depends on Washington keeping biofuel mandates generous. Verdict: PRICED_IN, reject.

## LW | Lamb Weston Holdings, Inc. | DOWN_FOR_REASON | advance N

Lamb Weston is down 49% from its 3-year high, but almost all of that is earnings falling, not the multiple: on FY27 guidance of $3.05-$3.35 adjusted EPS and flat adjusted EBITDA of $1.125-$1.215B it trades at about 16x earnings and 9x EBITDA, and EV/EBIT of 18.4x is above its 10-year median of 17.2x. The earnings fell because the fry shortage of 2022-23 ended and the industry, including new producers outside the big four, added capacity; Lamb Weston responded by cutting price (price/mix -6% in FY2026, -2% again in Q1 FY27) to win volume, which it did in North America (+7% volume, seventh straight quarter of growth), while International sales fell 8% and that segment's EBITDA dropped 54% to $27M, forcing a Dutch plant closure. Operating margin is 8.9% against a 15.5% ten-year median, debt is $3.9B, and the activist-driven board overhaul and new CEO have so far produced a cost programme, not pricing power. A commodity processor that has lost its pricing power, priced at a normal multiple on flat profits, is not a mispricing. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, reject.

## GIS | General Mills, Inc. | DOWN_FOR_REASON | advance N

General Mills trades at about 11x its own FY27 guidance of $3.00-$3.20 adjusted EPS, down 48% from its 3-year high, with a dividend near 7%, which looks like a classic bruised staple until you look at the trend. Organic sales were flat in Q1 FY27 only because pet and foodservice price/mix offset falling volume, North America Retail organic sales fell 3% with cereal down double digits, adjusted operating profit fell 11%, and management guides another 8% to 13% drop in operating profit for the year after already cutting prices in FY2026 to defend share against private label. The company names weight-loss trends as a demand risk in its own release, has written off $1.62B of brand value in three years, has sold yogurt and Brazil to shrink toward the better pieces, and has stopped buying back stock to protect a balance sheet carrying $12.1B of net debt. A falling P/E on falling earnings in a shrinking centre-of-store category is not a bruise, it is the market tracking a slow decline correctly. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, reject.

## CPB | The Campbell's Company | DOWN_FOR_REASON | advance N

Campbell's is down 47% from its 3-year high and yet on its own guidance it is not cheap: FY27 adjusted EPS of $1.65-$1.80 puts the stock at about 14x, above its 10-year median of 15.5x on trailing numbers only because the E is falling so fast. Adjusted EPS goes from $2.97 in FY2025 to $2.17 in FY2026 to roughly $1.73 in FY2027, organic sales are guided down 2% to 4% after falling 2%, and the Snacks business (Goldfish, Pepperidge Farm, the old Snyder's-Lance chips) has been weak enough that management reshuffled the segments. The 2024 purchase of Sovos Brands for Rao's sauce left $6.1B of net debt against a $7.3B equity value and interest cover of 2.6x, so the board cut the dividend 36% in September 2026 and started yet another cost programme, $500M by 2030, before finishing the last one. Short interest is 25% of the float. This is a shrinking, over-levered packaged-food company in the middle of its decline, not after it. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, reject.

## CAG | Conagra Brands, Inc. | DOWN_FOR_REASON | advance N

Conagra is the cheapest of the packaged-food rebuilds on paper, about 10x FY27 adjusted EPS guidance and 7x trailing free cash flow, and it is cheap because its earnings keep stepping down. Adjusted EPS was $1.72 in FY2026 and is guided to $1.40-$1.50 in FY2027, organic sales are guided down 1% to 3% after volume fell 2.1% in Q1 and 5.4% in Grocery & Snacks, and the company wrote off $2.0B of goodwill and brands in Q4 FY2026 alone ($7.0B over three years), which is management agreeing the Pinnacle-era brands are worth far less. Net leverage is 3.99x, and the $1.40 annual dividend now consumes about 97% of adjusted earnings, so a new CEO who talks of 'rebalancing capital allocation' is very likely to cut it. The free cash flow yield of 13.8% is real today but it is the yield on a portfolio of frozen dinners and microwave snacks that shoppers are buying less of every quarter. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, reject.

## MO | Altria Group, Inc. | PRICED_IN | advance N

Altria is a genuine toll road, 42% operating margins and 38% return on capital for a decade, but it made the toll-road list without being on sale: the stock is 3% below its 3-year high, up 38% in a year, and trades at about 13.4x the raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $5.66-$5.74 and 14x free cash flow, against a ten-year median near 7.5x on both measures. The re-rating came from cigarette volume declines easing (industry down about 5% in Q3 2026, helped by enforcement against illicit vapes) and steady 4-6% EPS growth from price increases and buybacks, while the smoke-free bet is mixed: on! pouches are growing, NJOY's ACE device remains off the market after the patent loss, and revenue still slides from $26.15B in 2020 to $23.28B in 2025. Everybody knows the shape of this business and the market now pays more for it than it used to, not less. Verdict: PRICED_IN, reject for this hunt.

## TPL | Texas Pacific Land Corporation | PRICED_IN | advance N

Texas Pacific Land owns about 880,000 surface acres and royalty interests in the Permian, and it is a real toll road: 74% operating margin, no debt, almost no capital spending. It screened as a slipped toll road because the stock is 50% below its 3-year high, but that high was the late-2024 peak when the market priced data-center land and produced-water desalination as if they were already big businesses. The business since then has done fine: Q3 2026 set records in revenue ($273.4M), net income ($167.4M), FCF ($170.3M) and royalty production (42.3k Boe/day). On that run rate the stock trades near 29x earnings and a 3.5% FCF yield, in line with its ten-year median P/E of 32 and above it on trailing numbers (41x); the yfinance forward P/E of 3.9 is a per-share data glitch, not a signal. The market is paying a full toll-road price on top of an oil-price-sensitive royalty stream, with optionality on data centers that is still mostly announcements. Verdict: PRICED_IN, reject for this hunt.

## EOG | EOG Resources, Inc. | DATA_ARTIFACT | advance N

EOG made the Quiet Vault list on a 12.9% free-cash-flow yield that does not exist: the metrics file recorded zero capex because EOG reports its drilling spend as 'Additions to Oil and Gas Properties', which the screen did not catch. FY2025 operating cash flow was $10.04B against $6.115B of that capex (plus $4.45B for the Encino acquisition funded partly with $4.47B of new debt), so true free cash flow was about $3.9B, roughly a 5% yield at today's $77.6B market cap. The real situation is a best-in-class shale producer enjoying an oil price spike: realized US crude went from $59.54 in Q4 2025 to $98.18 in Q2 2026 and $86.73 in Q3, nine-month net income is already $7.49B against $4.98B for all of 2025, and the stock is up 41% in a year to within 10% of its high. Roughly 8x earnings at war-inflated oil prices is what a cyclical normally trades at near a peak, so there is no mispricing to find once the capex is put back. Verdict: DATA_ARTIFACT, real numbers do not qualify, reject.

## NOV | NOV Inc. | PRICED_IN | advance N

NOV makes rigs, drill pipe and offshore production equipment, and its revenue of about $8.7B is less than half of what it sold at the 2014 peak; operating margins have hovered around zero for a decade (ten-year median 0%, five-year average return on capital -2.8%). It screened well because 2025 free cash flow was $0.88B, a 14.6% yield, but that was cash released from working capital after the 2022-23 build, and it has already swung back: nine-month 2026 operating cash flow was $32M and free cash flow -$202M as the company builds inventory against its backlog. On earnings the stock is not cheap: EV/EBIT of 13.5x sits on its own ten-year median of 12.9x and roughly 6x EBITDA is normal for oilfield equipment. The order book is soft too, with a 91% book-to-bill in Energy Equipment, backlog down 11% on the year, and management guiding Q4 flat while warning Middle East customers are trimming drilling. Higher oil prices from the conflict help sentiment, but this is a fairly priced, low-return cyclical. Verdict: PRICED_IN, reject.

## SOBO | South Bow Corporation | DATA_ARTIFACT | advance N

South Bow owns the Keystone crude pipeline from Hardisty, Alberta to the US Midwest and Gulf Coast, spun out of TC Energy in October 2024, so the toll-road screen judged it on a carve-out history of three years and on a 2025 cash flow line with capex missing. On real numbers it is an ordinary, leveraged pipeline: about $1.05B of annual normalized EBITDA, an enterprise value of $11.7B (roughly 11x EBITDA, the normal midstream range), net debt of $4.44B at 4.3x EBITDA, and a $2.00 dividend that takes about 60% of distributable cash flow. Volumes are flat to slightly down (Marketlink -6%, Gulf Coast leg -6% in Q3 2026) as the Trans Mountain expansion gives Canadian crude a western outlet, and the stock is up 22% in a year, so there is no slipped multiple to buy. The November 2026 auditor switch from KPMG to Deloitte followed a tender, which I note but which shows no disagreement in the filings I read. Verdict: DATA_ARTIFACT; the real situation is a fairly priced, indebted pipeline, reject.

## SEIC | SEI Investments Company | PRICED_IN | advance N

SEI runs back-office technology and outsourced operations for wealth managers, banks and fund managers, plus its own investment products, and it is a sturdy business: 27% operating margins for a decade, net cash, and a share count down 15% in five years. It landed on the toll-road list without any price slip worth the name: the stock is 5.5% off its 3-year high after rising 36% in a year, and at 17.5x trailing earnings it sits exactly on its ten-year median of 17.8x (EV/EBIT 19.4x is above its 16.7x median). The business is accelerating, not stalling: Q3 2026 revenue +14%, adjusted EPS +24%, a record year of new sales, and an $800M buyback top-up. On the AI question, SEI sells processing and outsourced operations priced on assets and accounts rather than seats, and its fund-administration revenue is growing 14%, so automation is so far a margin tailwind rather than a shrinking need. It is a good company at a fair price that everybody can see. Verdict: PRICED_IN, reject for this hunt.

## AMP | Ameriprise Financial, Inc. | BORING | advance Y

Ameriprise screened as a Quiet Vault on numbers that do not mean what they say: its 18.7% free-cash-flow yield and $6.2B of 'net cash' include Ameriprise Bank deposits and insurance-company cash that belong to regulated subsidiaries, so the honest owner-earnings figure is adjusted operating earnings of about $4.2B a year, roughly a 9-10% earnings yield on the $43.5B market cap. Even on that basis it is a dull, high-quality compounder: Q3 2026 adjusted operating EPS ex unlocking rose 22% to $12.07, wrap assets grew 12%, advisors from Huntington and Comerica are joining, return on equity is 56%, and 93% of earnings went back to shareholders, which is why the share count is down 22% in five years. The low multiple (about 10-11x forward, against a ten-year median of 12.5x) is old and has reasons: earnings track equity markets, which are high, falling rates squeeze cash-sweep and bank income, the annuity book carries tail risk, and the asset manager bleeds money ($9.3B of outflows in Q3). None of that is new or getting worse in the filings, and AI so far shows up as rising revenue per advisor (+13%) rather than fewer advisors. Verdict: BORING, advance, with market sensitivity and the annuity book as the risks to test.
