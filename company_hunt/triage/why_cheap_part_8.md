## JPM (JPMorgan Chase & Co.): PRICED_IN, advance N

JPMorgan is the honest answer to whether the Many-Weathers idea works: it does, and the market already knows. Its businesses really do offset each other (consumer banking fell 7% in 2020 while the investment bank rose 21%; the reverse in 2022), and Q3 2026 showed all of them growing at once: net income $15.3B, ROTCE 20%, CET1 13.9%, card charge-offs a tame 2.91%. But the stock is at $324, 15x trailing earnings against a 10x ten-year median and about 2.9x tangible book value ($112.64), after a 125% three-year run. Even management signals the price is full: net buybacks dropped to $664M in the quarter from $31.6B in all of 2025, because Dimon would rather keep capital than retire shares at this level, and he lists deficits, sticky inflation, high asset prices and rising rates as the things that could go wrong. Segment profit still swings together with credit reserves (+0.64 correlation), so the offset protects revenue, not earnings. Alex owns it and should keep owning it for quality, but as a mispricing it is not one. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.

## BAC (Bank of America Corporation): PRICED_IN, advance N

Bank of America looks hurt, and it is, but the hurt is already in a normal price. The stock is 24% below its three-year high because Q3 2026 carried a $1.2B anti-money-laundering penalty (about $0.17 a share) on top of a revenue miss: investment banking fees fell 11% and bond-trading revenue 17%. Underneath, net interest income grew 8% to $16.4B, wealth revenue grew 13%, charge-offs were flat at $1.3B and adjusted ROTCE was about 14.9%. At $48.91 the stock trades at 11x trailing earnings against a 10.8x ten-year median and 1.7x tangible book ($29.14), with a 9.4x forward multiple: a fair price for a 13-15% return bank, not a discount. Its segment mix is also the weakest of the three big banks for this hunt, since total segment profit still fell 36% in 2020 and the 2020 reserve build cut profit in three of four segments. Verdict: priced in at its own normal multiple; the penalty is a one-off, but there is no mispricing to buy. Reject.
