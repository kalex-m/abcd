## JPM (JPMorgan Chase & Co.): PRICED_IN, advance N

JPMorgan is the honest answer to whether the Many-Weathers idea works: it does, and the market already knows. Its businesses really do offset each other (consumer banking fell 7% in 2020 while the investment bank rose 21%; the reverse in 2022), and Q3 2026 showed all of them growing at once: net income $15.3B, ROTCE 20%, CET1 13.9%, card charge-offs a tame 2.91%. But the stock is at $324, 15x trailing earnings against a 10x ten-year median and about 2.9x tangible book value ($112.64), after a 125% three-year run. Even management signals the price is full: net buybacks dropped to $664M in the quarter from $31.6B in all of 2025, because Dimon would rather keep capital than retire shares at this level, and he lists deficits, sticky inflation, high asset prices and rising rates as the things that could go wrong. Segment profit still swings together with credit reserves (+0.64 correlation), so the offset protects revenue, not earnings. Alex owns it and should keep owning it for quality, but as a mispricing it is not one. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.

## BAC (Bank of America Corporation): PRICED_IN, advance N

Bank of America looks hurt, and it is, but the hurt is already in a normal price. The stock is 24% below its three-year high because Q3 2026 carried a $1.2B anti-money-laundering penalty (about $0.17 a share) on top of a revenue miss: investment banking fees fell 11% and bond-trading revenue 17%. Underneath, net interest income grew 8% to $16.4B, wealth revenue grew 13%, charge-offs were flat at $1.3B and adjusted ROTCE was about 14.9%. At $48.91 the stock trades at 11x trailing earnings against a 10.8x ten-year median and 1.7x tangible book ($29.14), with a 9.4x forward multiple: a fair price for a 13-15% return bank, not a discount. Its segment mix is also the weakest of the three big banks for this hunt, since total segment profit still fell 36% in 2020 and the 2020 reserve build cut profit in three of four segments. Verdict: priced in at its own normal multiple; the penalty is a one-off, but there is no mispricing to buy. Reject.

## C (Citigroup Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Citi was the classic cheap bank for a decade and the screen still remembers that, but the stock has moved from roughly 0.6x book to 1.15x tangible book ($117 against $101.59) and is up 182% in three years. Q3 2026 was good: revenue $23.8B (+8%), EPS $2.85, Services up 15%, charge-offs flat at $2.2B, CET1 12.8%. The 13.6x trailing multiple against an 8.5x median is flattered by a lower base, and the 11-13% return target (14-15% later) means the stock is priced for a turnaround that management has not yet finished; the ground-5 history is also blunt: Services offset Banking in 2022 but segment profit still fell 34% and EPS compounded only 7.7% a year. Not a hidden problem, just a fair price for a business that earns about its cost of capital. Verdict: priced in, reject for this hunt.

## BNY (The Bank of New York Mellon Corporation): PRICED_IN, advance N

BNY is a custody and clearing bank whose earnings improved a lot: ROTCE of 29% in Q3 2026 against about 15% a few years ago, revenue +12%, EPS +24%, assets under custody up 10% to $63.4T, almost no credit cost. The market noticed. The stock is up 236% in three years and trades at 17.9x trailing earnings against an 11.3x ten-year median, and 19x free cash flow against 8x. Its three segments did offset in 2022 (securities services +11% while investment and wealth management fell 12%), but the real driver under all of them is the level of global asset values and rates, which are now high, so the offset helps less than it looks. Nothing here is hidden or ignored; it is a good business priced as one. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## RJF (Raymond James Financial, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Raymond James had its worst day since March 2020 in February 2026 (-8.8%) when an AI tax tool made investors fear that advisors' fees would be competed away, and it carries a separate worry that cash-sweep income falls as clients move cash to higher-yielding accounts (sweep balances are down 40-50% per management). The numbers do not show damage: fiscal 2026 brought record revenue of $15.79B, record EPS of $11.91, adjusted ROTCE of 23%, advisor recruiting at records, and domestic net new assets of $98B, up 88%. At $162 the stock is about 13.6x FY26 earnings against a 12.9x ten-year median, so the recovery from the February scare has already happened and the price sits on its own history. The offset between the bank and capital markets was real in 2022-23, but private client (over 60% of revenue) and the bank both ride the same rate tailwind, so it is one rate bet in several boxes. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## HIG (The Hartford Insurance Group, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

The Hartford is a good insurer with three different engines (business P&C, personal P&C, employee benefits), and the mix has protected revenue: it never fell by more than 6% a year in any segment. But the price is already at its ten-year median (9.5x earnings, forward 10x), after an 88% three-year run, and the current direction is slightly down: core earnings fell 6% in Q3 2026 and the CEO describes an 'increasingly competitive market', which is how a softening P&C cycle starts. A 17.9% core ROE and 6% annual buyback shrink are real strengths, yet there is no gap between price and substance to exploit. Verdict: priced in, reject.

## ACGL (Arch Capital Group Ltd.): HURT, advance N

Arch is a great underwriter caught in the part of the cycle where prices for property and casualty cover stop rising. Q3 2026 still showed a 16% operating return on equity and an 82.5% combined ratio, but net premiums earned fell 6.3%, underwriting income fell 19%, and the CEO says the market is 'transitioning'. The 8x trailing multiple is a peak-earnings illusion: the forward multiple is 10.9x, above its own 10.1x median, and the stock is within 2% of its high at 1.6x book. The label is 'hurt by a cycle', but the stock is not hurt in price, and the ground-5 offset (mortgage insurance against P&C) has faded to 7% of premiums, so there is no real diversifier left. Verdict: a cycle turn already in the forward multiple; do not advance.

## MET (MetLife, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

MetLife's reported earnings are noisy because the accounting runs interest-rate hedges and market-risk benefits through net income (Q3 2026 net income $3.67 a share, adjusted $2.78, with a $698M derivative swing), which is why the screen sees 19.8x GAAP earnings against an 11.5x median. On adjusted earnings the multiple is closer to 9x, and the business is in strong shape: highest quarterly adjusted EPS in company history, revenue up in all six segments, adjusted ROE 19.6%, 17% of shares retired in three years. That is a fair-to-good business at a fair price, up 36% in a year and 3% below its high; I could not verify a ten-year adjusted-earnings multiple history to prove a discount, and nothing visible suggests one. Every segment feeds off the same rates and credit-spread machine, so the offset is thin. Verdict: priced in; the screen's multiple is a quirk and the corrected number is no bargain. Reject.
