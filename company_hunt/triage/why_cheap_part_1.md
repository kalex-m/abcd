# Phase 3 batch 1: why is it cheap?

Run date 2026-11-19. One paragraph per company, my own reading of the primary sources, ending on the verdict.

## CTVA (Corteva, Inc.): HURT, advance Y

The number that put Corteva on two screens is fake: the FY2025 free cash flow of $2.8B and the 50% FCF yield mix the old seed-plus-chemicals company with today's share price, but on 1 October 2026 the seed business (Pioneer, the bigger and better half) left as Vylor and what still trades as CTVA is a pure crop-protection company worth $5.6B. That company then reported a bad first quarter on its own: Q3 pro forma sales fell 9% to $1.59B, entirely because Latin America collapsed 31% organically as Brazilian farmers, squeezed by credit costs, stopped stocking up and generic copies pushed down prices on old molecules, while North America (+7%), EMEA (+14%) and Asia (+5%) grew; full-year EBITDA guidance dropped from about $1.3B to $1.10-1.125B and the stock fell 24% in a week to $8.36. The segment history in the 10-Ks shows crop-protection EBITDA of $1.0-1.1B in 2019-20, a $1.68B peak in 2022 and $1.35B in 2025, so 2026 is back at the old floor rather than through it, and at roughly 4.4x that floor with $0.8B of net cash (pro forma, before $0.8B of pension) the price implies the business is in permanent decline. The genuine risk is that Chinese generics keep eroding prices on off-patent chemistry, as they did to FMC, and that the spin left this half without the steadier seed earnings to hide behind; new-product growth in three of four regions argues against that being the whole story. Verdict: HURT, a Brazil credit cycle plus generic pricing on top of a spin-off the screen misread; advance, with Phase 4 checking standalone costs, Brazil receivables and how much of sales are off-patent molecules.

## CF (CF Industries Holdings, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

CF Industries is a very well run commodity producer that happens to be enjoying a war bonus. The Q3 2026 deck (filed 2026-11-04) shows LTM adjusted EBITDA of $3.8B and net earnings of $2.2B, against $1.5-1.8B of net income in 2024-25 and $0.4-0.9B in 2016-2020, because the 2026 Gulf conflict and the closing of the Strait of Hormuz cut Middle East nitrogen exports and pushed urea up roughly 77% at one point; CF ran its plants at 97% of available capacity and returned $1.3B to shareholders over the last year. The stock already moved: it is up 43% in a year and the 7.5x trailing P/E and 4.5x EV/EBITDA are what a nitrogen producer always trades at near the top, since the market rightly refuses to capitalise shock-level prices that will fade once Gulf supply returns and the new export plants of the next few years come on. There is nothing the market is missing here; the buybacks (shares -25% in five years) are real and the cost position on US gas is a genuine advantage, but none of that is hidden. Verdict: PRICED_IN, a cyclical at peak-shock earnings with a fair multiple; reject.

## CE (Celanese Corporation): HURT, advance Y

Celanese is cheap because it bought DuPont's Mobility & Materials business for about $11B with borrowed money in 2022, right before autos, industrial demand and Chinese acetyl capacity turned against it; the result was $5.8B of impairments over three years, two years of GAAP losses, a dividend cut to almost nothing and a stock down 71% from its high. The Q3 2026 release (filed 2026-11-02) shows the repair is working: sales +6% to $2.56B, operating EBITDA $548M against $517M a year earlier, free cash flow $461M in the quarter and a raised full-year FCF target of $750-800M, with roughly $650M of divestiture proceeds expected by year end. The catch is the capital structure: net debt is still about $10.2B and management itself expects to end 2026 at about 4.7x EBITDA, so the $5.3B equity is a leveraged bet on the chemical cycle, and the 7.9x adjusted P/E leans on an 8% adjusted tax rate and on adding back large items (Q3 GAAP EPS $0.68 vs adjusted $1.65). The franchise (acetyls cost position, engineering polymers into medical and electronics) is intact and the cash is real, which makes this a self-inflicted balance-sheet injury rather than a dying business, but the stock is already up 33% in a year on exactly that story. Verdict: HURT; advance, with Phase 4 testing GAAP-to-adjusted gaps, debt maturities and how much of the recovery is cycle versus self-help.

## TTD (The Trade Desk, Inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

The Trade Desk sells software that lets ad agencies buy ads across the open internet and streaming TV, and charges a cut of the spend; for a decade that cut held at around 20% and revenue compounded 20-30% a year. In 2026 it broke: Q3 revenue was $736M, down 0.5% on a year earlier after +18% in Q3 2025, adjusted EBITDA margin fell from 43% to 36%, and Q4 guidance of at least $755M implies roughly an 11% decline against about $851M a year ago (EX-99.1, filed 2026-11-09). The reasons in the coverage, which match the numbers, are that Amazon is offering its own buying platform at fees as low as 1% plus spending matches, that Publicis and others pushed back on fees, and that big CPG and auto brands are moving budget toward walled gardens, so the company is losing share and price at the same time in a market that is still growing. Customer retention above 95% and the Netflix integration show the product still matters, and CEO Jeff Green put $148M of his own money in at about $24-25 in March 2026, but stock pay of $0.49B a year swallows most of the $0.78B free cash flow, so the real owner yield is about 4%, not 11%. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, a toll collector whose toll is being cut by a bigger rival; reject.

## NTES (NetEase, Inc.): BORING, advance Y

Two of NetEase's three screen hits are partly fake: the '10-year median P/E of 82' comes from mixing ADS prices with ordinary-share counts (one ADS is five shares), so the stock is not 80% below its own history; on real numbers it trades at about 15x trailing earnings, which is the low end of where it has usually been. The real story is plain: 2026 is a flat year after the 2025 hits (Marvel Rivals, Where Winds Meet) matured, with Q3 revenue -1.8% to RMB27.9B and nine-month net income down 7% to RMB26.5B, while costs fell (opex -5.4%) and gross profit actually rose 4.8% (6-K filed 2026-11-19). Underneath, the balance sheet keeps fattening: net cash went from RMB163.5B to RMB175.2B (US$26.1B, a third of the $75.8B market value) in nine months, FY2025 free cash flow was $7.1B, and the company pays a quarterly dividend, so the ex-cash business sells for roughly 12x earnings and about 14% FCF on enterprise value. What the market is pricing is a hit-driven studio with no growth this year, sitting behind a VIE structure and a China discount everyone knows about; Fantasy Westward Journey is over twenty years old and still paying, which says the franchises last longer than the multiple assumes. Verdict: BORING, a cash machine in a flat year wearing a China discount; advance, with Phase 4 testing how much of the cash is freely distributable offshore and how dependent 2027 is on Ananta.

## SIRI (Sirius XM Holdings Inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

Sirius XM throws off a lot of cash, about $1.40B of free cash flow guided for 2026 against a $10.8B market value, and the Q3 2026 release (filed 2026-10-29) shows management squeezing costs well: adjusted EBITDA up 3% to $694M, margin up a point to 32%, FCF up 14%, guidance raised twice. But the top line tells the real story: revenue was $9.00B in 2022 and is guided to about $8.55B in 2026, Pandora's paying base keeps slipping (5.5M, subscriber revenue -2%), and the 'positive' 5,000 self-pay net adds in Q3 came only from 107,000 cheap companion add-on plans while the release itself admits lower conversion of new-car trials, which is the pipe that refills the base. That is what a business losing its slot to CarPlay, Android Auto and Spotify looks like in slow motion; cost cuts and buybacks can keep per-share cash rising for a while, as they did at many shrinking media companies, but the market is right not to pay a growth multiple, and after a 60% rally in twelve months the stock is no longer even obviously cheap at about 7x EBITDA with 3.3x net debt. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, a slowly shrinking audience managed for cash; reject.

## CHTR (Charter Communications, Inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

Charter's 5x earnings multiple is not an oversight; it is what a market pays for a heavily indebted business whose main product is losing customers. The Q3 2026 release (filed 2026-11-06) shows pro forma Internet customers down 233,000 in a single quarter, revenue -1.5%, adjusted EBITDA -4.0% and a $6.3B franchise impairment, and management responded by announcing a 'new operating model' and a $2B cost-cut plan with the explicit goal of returning to Internet customer growth, which is an admission that it currently has none. The cause is structural: fibre overbuilders and the wireless carriers' fixed-wireless home internet have broken the local cable monopoly that justified the old 16x multiple, and the Cox merger (closed 19 Aug 2026) doubled down on the same footprint with more debt, leaving $108.6B of borrowings against a roughly $25B equity value, so a few percent fall in EBITDA moves the equity value by far more. Mobile is the one growing line (+508,000 lines) but it is resold on Verizon's network at thin margins and exists mainly to slow broadband churn. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, an eroding moat with maximum leverage; reject.

## LULU (lululemon athletica inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

The 8x P/E that put lululemon on the Bruised Essential list uses FY2025 earnings that are disappearing: the Q2 FY2026 release (filed 2026-09-03) shows comparable sales -9%, Americas comps -12% (worse than about -6% in Q1), operating income -13% even with a one-off $134.5M tariff refund, and guidance for Q3 revenue -10% to -11% with EPS of only $0.93-0.98. Full-year revenue guidance was cut from $11.0-11.15B to $10.35-10.50B, and stripping out the $0.86 refund the company is earning about $8.70 a share this year, so at $110.78 it trades near 13x a still-falling number, not 8x. The cause is not the economy (international is roughly flat and China grew 30% in Q1) but the brand losing its edge at home: product went stale, younger customers moved to Alo and Vuori, the founder ran a proxy fight arguing the company lost its product discipline, and the CEO seat was held by interim co-heads until Heidi O'Neill arrived from Nike in September. Brand turnarounds happen, and the balance sheet ($1.4B cash, no debt) gives O'Neill time, but nothing in the numbers shows the decline has stopped. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, an eroding brand moat with earnings still falling; reject until Americas comps stop getting worse.

## KMX (CarMax, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

CarMax got onto the Mid-Rebuild list for good reasons (a CEO change, a lagging stock) and one bad one: its 17% 'free cash flow yield' and 5% FCF/EV come from CarMax Auto Finance, whose loan originations and $16.8B of non-recourse securitisation debt swing operating cash flow and enterprise value in ways that say nothing about the retail business. The real story is a successful-looking turnaround that everyone can see: new CEO Keith Barr cut prices to win back buyers from Carvana, comparable used units rose 13% in Q2 FY2027, EPS went from $0.64 to $1.16 (8-K filed 2026-09-29), and the 3 November strategic update promised mid-teens EPS growth to FY2030. The stock has already responded, up 52% in a year, and trades at 29x trailing and about 14x forward earnings, so the obvious recovery is in the price; what is less obvious, and not in CarMax's favour, is that part of the earnings growth comes from CAF doubling its share of Tier 2 lending to weaker borrowers, which is profit borrowed from the next credit downturn. Verdict: PRICED_IN, a real hurt that has already been repriced as a recovery, with captive-finance numbers inflating the screen; reject.

## APTV (Aptiv PLC): HURT, advance Y

Half of Aptiv's apparent collapse is bookkeeping: on 1 April 2026 it spun off its low-margin wire-harness business as Versigent (one VGNT share for every three APTV), and the unadjusted price series and the FY2025 financials in the screen still describe the old combined company, so the 1-year loss is really about -32% once the $14 of Versigent stock per Aptiv share is counted, and the P/E of 47 and FCF yield of 20% are meaningless. The other half is real: the remaining company (connectors, high-voltage parts, active safety electronics and Wind River software) reported Q3 2026 revenue +2% to $3.2B with Europe -5% and China +1%, adjusted EBITDA margin down from 19.1% to 17.7%, and full-year guidance of $12.7B sales and $5.60-5.80 adjusted EPS, after missing on Q2 and guiding light post-spin (8-K filed 2026-11-03). At $38.58 that is about 6.8x adjusted and 9.5x GAAP earnings and 5.3x EBITDA, with buybacks of $558M this year shrinking the share count about 2% a quarter, which prices New Aptiv like a plain auto parts maker even though its connector business resembles TE Connectivity, which sells at far higher multiples. The genuine risks are that Western carmakers keep losing share to Chinese brands that buy locally, that the 40% gap between adjusted and GAAP earnings hides recurring restructuring (2.9% of revenue over three years), and that ADAS software turns into an in-house OEM product. Verdict: HURT, an auto-cycle and spin-overhang discount on a business whose model is intact; advance, with Phase 4 checking non-auto growth, the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge and post-spin debt.
