# Phase 3, batch 4: why is it cheap?

Run date 2026-11-19. One paragraph per company, my own reading of the primary filings, ending on the verdict.

## DLB (Dolby Laboratories, Inc.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Dolby is a royalty collector on audio and video formats, and 93% of its $1.42B FY26 revenue (year to 2026-09-25) is licensing, most of it paid per device on TVs and set-top boxes (34% of licensing), phones (21%), consumer electronics (11%) and PCs (11%). Those unit markets are flat at best, and the 10-K now adds AI-driven memory shortages squeezing device makers. The growth story (Atmos, Vision, cars, the video-platform patent pool that signed Meta) is real but small enough that management guides FY27 revenue flat at $1.42B and GAAP EPS of $2.67-2.82 against $2.70 just reported (8-K 2026-11-17). Over ten years revenue compounded 3.3% a year, EPS fell 1.9% a year over five, and $130M of annual stock pay eats a third of free cash, leaving a 4.3% owner-cash yield. The stock is 33% off its three-year high and P/E 20.6 sits under its 27 median, but that median was paid for growth that never came; at a flat business and a 4% owner yield the price is about right. Verdict: PRICED_IN, reject.

## GGG (Graco Inc.): HURT, advance Y

Graco makes the pumps, sprayers and dispensing gear that contractors and factories use to move paint, foam, sealants and adhesives, and it has earned 26-28% operating margins and a 30% average return on capital doing it. The problem is volume: revenue has compounded only 1.4% a year over three years, net income has been flat at $0.46-0.52B since 2022, and year-to-date 2026 organic sales were 0% in the Americas and -3% in EMEA because US housing and remodeling have been weak for two years (10-Q filed 2026-10-21). Q3 2026 sales grew 8%, but half of that was bought, gross margin dropped about 3 points, and the new adhesives business Valco Melton lost $5M on $12M of sales; the Q4 guide of $610-640M disappointed and the stock fell around 15% on 2026-10-22. Management answered with a $600M accelerated buyback (about 5% of the company) on 2026-11-10. At P/E 22 against a 28 ten-year median and a 5.3% owner-cash yield, the market is pricing a stalled cyclical, while incoming organic orders grew 7% in the quarter and nothing in the filings suggests the product is losing share. This is not deep value, because margins are not at a trough, but it is a quality franchise priced for no recovery. Verdict: HURT, advance, with the caveat that the cushion is a cyclical upturn, not a bargain multiple.

## LOGI (Logitech International S.A.): PRICED_IN, advance N

Logitech sells mice, keyboards, webcams, headsets, gaming gear and meeting-room video kits, and it is a well-run, net-cash business that has cut its share count 13.7% in five years and lifted gross margin to 44.9% in the September 2026 quarter (8-K filed 2026-10-27). The reason the stock is 19% off its three-year high is mostly a single event: a semiconductor supplier's plant shut after an incident at the end of June 2026, which knocks an estimated $70-80M off the December quarter and turns the holiday guide into -5% to 0% in constant currency. That is a genuine one-off, but it does not make the stock cheap: at 20 times earnings against a 19 ten-year median and a 6% owner-cash yield, it is priced in line with its own decade, for a company whose sales are still 12% below the FY2021 pandemic peak and whose five-year EPS growth is -2.7%. Nothing here is mispriced in either direction. Verdict: PRICED_IN, reject.

## IT (Gartner, Inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

Gartner sells annual seats to its research and analyst calls, and the number that matters is how much existing clients renew. At Global Technology Sales, wallet retention (this year's spend from last year's clients, divided by what they spent last year) was 102% at 2023-09-30, 101% in 2024 and 98% in both 2025 and 2026; at Global Business Sales it slid from 108% to 106% to 102% to 99% (10-Qs filed 2024-11-05 and 2026-11-03). Contract value grew only 2.5% in Q3 2026 to $5.3B, revenue fell 1.3% to $1.505B, and the growth that exists comes from new logos, not from deeper use by existing ones. The federal cuts of 2025 explain part of it, but the decline in existing-client spend is broad and has lasted three years, during exactly the period when AI assistants became a cheap substitute for asking an analyst; a technology-change boom used to be Gartner's best sales environment and now coincides with shrinking renewals. The stock is down 68% from its three-year high to 15 times earnings and a 10% owner-cash yield, and management is shrinking the share count hard with $1.7B of buybacks in a year, partly on debt. Cheap and cash-rich, but the core evidence says clients need less of it each year, which is the Zoom pattern. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, reject; revisit only if wallet retention climbs back above 100%.

## FIS (Fidelity National Information Services, Inc.): HURT, advance Y

FIS runs core processing for banks and, since buying Global Payments' card-issuer processing business for $13.5B on 2026-01-09, the back end behind many credit cards too. The business underneath is growing slowly but steadily: Q3 2026 pro forma revenue rose 3.7%, adjusted EBITDA margin was 42.9% and free cash flow grew 8% to $862M (8-K filed 2026-11-04). What broke the stock is everything around it: a Capital Markets cut on 2026-08-04, then on 2026-11-04 a second cut to 3.1-3.6% pro forma growth from 4.5-5% because fewer than 10 large banks delayed conversions, plus extra security and implementation spending that management says weighs on 2027 and fades in 2028, plus the CFO's exit. With $20.8B of debt against a $13.8B market cap and buybacks paused, the equity now trades at about 4.6 times adjusted EPS and about 16% free-cash yield on market cap (6.5% on enterprise value). This management team has a poor record (the Worldpay round trip produced more than $18B of impairments), and that is the main risk, but the evidence points to delayed revenue and a temporary cost bump, not customers leaving: RPO is $24.5B and recurring revenue is still growing. The screen's numbers were stale FY2023 figures, but the real 2026 numbers still qualify. Verdict: HURT, advance, with leverage, quality of free cash flow and management credibility as the first things to test.

## EPAM (EPAM Systems, Inc.): DOWN_FOR_REASON, advance N

EPAM rents out software engineers, mostly by the hour, and for a decade that compounded revenue at 19.6% a year. In 2026 it is guiding to 2.5-2.7% organic growth, and the mix inside Q3 shows why that matters: time-and-materials revenue (billed per engineer-hour) fell to $1,106M from $1,119M a year earlier, with the Americas down 3.9%, while fixed-price work rose 18% (10-Q filed 2026-11-05). When clients shift from paying for hours to paying for outcomes while AI coding tools make each engineer more productive, the hours pool shrinks, and EPAM's delivery headcount rose only 1.2% in the quarter to 57,350. Margins improved after two cost programs, but nine-month operating cash flow fell to $250M from $372M, and the answer from management is a $500M accelerated buyback partly funded on the credit line plus a promise to hand back half of free cash flow through 2029, which is what a company does when it cannot find growth to fund. At about 18 times 2026 GAAP EPS and a 5.5% owner-cash yield, the stock is not cheap enough to pay for the risk that the core billing unit keeps shrinking. Verdict: DOWN_FOR_REASON, reject.

## ACN (Accenture plc): HURT, advance Y

Accenture is the largest IT services and consulting firm in the world, and its stock is 41% below its three-year high at 16 times earnings against a 24 ten-year median because investors assume AI agents will shrink the hours it bills. The filings do not show that yet: FY26 revenue (to 2026-08-31) grew 5% in local currency to $74.18B, beating its own 3-4% guide, Q4 grew 7%, bookings were $84.5B, and the long-term outsourcing book (managed services) signed 1.4 dollars of new work per dollar billed in Q4 (8-K filed 2026-10-01). Headcount still grew to about 814,000, so revenue per head is roughly flat at about $91,000, meaning AI has not yet visibly cut the labor content of a dollar of revenue, and the 10-K openly flags that it could. Margins and cash are intact: 15.4% operating margin, $11.6B of free cash flow, a 7.1% owner-cash yield after stock pay and $11.5B returned in a year. Unlike EPAM, whose hourly billings are already shrinking, Accenture's numbers say demand is holding while the multiple has priced in a decline. The key test for the next phase is pricing and revenue per head over the next four quarters. Verdict: HURT (a narrative de-rating with the business intact), advance.
