# The Deep Company Hunt: final report

Run 2026-11-19 to 2026-11-21. Prices are closes of 2026-11-18 (yfinance) unless stated. Numbers come from SEC EDGAR filings and XBRL unless marked otherwise. Every figure here traces to a dossier, kill test or red-team file in this folder.

## The one thing to know first

I hunted 1,254 famous US-listed companies across all seven grounds, and the market is mostly right about the cheap ones. Of 140 names that screened cheap or high-quality, 59 were already fully priced, 23 were cheap because their business is shrinking, and only 44 earned a closer look. Of those 44, just 6 survived the ten-gate kill test, and after full valuation only one trades below what I think it is worth by a useful margin: **Ameriprise (AMP), about 14% below a $580 midpoint.** The rest are good businesses at fair prices. The honest output of this hunt is one small position and a watch list with buy-below prices, not a portfolio.

**1. Ameriprise (AMP).** At 10-11x real operating earnings ($4.16B adjusted, trailing twelve months), the price implies owner cash shrinking 2.3% a year at a 10% discount rate. Meanwhile the advice business, now about two-thirds of profit, grew wrap assets 12% and advisor productivity 13%, and the company retires about 7% of its shares a year (diluted shares -21.9% over five years) with a $5.5B buyback authorization equal to 12.6% of its market value. The market sees it as a stock-market-linked financial with annuity baggage; what it underweights is how much per-share value compounds (about 12% a year) with no re-rating at all. The red team landed real blows. The screen's 18% "free cash flow yield" is fake, inflated by derivative collateral flows. Retirement & Protection earnings fell 39% in Q3 2026. Asset management has lost about $50B of flows in eight quarters. Insiders sold $161M and bought nothing in three years. Earnings sit near a cyclical high. That makes it a small position, not a large one.

**2. Mobility Global (MBGL, i.e. CARFAX).** Spun out of S&P Global in 2026. CARFAX is a 49%-margin toll on used-car transactions growing about 8% a year, packaged with a slower B2B unit, and the whole thing trades at 11.7x EBITDA against 13-20x for data peers. The spin left it with about $100M of one-time stand-up costs and allocation drag that should fade, plus a $350-450M buyback through 2027. But standalone cash flow per share is flat, interest costs about $111M a year, cash taxes run $80-90M a year above book tax for 12 years, and net income fell 41% in the first standalone quarter. The stock is already up 40% from its September low, so I'd buy below $19-20, not at $24.

**3-5. Acuity, Textron, Graco** are quality businesses that screened as cheap against their own history, but valuation work showed the history was the wrong anchor. Acuity's hidden controls unit is already priced at about 18x EBITDA, and it is funding a second full-price acquisition with a new $400M term loan. Textron's 12.5x P/E is about 18x on core earnings once LIFO add-backs and non-cash pension income come out, and its segments offset each other in revenue but not in profit (segment profit fell 41% in 2020). Graco's toll is intact, but organic growth has stalled at about 1% a year since 2022, and a $600M revolver-funded buyback in November turned its net cash into about $0.66B of net debt.

**6. Smurfit Westrock** survived the kill test only narrowly, and the red team nearly finished it. Nine-month free cash flow of $240M covered $712M of dividends only with $334M of new debt. Return on capital is 4.1%. A DOJ grand jury subpoena into containerboard pricing (2026-09-22) is not sized. Downgraded.

## Portfolio fit and Alex's own list

- **JPM (held):** tested honestly as the Ground 5 model, and it *is* the model on revenue offsets (segment revenue correlation -0.24). But profit changes correlate +0.64, because credit reserves hit every segment at once. At 15x earnings vs a 10x ten-year median and 2.9x tangible book, it is priced in, not cheap.
- **INTU (watchlist):** screened in four grounds and scored top of Ground 5 on revenue offsets, then failed the kill test on gates 3, 8, 9 and 10. TurboTax units are eroding, and the AI-agent bear case is fully priced into an 18x multiple.
- **SPGI (watchlist):** priced in at its own 30x history. MBGL is its spun-off CARFAX business and now trades separately; holding both is not a doubled bet.
- **UNP (held):** screens as a toll road but trades above its own history (25x vs 19x), so it never reached the shortlist.
- **META, UBER, AZN, Alphabet:** none screened into any ground's top list as mispriced. The Ground 6 agent checked Alphabet for a hidden-room structure: its AI and SpaceX stakes are a few percent of its value, not a hidden room.
- **Correlation:** AMP rides equity markets the way JPM rides credit. Owning both doubles exposure to a financial-markets downturn. That is another reason to keep AMP small.

## The three biggest risks across the whole list

1. **Market-level risk in the one actionable name.** AMP's earnings depend on asset levels and spread income near a cyclical high. A 25% equity drawdown would hit fees, flows and its insurance capital at once (insurer capital already fell from $2.73B to $2.38B in nine months).
2. **Leverage creeping into "quality" names.** Graco, Acuity and CARFAX all added debt in late 2026 (revolver-funded buyback, acquisition term loan, $2.0B of spin notes). None of them is a war-chest company any more.
3. **Unsized legal exposure.** SW (DOJ containerboard probe), CPRT (DOJ money-laundering controls), ABT (infant-formula litigation and a criminal probe). Exposures that can't be sized are why gate 8 killed otherwise good businesses.

## Grounds without a finalist
