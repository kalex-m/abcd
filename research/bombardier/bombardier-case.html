The Bombardier Case

Bombardier is no longer the debt-laden trains-and-airliners conglomerate it was. It now builds only large business jets, in a market with two serious players. Its numbers run through your whole cycle: revenue, margins, cash flow, debt paid down, investment phase finished, and a services business that keeps paying.

FY2022 → FY2025 → trailing 12 months to Sep-2026. US$. Statements via yFinance; backlog, services and defense from company results.

Your survival tree

Aerospace is the definition of a capital-intensive industry. The test is whether the company can carry it.

What to keep watching

Deliveries lag demand33 jets in Q3-26 vs 34 a year earlier while backlog jumped. Engine and supplier bottlenecks cap output; management is weighing higher rates only after checking supplier capacity.
Business jets are cyclicalDemand follows corporate profits and wealth. The $22.2B backlog and the services annuity cushion a downturn, but new-jet volumes would fall.
Balance sheet still healingShareholders' equity is still negative (−$0.48B) and debt is $4.1B. Both are moving the right way every quarter.
