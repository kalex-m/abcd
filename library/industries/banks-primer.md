# Banks primer

Last updated: 2026-11-26 (run 20261126-full_hunt). Edit and extend this file each run; keep the run log at the bottom.

## What the industry is
US banks take in deposits and wholesale funding, lend and invest at higher yields, and add fee and trading businesses on top. The Fed's H.8 data (week of Nov 11, 2026) shows US commercial banks hold about $19.8T deposits, $14.3T loans and $5.9T securities (https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h8/current/default.htm).

Four groups matter:
- Money-centre banks (JPM, BAC, C, WFC): everything at once.
- Trading and wealth (GS, MS, SCHW): fees and trading, less deposit-funded lending.
- Regionals (USB, PNC, TFC, FITB, MTB, HBAN, KEY, RF, CFG): mostly spread lenders to local firms, commercial real estate (CRE) and households.
- Card lenders (COF, SYF; AXP is payments plus lending) and custody banks (BNY, STT, NTRS).

## How money flows
1. Spread: loan and bond yields minus deposit and funding costs. Measured as net interest income (NII) and net interest margin (NIM).
2. Fees: cards, wealth, advisory, custody, service charges.
3. Trading: client buying and selling, financing, underwriting. Swings with volumes and volatility.
Costs: credit losses, staff and technology, deposit interest, and capital held back by regulators.

The "stronghold" pattern (JPM): NII, card fees, markets, investment banking and wealth offset each other. In Q3 2026 JPM had NII $27.1B (+13%), noninterest revenue $23.8B (+3%), CET1 13.9%, overhead 54% (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/19617/000162828026065582/a3q26erfexhibit991narrative.htm). A prior repo study named JPM the bank champion; that is unverified context here, and the filings fit the stronghold shape but no valuation check was run in this pass.

## Where the cycle is (Nov 2026)
- Loans: H.8 loans +8.9% annualized in October, +6.2% in Q3, +8.1% in Q2. C&I +11.7% and CRE +5.1% in October.
- Margins: NIM up or flat year on year at most banks (USB 2.84%, RF 3.68%, HBAN 3.25%; WFC down to 2.43% from 2.61%).
- Deposit cost: first upticks of 2-3bp in Q3 (TFC 1.59%, HBAN 1.90%, KEY 1.66%, COF 2.94%), after a year of declines.
- Credit: charge-offs flat to lower; reserves built at wholesale-heavy JPM (+$539M) and C (+$369M), released at COF, PNC, TFC. COF domestic card delinquency 3.87% in October (3.99% a year ago).
- Capital: CET1 down year on year at BAC (10.9%), WFC (10.0%), PNC (9.8%), MTB, SYF from buybacks. Rules: March 2026 capital proposals would modestly cut requirements (not final); the stress-capital-buffer averaging rule was published Oct 2 and takes effect Dec 1.
- Trading: equities trading very high (GS +72% yoy), fixed income falling (GS FICC -24% QoQ). The market is selling GS, MS, BAC, COF (-16% to -23% over 3 months) and the regionals are also down 4-11% over 3 months.
- Macro: Fed leaning to hike Dec 8-9; 10y 4.85%; HY spreads 3.72% and widening.

Regime read: hard data Overheat (good for banks), markets pricing a Slowdown (bad). Credit hasn't turned in filings yet; credit usually turns after spreads, not before.

## Lingo
- NII: interest earned minus interest paid.
- NIM: NII divided by earning assets.
- Deposit beta: share of a Fed rate move passed on to depositors.
- Net charge-off (NCO) rate: loans written off net of recoveries, annualized, as a percent of loans.
- Reserve build/release: provisioning more or less for future losses than were written off.
- CET1: highest-quality capital as a percent of risk-weighted assets; the cushion.
- Stress capital buffer (SCB): extra CET1 set from the Fed's annual stress test.
- Basel III endgame: the final pieces of international capital rules; reproposed Mar 2026.
- Efficiency ratio: costs divided by revenue; lower is better.
- AOCI: unrealized gains or losses on bonds held available-for-sale; hurt by high long yields.
- H.8: the Fed's weekly bank balance-sheet release.
- SLOOS: Fed survey of loan officers on lending standards and demand.
- FICC / equities: fixed-income, currencies, commodities and stock trading desks.

## The 5 numbers to watch
1. H.8 loan growth by category (weekly, Fridays 4:15pm ET): demand and the ISM/GDP link.
2. NIM and cost of deposits (quarterly): does the hike bias squeeze margins?
3. Net charge-off rate and reserve builds, especially cards (COF monthly 8-K) and CRE.
4. CET1 and buybacks: capital room as rules change.
5. HY spread (FRED BAMLH0A0HYM2): above 4% would be the stress line.
